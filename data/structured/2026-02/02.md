## 16th Finance Commission report (2026–31) | from: 2026-02-05, CA360-2026-02, OL-2026-02

## SHANTI Act — nuclear sector opened to private players | from: 2026-02-13
# 1. Act
- **Name:** **Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India (SHANTI) Act, 2025** — amends liability regime of **CLNDA 2010**
- **Private entities allowed:** operate plants, manufacture equipment, fuel fabrication, enrichment of **U-235 up to prescribed limit**
- **Reserved for Centre:** enrichment beyond limits, **spent fuel** management
- **AERB** (est. **1983**) gets **statutory status** — accountable to Parliament
# 2. Liability caps
| Plant size | Operator liability |
|---|---|
| **Large** | **₹3,000 crore** |
| **Medium** | **₹1,500 crore** |
| **Small Modular Reactors** | **₹100 crore** |
- Excess covered by a **Nuclear Liability Fund**; ⚠ **supplier liability eliminated** (CLNDA allowed recourse against suppliers)
- Operators exempt for **"grave natural disasters"**; overall ceiling about **₹3,900 crore**
- **Bodies:** **Atomic Energy Redressal Advisory Council**; **Nuclear Damage Claims Commission**; appeals to **Appellate Tribunal for Electricity** (Electricity Act 2003)
# 3. Background
- **Atomic Energy Act 1962** replaced **1948** Act; amended **1986, 1987, 2015**; **CLNDA 2010** — **no-fault liability**
- Nuclear share **~3%** of electricity; targets missed — **10 GW by 2000** (got **2.86 GW**), **20 GW by 2020** (got **6.78 GW**); new target **100 GW by 2047**
- ⚠ Reactor principle: **controlled nuclear fission**
| Plant | State |
|---|---|
| **Tarapur** | **Maharashtra** |
| **Rajasthan (Rawatbhata)** | **Rajasthan** |
| **Kakrapar** | **Gujarat** |
| **Kudankulam** (PWR) | **Tamil Nadu** |

## PRAHAAR — India's first national counter-terrorism policy | from: 2026-02-24
# 1. Policy
- **Released by:** **MHA** on **23 February 2026** — ⚠ **India's first-ever** national counter-terrorism policy
- Drafting announced **November 2024**; earlier **National Policy and Action Plan for LWE (2015)**; **Pahalgam attack (22 April 2025)** spurred NIA–state coordination
# 2. Seven pillars
- **Prevention**; **Response**; **Aggregating internal capacities**; **Human rights and rule-of-law processes**; **Countering radicalisation**; **Alignment with global efforts**; **Recovery and resilience** (whole-of-society)
# 3. Provisions
- **Zero tolerance** — deny funds, weapons, safe havens
- Threats on **land, air (drones), water**; critical sectors — power, railways, aviation, ports, defence, space, atomic energy
- **CBRNED:** Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear, Explosive, **Digital**
- Recognises **cyber-attacks by nation-states**, crypto funding, dark web
- ⚠ Does **not** link terrorism to any religion, ethnicity or civilisation
- Graded counter-radicalisation; prison de-radicalisation; legal experts from **FIR stage**; standardised state anti-terror structures

- **Reasons:** LoP **Rahul Gandhi** not allowed to quote former Army Chief **Gen M.M. Naravane's** unpublished memoir; Speaker's remark that PM could have been attacked in the House
# 2. Removal procedure
- **Article 94(c):** removal by resolution passed by **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) — ⚠ not "present and voting"
- **Notice:** at least **14 days**, in writing to **Secretary-General**
- **Leave:** at least **50 members** must stand in support; discussion within **10 days** of leave
- **Rules 200–203** of Lok Sabha Rules; **Rule 200A** — charges specific, no sarcasm or imputations; mover speech capped **15 minutes**
- **Article 96:** Speaker cannot **preside** but can speak and **vote in first instance**; ⚠ **no casting vote**
- On dissolution, Speaker continues until just before first meeting of next Lok Sabha
- ⚠ Removal provision does **not** apply to **Rajya Sabha Chairman** this way
# 3. Past motions — none succeeded
