## Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, 2025 — removal of arrested Ministers | from: 2025-08-20, 2025-08-21
# 1. News
- **Introduced:** in **Lok Sabha** by the **Union Home Minister**
- **Amends:** **Article 75** (Council of Ministers incl. PM)
- **Provision:** a Minister **arrested and detained for 30 consecutive days** on allegation of an offence punishable with **5 years or more** imprisonment shall be **removed by the President on the advice of the PM** by the **31st day**
- **Applies to:** both **Union and State Ministers**
- **UTs:** separate amendments to **Government of Union Territories Act, 1963** and **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**
- **Backdrop:** **V. Senthil Balaji case (2023)**, Tamil Nadu — Minister continued in office despite arrest in money-laundering case
- ⚠ Not limited to Union Ministers — applies to **State Ministers** too
# 2. Related bills and procedure
- **Bills:** **Government of UTs (Amendment) Bill, 2025**; **J&K Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2025**; **Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Bill, 2025** (same session)
- **Rules waived:** **Rule 19A** (prior notice before introduction) and **Rule 19B** (advance circulation to MPs) of Lok Sabha
- **Reason:** **Monsoon Session** ending **21 August 2025**
- **Critic:** Congress MP **Abhishek Singhvi** — could be weaponised by "biased central agencies"
# 3. Background
- **Article 75:** Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President**
- **Article 164(1):** State Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the Governor**
- Constitution did **not** explicitly bar Ministers in detention from holding office
# 4. More provisions and JPC
- **Covers:** **PM**, **Chief Ministers**, Union and State Ministers — custody **30 consecutive days without bail**
- **Removal by:** **President** (PM/Union Ministers) or **Governor** (CM/State Ministers) on advice of the executive head
- **Automatic cessation:** if advice not tendered by the **31st day**, the person **automatically ceases** to be Minister
- **Re-appointment:** allowed after release from custody under **Article 75(1)**
- ⚠ **No permanent disqualification** — can be re-appointed
- **Three bills:** Govt of UTs (Amendment), Constitution (130th Amendment), J&K Reorganisation (Amendment) — all **2025**
- **JPC:** **31 members** — **21 Lok Sabha** + **10 Rajya Sabha**; report by the **Winter Session**
- **JPC background:** ad hoc body of both Houses; can summon officials and documents; recommendations **not binding**

## Section 152 BNS — sedition debate and press freedom | from: 2025-08-20
# 1. News
- **Event:** **Assam Police** summoned **The Wire** founding editor **Siddharth Varadarajan** and consulting editor **Karan Thapar** under **Section 152** of **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**
- **Related to:** coverage of **Operation Sindoor**
- **Same day:** **Supreme Court** issued notice on The Wire's petition challenging Section 152 and protected journalists from coercive action
- **Lapses:** summons lacked FIR date, offence details, FIR copy — against **BNSS** provisions
# 2. Background
- **Section 152 BNS:** seen as rebranded **Section 124A IPC** (sedition) — SC kept 124A **in abeyance in 2022**
- **Covers:** acts endangering **sovereignty, unity and integrity of India**; vague term "**knowingly**"
- ⚠ Unlike 124A, Section 152 does **not** demand a high standard of proof of intent
- **Right involved:** **Article 19(1)(a)** — freedom of speech and expression

## Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 | from: 2025-08-21, 2025-08-27
# 1. News
- **Passed:** **Lok Sabha** by **voice vote**, Monsoon Session
- **Bans:** offering, operation, facilitation, advertisement, promotion and participation in **online money games** (fantasy sports, card games with stakes)
- **Promotes:** **e-sports** and recreational/social gaming — through a **regulatory authority**
- **Not released** for public consultation — govt: prohibition laws need no public debate
- **Persons:** Speaker **Om Birla**; IT Minister **Ashwini Vaishnaw**; IT Secretary **S. Krishnan**
- **Became law:** **22 August 2025** after Presidential assent
# 2. Classification of games
| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **E-sports** | recognised under **National Sports Governance Act, 2025**; registration; entry fee allowed, performance-based prizes (e.g. Call of Duty, GTA competitive) |
| **Online social games** | casual/educational; no betting; **promoted** by govt |
| **Real Money Games (RMGs)** | stake money/tokens for monetary reward (poker, rummy, fantasy sports, paid ludo) — **completely banned** |
# 3. Penalties and institutions
- **Offering RMGs / fund transactions:** up to **3 years** jail, fine up to **₹1 crore**
- **Advertising RMGs:** up to **2 years**, fine up to **₹50 lakh**
- **Offences:** **cognisable and non-bailable** (BNSS 2023)
- **Blocking:** **CERT-In**; **Interpol** for foreign operators
- ⚠ **No penalty for players** — only operators/advertisers
- **Regulatory Authority**; funds from **Consolidated Fund of India** for social gaming
- **Earlier:** **IT Rules, 2021** amendments (**2023**) — KYC, parental controls, age rating
# 4. Data and issues
- **Losses:** ~**₹15,000 crore** a year to RMGs; **32** gaming suicides in **Karnataka** in 31 months
- **Fraud cited:** **₹400 crore** via Chinese app **FIEWIN**; **₹30,000 crore** GST evasion; **₹2,000 crore** tax evasion (2022)
- **Constitution:** betting & gambling — **Entries 34 & 62, State List**
- **GST:** uniform **28%** on entry fee/deposit (2023) — SC stayed notices
- **Challenge ground:** **Article 19(1)(g)** — skill vs chance
- **Industry:** **2 lakh** jobs, **400+** firms
- ⚠ Act has **no** explicit child-protection provisions

## Machine-readable electoral rolls debate | from: 2025-08-21
# 1. News
- **Rolls:** over **99 crore** entries; EC shares them as **image PDFs** (no voter photos)
- **Database:** **ERONET** — EC's digital application for voter details; rolls prepared by district officials
- **Example:** Congress manually found **11,965 duplicate entries** in **Mahadevapura** (Bengaluru)
- **EC stand:** before **2019** elections, EC told CEOs to stop uploading machine-readable rolls (prevent foreign access)
- **Case:** **Kamal Nath vs EC of India (2018)** — SC refused to compel EC to give machine-readable rolls; citizens may use **OCR**
- **Scale:** over **6 crore** pages nationwide
- **Persons:** activist **P.G. Bhat**; transparency advocate **Srinivas Kodali**; **Rahul Gandhi**

## Presidential Reference under Article 143 on timelines for assent to Bills | from: 2025-08-25
# 1. News
- **Reference by:** President **Droupadi Murmu** — can President and Governors be judicially compelled to act within timelines on State Bills?
- **Bench:** Constitution Bench led by **CJI B.R. Gavai**
- **Trigger:** SC judgment of **8 April 2025** — Justices **J.B. Pardiwala** and **R. Mahadevan**
  - Tamil Nadu Governor **R.N. Ravi**'s delay on **10 re-passed Bills** held **illegal**
  - ⚠ **First time** judicially enforceable timelines imposed on Governors and President
# 2. Background — Article 143
- **143(1):** President may refer a question of **law or fact** of public importance for **advisory opinion**
- **143(2):** disputes on **pre-Constitution treaties**, agreements
- **Opinion:** **advisory, not binding** (but persuasive)
- ⚠ SC **cannot directly overrule** its own precedent in a Reference — can only clarify or suggest a larger bench
