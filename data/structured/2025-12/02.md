## SHANTI Bill, 2025 — nuclear energy reform | from: 2025-12-22
# 1. News
- **Bill:** **Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India (SHANTI) Bill, 2025** — introduced in **Lok Sabha**
- **Replaces:** **Atomic Energy Act, 1962** and **Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage (CLND) Act, 2010**
- ⚠ **First-ever** private and foreign participation in nuclear power generation — ends **NPCIL** monopoly
# 2. Key provisions
- **Private/foreign firms:** can build, own, operate, decommission plants
- **Retained by government:** fuel production, **heavy water**, radioactive waste, enrichment/reprocessing, transport of sensitive materials
- **AERB:** gets **statutory** status, accountable to **Parliament**
- **Liability:** **supplier liability removed**; operator liability **capped** and linked to reactor capacity
- **Atomic Disputes Tribunal**; **Claims Commissioners**
- **Section 39:** Centre can declare nuclear information "restricted" — overrides **RTI Act**
# 3. Targets and numbers
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| **Current capacity** | **8.18 GW** |
| **Target 2032** | **22 GW** |
| **Target 2047** | **100 GW** (needs **₹15 lakh crore**) |
| **SMRs** | At least **five** indigenous SMRs by **2033** |
| **Nuclear Energy Mission (Budget 2025–26)** | **₹20,000 crore** |
- **BARC designs:** **BSMR-200** (200 MWe), **SMR-55** (55 MWe), up to **5 MWth** high-temperature gas-cooled reactor for **hydrogen**
# 4. Background
- **Atomic Energy Act, 1962** replaced the **1948** law; amended **1986**, **1987**, **2015** (JVs of govt companies)
- **CLND Act 2010:** **no-fault** liability
- **IAEA safeguards:** apply to reactors using **imported uranium**

## National Judicial Policy and NJAC revival plea | from: 2025-12-02
# 1. News
- **CJI Surya Kant:** called for a **National Judicial Policy** to reduce divergence across courts
- **SC to examine:** plea seeking revival of the **National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)**
- **Pendency:** over **5 crore** cases; about **one-third** of HC posts vacant
# 2. NJAC
- **Created by:** **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014**
- **Members (6):** **CJI** (Chairperson), **two senior-most SC judges**, **Union Law Minister**, **two eminent persons** (chosen by **PM + CJI + LoP in Lok Sabha**)
- **Struck down:** **2015** — **Fourth Judges Case** (**Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association vs Union of India**) — violated **judicial independence** (basic structure)
  - ⚠ Any **two members** could veto a name
- **Judicial independence** as basic structure: **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**
# 3. Constitutional provisions
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **124** | Appointment of **SC judges** by President |
| **217** | Appointment of **HC judges** (consultation with CJI, Governor, HC CJ) |
| **126** | **Acting CJI** |
| **127** | **Ad hoc** SC judges |
| **128** | **Retired** judges sitting in SC |
- ⚠ **44th Amendment (1978)** did **not** place the PM's election beyond judicial review (UPSC 2019 trap)

## Political funding — electoral trusts after electoral bonds verdict | from: 2025-12-30
# 1. News
- **February 2024:** Supreme Court struck down the **Electoral Bonds Scheme (2018)** — violated **Article 19(1)(a)** (right to information); called quid pro quo **"institutionalised corruption"**
- **FY 2024–25:** **electoral trusts** became the main lawful channel for corporate funding
# 2. Electoral trusts
- **Introduced:** **Electoral Trusts Scheme, 2013** by **CBDT**
- **Tax benefits:** **Section 80GGB** (companies), **Section 80GGC** (individuals)
- **Disclosure:** annual report to **ECI** — donor names, amounts, recipient parties; ⚠ not anonymous to ECI
- **Not disclosed:** which company funded which party
- **Prudent Electoral Trust:** **₹33,330.54 crore** (**86.38%** among top 10); FY 2024–25 received **₹2,668.49 crore**, gave **₹2,180.71 crore** to BJP and **₹216.34 crore** to Congress
- **BJP share:** **84.65%** of declared direct corporate donations (FY14–FY24); **71.67%** of electoral trust funds
# 3. Electoral Bond Scheme facts
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Issuer** | **SBI** |
| **Denominations** | **₹1,000**, **₹10,000**, **₹1 lakh**, **₹10 lakh**, **₹1 crore** |
| **Validity** | **15 days** |
| **Eligible parties** | At least **1%** votes in last election |
| **Unencashed bonds** | To **PMNRF** |
- **Corporate donations banned:** **1969–1985**
- ⚠ No legal cap on **party** expenditure — only candidates capped

## Breach of privilege notice against eight Congress MPs | from: 2025-12-25
# 1. News
- **Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla** examining a BJP MP's **breach of privilege** notice against **eight Congress members** for disruption during the **VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025** debate
# 2. Parliamentary privileges
- **Articles:** **105** and **122** (Parliament); **194** and **212** (State Legislatures)
- **Extend to:** **Attorney General** — ⚠ **not** the **President**
- **Article 105(3):** until defined by law, privileges as of **British House of Commons** on **26 January 1950** — no law enacted yet
- **Freedom from arrest (civil cases):** during session and **40 days** before/after — **Section 135A, CPC 1908**
- **44th Amendment, 1978:** fair press reporting of proceedings (except secret sittings)
- **Committee of Privileges:** **15** members (Lok Sabha), **10** (Rajya Sabha) — semi-judicial
- **2008 Lok Sabha Privileges Committee** opposed codification
# 3. Key judgments
| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **Pandit M.S.M. Sharma vs Sri Krishna Sinha (Searchlight)** | **1958** | Privileges under **Art 194(3)** prevail over press freedom |
| **P.V. Narasimha Rao (JMM Bribery)** | **1998** | Immunity for bribes linked to speech/vote |
| **State of Kerala vs K. Ajith** | **2021** | Privileges do not override criminal law |
| **Sita Soren vs Union of India** | **2024** | ⚠ Overruled JMM case — bribery not protected |

## Karnataka Hate Speech and Hate Crimes (Prevention) Bill, 2025 | from: 2025-12-10
# 1. News
- ⚠ **Karnataka** — **first state** to bring a dedicated law on hate speech and hate crimes
- **Why:** India has **no statutory definition** of hate speech
- **Grounds covered:** religion, race, caste, **gender**, **sexual orientation**, disability, place of birth
- **Collective liability:** office-bearers of linked organisations
- **Online:** State can block/remove hateful content
# 2. Punishment
| Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|
| **First offence** | **1–7 years** and fine **₹50,000** |
| **Repeat offence** | Not less than **2 years**, up to **10 years**, fine **₹1 lakh** |
- **Nature:** **cognisable**, **non-bailable**, triable by **Judicial Magistrate First Class**
# 3. Existing law
| Provision | Subject |
|---|---|
| **BNS Section 196** (ex-IPC 153A) | Promoting enmity between groups |
| **BNS Section 299** (ex-IPC 295A) | Insulting religion |
| **BNS Section 353** | Incitement to offences endangering public order |
| **IT Act Section 66A** | ⚠ Struck down — **Shreya Singhal (2015)** |
| **RPA 1951 Section 8** | Disqualification for promoting enmity |
| **SC/ST (PoA) Act, 1989** | Caste-based insults |
| **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** | Untouchability |
- **Conviction rate:** **20.2%** under IPC 153A (NCRB **2020**)
# 4. Judgments and reports
| Case/Report | Point |
|---|---|
| **Pravasi Bhalai Sangathan (2014)** | Asked Law Commission to define hate speech |
| **Tehseen Poonawalla (2018)** | Mob lynching guidelines |
| **Shaheen Abdulla (2022)** | Police to file **suo motu FIRs**; extended to all states **2023** |
| **Law Commission Report 267 (2017)** | New **Section 153C** (incitement to hatred), **505A** (provocation of violence) |
| **Bezbaruah Committee (2014)** | Racial and regional discrimination |
| **Viswanathan Committee (2015)** | Identity-based offences |
- **Private Member Bill (2022):** Hate Speech & Hate Crimes Bill — not passed

## Right to Disconnect Bill, 2025 | from: 2025-12-25
# 1. News
- **Private Member's Bill** on **Right to Disconnect** introduced in Parliament — right not to respond to work calls/emails beyond working hours
- ⚠ India's **first formal legislative attempt** on after-hours digital work
- **Context:** **29** labour laws consolidated into **four Labour Codes**; **OSH Code, 2020** silent on digital labour
- **Gap:** no definition of digital "work"; not explicitly linked to **Article 21**
# 2. Abroad
- **EU:** on-call time counts as work — **SIMAP**, **Jaeger**, **Tyco** cases
- **France:** collective bargaining on digital communication; **Germany:** strict hour caps
