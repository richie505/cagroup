## One Nation, One Election — 129th Amendment Bill | from: 2026-07-17
# 1. The Bill
- **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024:** inserts **Article 82A**; amends **Article 83** (Parliament duration) and **Article 172** (State Legislature duration)
- **Appointed date** notified by **President** (first sitting of new Lok Sabha); mid-term dissolution → election only for **unexpired term**
- ⚠ **Local bodies excluded** from the Bill
- **Companion:** **Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024** — **Delhi, Puducherry, J&K**
# 2. History and committees
- **Simultaneous elections:** **1952, 1957, 1962, 1967**; broken in **1968–69**; 4th Lok Sabha dissolved **1970** → elections **1971**
| Body | Year | View |
|---|---|---|
| **Election Commission** | **1983** | Recommended return |
| **Law Commission 170th Report** (Justice **B.P. Jeevan Reddy**) | **1999** | Supported |
| **Parliamentary Standing Committee** | **2015** | MCC burden |
| **NITI Aayog paper** | **2017** | Phased model |
| **Law Commission draft** | **2018** | Constructive no-confidence |
| **Kovind Committee** | **2023–24** | Phased; local bodies within **100 days** |
# 3. Numbers
- **CMS:** 2024 election cost about **₹1.35 lakh crore** (vs **₹60,000 crore** in 2019)
- **JPC:** possible saving **₹7 lakh crore**; GDP boost up to **1.6 pp**
- **MCC period:** **45–60 days**
- **Machines needed:** **46.75 lakh** BUs, **33.63 lakh** CUs, **36.62 lakh** VVPATs; replacement about **₹10,000 crore every 15 years**
# 4. Concerns
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** free and fair elections, parliamentary democracy = **basic structure**
- **Constructive no-confidence motion** borrowed from **Germany**
- **73rd/74th Amendments:** local elections by **State Election Commissions**

## Trial in absentia — Section 356 BNSS | from: 2026-07-15
# 1. What happened
- **Special NIA Court, Jammu** issued **non-bailable warrant** against **Hafiz Saeed** (**Lashkar-e-Taiba** chief) in **Pahalgam** attack case
- NIA may seek **trial in absentia** under **Section 356, BNSS 2023**
# 2. Provisions
- **Applies only to:** **proclaimed offender** under **Section 84 BNSS** — offence punishable with **10 years+**, **life** or **death**
- Court must **record reasons**; absence treated as **waiver** of right to be present
- **Safeguards:** **two consecutive arrest warrants** at least **30 days** apart; newspaper notice with **30 days** to appear; notice at last residence; inform relative/friend; trial only after **90 days** from framing of charges; **State-appointed defence lawyer**; **audiovisual** recording
- Accused appearing later may **cross-examine** witnesses
- ⚠ Not for minor offences
# 3. CrPC comparison
| CrPC section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **82** | Proclamation against absconder |
| **299** | Evidence in absence of absconder |
| **317** | Dispense with personal attendance |
- ⚠ CrPC did **not** permit full trial in absentia; BNSS replaced **CrPC 1973**

## FCRA — FAQ and Amendment Bill 2026 | from: 2026-07-23
# 1. About FCRA
- **Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act:** administered by **Ministry of Home Affairs**
- **History:** first Act **1976**; registration mandatory **1984**; replaced by **FCRA 2010**; **2020** amendment — single **SBI New Delhi Main Branch** account, sub-granting banned; **2022** relatives' limit raised
- **2024–25:** about **16,200** registered associations received about **₹22,963 crore**
- ⚠ FCRA does **not ban** foreign donations — registration (organisations **3 years+** old) or **prior permission**
- **Registration validity:** **5 years**; annual return **Form FC-4**
- **Admin expense cap:** **20%** of annual FC
- **Barred:** election candidates, legislators, **judges**, public servants, political parties, political organisations, certain media persons
# 2. 2026 changes
- **Provisional / permanent vesting** of FC-created assets in **Designated Authority** (vesting concept from **FCRA 2010**)
- **Revision and appeal** to **District Judge**
- **State agencies** need **Central approval** before FCRA probes
- Registration to specify **purpose** and **State/UT**; existing bodies file **Form FC-6F**
- **Renewal:** must show utilisation of at least **₹10 lakh** over prior **two years**
- **Ultimate-donor**, project-wise disclosure
# 3. Global comparison
| Country | Law |
|---|---|
| **USA** | **FARA** — Foreign Agents Registration Act |
| **Australia** | **FITS** — Foreign Influence Transparency Scheme |
| **UK** | **FIRS** — Foreign Influence Registration Scheme |
| **Canada** | Foreign Influence Transparency and Accountability Act |

## Bar Associations cannot refuse to represent an accused | from: 2026-07-07
# 1. What happened
- **Faizabad/Ayodhya Bar Association** resolved not to represent accused in alleged **Ayodhya Ram Temple funds embezzlement case**
- **Legal position:** such resolutions are **illegal, unconstitutional, null and void**
# 2. Constitutional basis
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **22(1)** | Arrested person's right to consult and be defended by a **legal practitioner of choice** |
| **21** | **Fair trial** part of right to life |
| **14** | Equality before law |
| **39A** | Equal justice and **free legal aid** (DPSP) |
# 3. Cases
| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| **A.S. Mohammed Rafi v. State of Tamil Nadu (2010)** | SC — boycott resolutions **wholly illegal**, null and void (Coimbatore police case) |
| **Kuldeep Agarwal v. State of Uttarakhand (2019)** | Uttarakhand HC — "special circumstances" refusal is for **individual advocate**, not Bar |
| **Karnataka HC (2020)** | Obstruction of lawyers for Kashmiri students may be contempt |
| **Madras HC (2025)** | Bar is **not a trade union** |
- **BCI Rules:** advocate generally bound to accept a brief; may refuse only in **special circumstances**
