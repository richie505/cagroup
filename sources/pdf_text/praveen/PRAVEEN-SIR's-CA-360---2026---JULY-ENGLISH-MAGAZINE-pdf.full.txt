India–United Kingdom Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA)
• The CETA between India and the United Kingdom officially came into effect on July 15, 2026.
• It is the sixth Free Trade Agreement (FTA) implemented by the Indian government, following deals with
Mauritius, UAE, Australia, EFTA, and Oman.
• The agreement is the largest trade deal India has concluded in recent years.
• Under this pact, 99% of Indian products gain duty-free access to the UK market.
• Major beneficiary sectors in India include textiles, apparel, footwear, carpets, processed food, agriculture,
spices, seafood, and meat.
• Exports of automobiles, machinery, electronics, metal products, ceramics, and cement also benefit from
this agreement.
• Signed on July 25, 2025, the treaty aims to boost bilateral trade, investments, and economic cooperation.
• An FTA is defined as an agreement between two or more countries to reduce or eliminate trade barriers
and import duties to promote growth.
• United Kingdom: The capital is London, the currency is the Pound Sterling (£), the Head of State is King
Charles III, and the Prime Minister is Keir Starmer.
European Free Trade Association (EFTA)
• Established in 1960, the association is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.
• It consists of four member states: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland.
PM Narendra Modi’s Visit to Indonesia, Australia, and New Zealand
• Prime Minister Modi conducted a six-day official tour of these three nations from July 6 to July 11, 2026.
• The visit focused on strengthening India’s diplomatic, strategic, and economic relations in the Indo-Pacific
region.
• The itinerary included Indonesia (July 6–8), Australia (July 8–10), and New Zealand (July 10–11).
• PM Modi held high-level meetings with Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto, Australian PM Anthony
Albanese, and New Zealand PM Christopher Luxon.
• The tour is a key part of India’s "Act East Policy" and its broader strategy for Indo-Pacific cooperation.
• Discussions prioritized trade, investment, strategic partnerships, defense, and engagement with the Indian
diaspora.
• This was the first official visit by an Indian Prime Minister to New Zealand in approximately 40 years.
• Indonesia: The capital is Jakarta, the currency is the Indonesia Rupiah, and the President is Prabowo
Subianto.
• Australia: The capital is Canberra, the currency is the Australian Dollar, and the Prime Minister is Anthony
Albanese.
• New Zealand: The capital is Wellington, the currency is the New Zealand Dollar, and the Prime Minister
is Christopher Luxon.
Act East Policy
• Launched in 2014, the policy aims to strengthen India’s political, economic, and strategic ties with
Southeast Asia, East Asia, and the wider Indo-Pacific region.

Australia–Canada–India Technology and Innovation Partnership (ACITI)
• Launched on July 9, 2026, the partnership involves India, Australia, and Canada.
• The initiative was first announced following a meeting at the G20 summit in Johannesburg in November
2025.
• It focuses on cooperation in Artificial Intelligence (AI), green energy, critical minerals, resilient supply
chains, and net-zero technologies.
• The partnership leverages Canada’s AI research, India’s digital infrastructure and scale, and Australia’s
critical mineral resources.
• Critical Minerals: These include lithium, cobalt, nickel, and graphite, which are essential for batteries,
semiconductors, and defense equipment.
India–Australia Energy and Nuclear Cooperation
• Australia has agreed to export uranium to India for peaceful civil nuclear energy requirements.
• The agreement enhances India’s energy security and supports its clean energy programs.
• This builds upon the 2014 Civil Nuclear Cooperation Agreement.
• India’s nuclear programs are managed by the Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) under
the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE).
• Uranium: A radioactive mineral used as fuel in nuclear power plants for low-carbon electricity production.
Partnership on Cyber, Critical Technologies and Supply Chains (PACTS)
• Launched in 2026 by India and Australia to bolster cooperation in cyber security, critical technologies,
and defense research.
• PACTS replaces the 2020 Framework Arrangement on Cyber and Cyber Enabled Critical Technology
Cooperation.
• It is overseen by India’s Deputy National Security Advisor and Australia’s Deputy Secretary of the
International and Security Group.
• This initiative falls under the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership established in 2020.
India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership and Trade Goals
• Relations were elevated to a Strategic Partnership on July 11, 2026, in Auckland.
• The nations approved the "Strategic Partnership: Roadmap to 2030" to guide cooperation over the next
four years.
• The target is to double annual bilateral trade to ₹35,000 crore (7 billion NZD) by 2030.
• Cooperation spans 18 areas, including agriculture, education, maritime security, and innovation.
• Diplomatic relations began in 1952, and both nations are members of the Commonwealth of Nations.
