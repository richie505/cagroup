Grounds of Arrest Must be Given in Writing and in a Language Understood by the Accused: Supreme Court

• The Supreme Court clarified that every arrested person must be informed of the grounds of arrest in writing and in a language they understand; the protection under Article 22(1) applies irrespective of the nature or gravity of the offence.

• This safeguard applies to arrests for ordinary offences as well as those under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA).

• Written grounds should ordinarily be supplied at the time of arrest. In exceptional circumstances where this is not possible, the grounds may initially be communicated orally but must subsequently be provided in writing within a reasonable time.

• In all circumstances, written grounds must be supplied at least two hours before the arrested person is produced before a Magistrate for remand; failure to comply renders the arrest and subsequent remand illegal.

• If a person released due to violation of Article 22(1) is to be re-arrested, written grounds must first be provided and permission obtained from the concerned Judicial Magistrate.

• The re-arrest application must explain why the grounds were not supplied during the initial arrest; approval of the immediate superior of the original arresting officer must be obtained, and further investigation must be assigned to another officer. The Court also directed departmental disciplinary action against the officer responsible for the violation.

• Article 22(1) – Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention; right to be informed of the grounds of arrest; right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of one’s choice.

• Section 47 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) – Requires an arrested person to be informed of the grounds of arrest and information concerning the right to bail.

• India welcomed the initiative to provide a more accurate representation of global geography.

• Equal-area maps preserve the relative proportions of countries and continents more accurately, increasing their importance as an alternative to the Mercator Projection, widely used since the 16th century.

• In the Mercator map, high-latitude regions appear larger than their actual size, while areas near the Equator appear relatively smaller. As a result, regions such as Africa and India appear smaller, while Greenland appears disproportionately large.

• Africa is actually about 14 times larger than Greenland, although the two may appear nearly similar in size on a Mercator map. The proposed change concerns only the visual representation of land areas and does not alter national borders, territories or sovereignty.

• The Mercator Projection was developed by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569; it became useful for marine navigation because constant compass bearings appear as straight lines.

• When representing the Earth’s curved surface on a two-dimensional map, area, shape, distance and direction cannot all be preserved perfectly at the same time. Equal-area maps preserve relative area but may distort shapes, angles or distances.

Andhra Pradesh Governor Approves Human Organ Transplantation Amendment Act–2026

• The Governor approved the Human Organ Transplantation Amendment Act–2026 passed by the Andhra Pradesh Legislature; the Law Department notified it as Andhra Pradesh Act No. 25 of 2026.

• The amendment was introduced based on Supreme Court directions and Jeevandan Board proposals to improve transparency in organ transplantation and prevent illegal trafficking.

• The scope of the law has been expanded beyond organs to include transplantation of tissues such as skin, cornea, heart valves, muscles and bone marrow.

IIT Madras Develops Polymer Technology that Physically Kills Pathogenic Bacteria

• IIT Madras researchers developed and patented an advanced implant-grade polymer technology that physically destroys pathogenic bacteria without using antibiotics, disinfectants, radiation or conventional antimicrobial chemicals, primarily to address Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR).

• Nanoscale structures engineered on the polymer surface physically damage and kill bacteria that come into contact with them.

• Since the technology does not depend on antimicrobial chemicals, it can reduce selective pressure that contributes to drug resistance. Designed for polymers used in medical implants, the surface targets bacteria while remaining compatible with human cells.

• Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) – Occurs when bacteria, viruses, fungi or parasites develop resistance to medicines used against them; Superbugs are microorganisms resistant to multiple antimicrobial drugs.

• Nanostructures – Extremely small structures engineered at the nanometre scale; 1 nanometre = 10⁻⁹ metre.

• Biomaterials – Materials designed to interact with biological systems; used in implants, artificial organs, tissue engineering and medical devices.

India Gets ‘Dialogue Partner’ Status in GCAP Sixth-Generation Fighter Aircraft Programme

• India received ‘Dialogue Partner’ status in the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP) in August 2026, marking an important step towards studying sixth-generation fighter aircraft technologies.

• GCAP is a joint next-generation combat air programme of the United Kingdom, Japan and Italy. Dialogue Partner status does not make India a full member, co-developer, manufacturer or buyer.

• The status enables India to participate in discussions with the founding countries on the project’s concept, progress, capabilities and future technologies, including stealth, Artificial Intelligence (AI), sensors, electronic warfare, propulsion, networked warfare and weapons integration.

• GCAP was launched in December 2022 by bringing together the UK–Italy Tempest/Future Combat Air System and Japan’s F-X programme under a common framework; the next-generation fighter is targeted for induction by 2035.

• Major technology areas: Advanced stealth, AI, sensor fusion, electronic warfare, network-centric combat, next-generation propulsion and manned–unmanned teaming.

SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY ISRO Launches EOS-05 Satellite on September 4

• The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) launched the EOS-05 (GISAT-1A) Earth Observation Satellite aboard GSLV-F17 from the Second Launch Pad of the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota, on September 4, 2026.

• The 2,367-kg satellite was initially placed in a sub-Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit and will subsequently be moved to its designated geosynchronous orbit. GSLV-F17 marked the 19th launch of the GSLV series.

• Its primary objective is to frequently image the Indian subcontinent from an altitude of around 36,000 km over the equatorial region. It can generally observe the region every 30 minutes and priority areas during emergencies approximately every 5 minutes.

• EOS-05 is India’s first imaging satellite designed to operate from geosynchronous orbit. It will support agriculture, land resources, environmental and weather monitoring and disaster management.

• Unlike conventional Earth observation satellites operating mainly in Low Earth Orbit or Sun-Synchronous Orbit, EOS-05 can observe the same large geographical area with very high temporal frequency.

• Geostationary Orbit – Altitude: approximately 35,786 km above the equator; Orbital period: about 24 hours; a satellite appears stationary over the same region; used for communications, weather observation and continuous regional monitoring.

• Satish Dhawan Space Centre – Location: Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh; ISRO’s principal launch centre; Former name: Sriharikota Range; named after Professor Satish Dhawan.

Patent Granted for New Rocket Propellant Material ‘BAM-H24’

• The rocket-propellant high-energy material ‘BAM-H24’, jointly developed by DRDO and the University of Hyderabad’s Advanced Centre of Research in High Energy Materials, received an Indian patent.

• The boron-, hydrogen- and nitrogen-rich compound was designed for advanced rocket propulsion systems. Its high chemical and thermal stability and resistance to friction could provide improved safety compared with conventional high-energy materials.

• High specific impulse and low ignition delay can improve the performance of solid rocket propellant systems, while low moisture absorption enhances storage stability.

• The innovation can support the development of safer, more efficient and high-performance indigenous rocket propulsion materials.

• Specific Impulse – Key measure of rocket-engine efficiency; a higher value indicates better propulsion performance for a given quantity of propellant.

• Solid Propellant – Rocket propellant containing fuel and oxidiser in a solid energetic mixture; major advantages include long-term storage and rapid operational readiness.

• DRDO – Established: 1958; Ministry: Ministry of Defence; operates under the Department of Defence Research and Development.

India–France ‘TRISHNA’ Earth Observation Mission to be Launched by PSLV

• The joint India–France ‘TRISHNA’ Earth Observation Mission is scheduled to be launched next year aboard the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV).

• TRISHNA is being jointly developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and France’s National Centre for Space Studies (CNES).

• TRISHNA – Full form: Thermal InfraRed Imaging Satellite for High-resolution Natural resource Assessment.

• The mission will observe land-surface temperature, emissivity and biophysical-radiative variables at high spatial and temporal resolution to assess surface energy balance from regional to global scales.

• Major objectives include monitoring land energy and water balance, water stress and use, coastal and inland water quality and dynamics, urban heat islands, volcanic thermal anomalies, geothermal resources, snowmelt runoff and glacier dynamics.

• It will also provide information on Aerosol Optical Depth, atmospheric water vapour and cloud cover and support agriculture, water management, climate studies and natural-resource assessment.

• ISRO – Established: 1969; Headquarters: Bengaluru; Parent Department: Department of Space; Chairman: V. Narayanan.

• CNES – Established: 1961; Country: France; Headquarters: Paris.
