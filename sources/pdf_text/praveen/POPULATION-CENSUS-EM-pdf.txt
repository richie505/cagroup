Census 2027 – India’s First Fully Digital Census

• India’s first fully digital census began on April 1, 2026.

• It will be the 16th Census overall and the 8th Census after Independence.

• More than 3 million officials, enumerators, and supervisors will participate in this process.

• For the first time, data collection will be carried out through mobile applications.

• Citizens can undertake self-enumeration through a secure online portal available in 16 languages.

• After submitting details, households will receive a Self- Enumeration ID, which will be verified during the enumerator’s visit.

• The census will be conducted in accordance with the Census Act, 1948 and the Census Rules, 1990.

• The reference date for most parts of the country is March 1, 2027.

• For snow-bound regions such as Ladakh, Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand, the reference date is October 1, 2026. The Census will be conducted in two phases

• Phase I (April–September 2026): House listing, housing conditions, amenities, and assets.

• Phase II (February 2027): Population details, socio-economic information, educational information, and caste enumeration.

• The government has allocated ₹11,718.24 crore for this exercise.

• It will cover 36 States and Union Territories, more than 7,000 sub-districts, and about 6.4 lakh villages.

• Mobile applications, web-mapping tools, and real-time monitoring systems have been developed for the census process.

• A pre-test conducted in November 2025 confirmed the efficiency of the digital systems. Census in India

• First census: 1872; first complete census: 1881.

• It is conducted once every 10 years.

• Conducting authority: Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, India.

• Administrative Ministry: Ministry of Home Affairs. Census Act, 1948

• It provides the legal framework for conducting the census.

• It protects the confidentiality of personal data. Constitutional Importance

• Article 246 and the Seventh Schedule place census under the Union List.

• Articles 81 and 82 provide that allocation of Lok Sabha seats and delimitation of constituencies are based on census data. Previous Census

• India’s population in the 2011 Census was 121.08 crore.

• The 2021 Census was postponed due to COVID-19. Link with Digital India

• It is aligned with the Digital India Mission (2015).

• It will support data-driven policy formulation. Importance

• The census is crucial for designing welfare schemes and distribution of resources.

• Caste enumeration will have an impact on social justice policies.

Indian Census: Important Information from the Competitive Exam Perspective

• The Ministry of Home Affairs issued the Gazette notification related to the census.

• This will be the census conducted after a gap of 16 years in the country.

• It will be conducted in two phases during 2026–2027.

• It will be the 8th Census after Independence and the 16th overall. Special Feature – Caste Enumeration

• For the first time since 1931, caste enumeration will be carried out along with the census.

• Caste enumeration will also be conducted under Section 3 of the Census Act, 1948.

• Earlier, only SC/ST details were collected, but this time caste details of every individual will be recorded.

• For the first time, population data collection will be carried out entirely through a digital method. Historical Background

• First census in India: 1872

• First official census: 1881

• First census after Independence: 1951

• Last census: 2011 Significance

• It is important for framing government development programmes and welfare schemes.

• It plays a key role in the delimitation of Parliamentary and Legislative Assembly constituencies.

• It provides vital statistics for implementing social and economic policies. Additional Information

• The process will be carried out under the supervision of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner of India.
