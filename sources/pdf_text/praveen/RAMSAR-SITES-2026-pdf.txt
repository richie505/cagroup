Two New Ramsar Wetlands Added in India

• The Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Bhupender Yadav, announced the inclusion of two new wetlands in India’s Ramsar network.

• The newly designated Ramsar sites are Patna Bird Sanctuary in Etah district, Uttar Pradesh, and Chhari Dhand in Kutch district, Gujarat.

• With this addition, the total number of Ramsar sites in India has increased to 98.

• In 2014, India had only 26 Ramsar sites; the number has now risen to 98, marking a growth of over 276%.

• Prime Minister Narendra Modi expressed satisfaction over the inclusion of Patna Bird Sanctuary and Chhari Dhand, stating that it reflects India’s strong commitment to wetland conservation and biodiversity protection. Other Ramsar Sites Recognised in 2025

• In December 2025, the following wetlands were added to the Ramsar list

o Siliserh Lake, Alwar district, Rajasthan. o Kopra Reservoir, near Bilaspur, Chhattisgarh.

• With these additions, the total number of Ramsar sites in India reached 96 at that time.

• In October 2025, Goga Beel Lake in Bihar received Ramsar recognition. Wetlands Recognised in September

• Gokul Reservoir – Buxar district, Bihar.

• Udaipur Jheel – West Champaran district, Bihar. Ramsar Sites Added on Special Occasions

• On World Environment Day (5 June), India added two Ramsar sites in Rajasthan

o Menar (Udaipur district). o Khichan (Phalodi district).

• On World Wetlands Day (2 February), four wetlands were recognised

o Sakkarakottai Bird Sanctuary – Tamil Nadu. o Therthangal Bird Sanctuary – Tamil Nadu. o Khecheopalri Wetland – Sikkim. o Udhwa Lake – Jharkhand. Key Facts on Ramsar Sites in India

• First Ramsar sites in India

o Chilika Lake – Odisha. o Keoladeo National Park – Rajasthan.

• States with the highest number of Ramsar sites

o Tamil Nadu – 20. o Uttar Pradesh – 11.

• Largest Ramsar site in India

o Sundarbans Mangrove Forests, West Bengal.

• Smallest Ramsar site in India

o Renuka Wetland, Himachal Pradesh.

• Ramsar site refers to a wetland of international importance.

• The Ramsar Convention was signed in 1971 at Ramsar, Iran, under the aegis of UNESCO.

• Wetlands are crucial for ecosystem services, biodiversity conservation, and local livelihoods. India and the Ramsar Convention

• Date of India’s accession: 1 February 1982.

• Total Ramsar sites: 98.

• Total area covered: approximately 14 lakh hectares.

• Global ranking

o 1st in Asia. o 3rd in the world.
