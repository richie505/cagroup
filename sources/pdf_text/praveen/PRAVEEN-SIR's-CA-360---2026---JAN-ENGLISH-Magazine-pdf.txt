United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)

• India has assumed membership of the United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) for the 2026–28 term.

• ECOSOC was established in 1945 under the UN Charter, is one of the six principal organs of the United Nations, and marked its 80th anniversary on January 23, 2026.

• The Council consists of 54 member states, each serving a three-year term.

• It serves as the central platform within the UN for discussions on economic, social and humanitarian issues.

• The main objectives of ECOSOC include raising living standards, achieving full employment, improving health and education, promoting cultural cooperation, and protecting human rights.

• It acts as the coordinating body for 15 UN specialised agencies, including WHO, UNESCO, ILO and FAO.

• ECOSOC oversees the work of eight functional commissions and five regional commissions.

• It organises the High-Level Political Forum (HLPF) to review progress on the implementation of the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

• It also holds joint meetings with the IMF and the World Bank to support coordination of global economic policies.

India–Pakistan Annual Exchange of Lists (Confidence Building Measure)

• India and Pakistan exchange lists of nuclear installations and civilian prisoners–fishermen with each other every year on January 1.

• This is one of the oldest ongoing bilateral confidence-building measures between the two countries.

• The Agreement on the Prohibition of Attacks against Nuclear Installations and Facilities was signed on December 31, 1988, came into force in 1991, and the first exchange of lists took place on January 1, 1992.

• Under the agreement, lists of nuclear power plants, research reactors, and fuel fabrication and enrichment facilities are exchanged annually.

• Despite wars, terrorist attacks and diplomatic tensions, the process has continued uninterrupted, serving as an example of nuclear restraint and regional stability.

United States Officially Withdraws from the World Health Organization (WHO)

• The United States is set to officially withdraw from the World Health Organization (WHO) after completing the mandatory one-year notice period.

• Under US law, full payment of outstanding financial obligations is required along with the notice of withdrawal.

• Delays in payment of membership dues have raised legal questions, and the final procedures are under discussion within the WHO Executive Board.

• WHO is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for international public health coordination.

• Established on April 7, 1948, WHO is headquartered in Geneva and has 194 member states.

• Major achievements of WHO include the eradication of smallpox, progress towards polio eradication, and global coordination during Ebola and COVID-19 outbreaks.

• The US withdrawal could create a funding gap of about 18% in WHO’s budget, potentially weakening pandemic preparedness and disease surveillance.

• It may also reduce US influence in shaping global health data systems and international health standards.

Governor’s Address to the State Legislature – Constitutional Position

• The Governor’s address is a mandatory constitutional process delivered at the first session of the State Legislature after general elections and at the beginning of the first session each year.

• The address reflects only the policies and priorities of the elected state government and not the personal views of the Governor.

• Under Article 163, the Governor is generally bound to act on the advice of the Council of Ministers, except in limited discretionary situations.

• Powers related to summoning, proroguing or dissolving the legislature under Article 174 must be exercised only on cabinet advice.

• The Governor has no authority to alter, delete or rewrite the address prepared by the elected government.

• The address is symbolic and procedural, serving to communicate government policy to the legislature.

• Article 176 mandates discussion on the Governor’s address in the legislature, ensuring democratic oversight.

• In the Nabam Rebia case (2016), the Supreme Court clarified that the Governor has no independent discretionary power under Article 174.

Sammakka–Saralamma Jatara

• The biennial Sammakka–Saralamma Jatara was held with grandeur at Medaram village in Mulugu district of Telangana, within the Eturunagaram Wildlife Sanctuary area.

• The festival honours Sammakka and Saralamma, ancestral deities of the Koya tribal community, and is recognised as Asia’s largest tribal congregation and one of the largest human gatherings in the world.

• Held during the Hindu month of Magha on full-moon day, the jatara commemorates the resistance of Sammakka–Saralamma against oppressive taxation by the Kakatiya rulers.

• Instead of permanent temples, the deities are symbolically brought from the forests, and all rituals are performed in traditional ways by Koya tribal priests.

• Offering jaggery as “gold” instead of money or jewellery is a unique tradition of the festival.

• Worship is centred around sacred trees, bamboo symbols, Dalgudda flags and clan insignia rather than idols.

• More than one crore devotees attend each jatara, making it the largest gathering in India after the Kumbh Mela.

• The festival reflects tribal identity, collective memory and resistance against historical injustice.

Repatriation of Ancient Bronze Sculptures from the United States to India

• The Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art in the United States announced the return of three ancient Indian bronze sculptures to India.

• The decision followed a detailed review of provenance and acquisition history, reflecting the growing emphasis on ethical museum practices.

• The artefacts include a 10th-century Chola-period Shiva Nataraja, a 12th-century Somaskanda sculpture, and a 16th-century Vijayanagara-period sculpture of saints Sundarar and Paravai.

• All three are exemplary works of South Indian bronze sculptural tradition, originally created for temple worship and ritual processions.

• The repatriation is a significant example of global efforts to return illegally removed cultural heritage to its rightful country of origin.

World’s Oldest Cave Art Discovered in Indonesia

• Scientists discovered the world’s oldest known cave art in a limestone cave in the Muna region of Sulawesi Island, Indonesia.

• The hand-stencil cave painting has been dated to at least 67,800 years old, making it significantly older than famous cave art found in Spain and France.

• The discovery establishes that ancient human artistic expression was not confined to Europe and evolved independently in multiple regions of the world.

• These findings reshape scientific understanding of early human culture, cognition and symbolic thought.

Rare Gita Upadesa Wall Sculpture Identified at Pushpagiri

• A rare wall sculpture depicting Lord Krishna delivering the Bhagavad Gita discourse was identified on the wall of the Chennakesava Temple at Pushpagiri in Vallur Mandal, YSR Kadapa district of Andhra Pradesh.

• The sculpture was identified by writer and historical researcher Bommisetti Ramesh.

• Unlike common depictions, the sculpture shows Lord Krishna seated while Arjuna stands, symbolising Arjuna’s hesitation before the Kurukshetra war.

• The scene creatively portrays Krishna urging Arjuna to uphold dharma and perform his duty, with Arjuna shown offering salutations with folded hands and bow.

• The sculpture is considered a fine example of the artistic skill and expressive depth of medieval sculptors.
