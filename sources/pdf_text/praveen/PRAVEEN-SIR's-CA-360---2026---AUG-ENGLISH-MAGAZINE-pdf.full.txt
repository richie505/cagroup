India Gifts Five Peafowls to UN Office in Geneva
• On 7 August 2026, India gifted five peafowls to the United Nations Office at Geneva (UNOG), comprising
four Blue Indian Peafowls and one rare White Peafowl. They will be housed at Ariana Park within the
Palais des Nations complex in Geneva.
• Arindam Bagchi, India’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations in Geneva, formally handed
over the birds to UN officials. The Indian Peacock symbolises India’s natural heritage and cultural identity
and was declared the National Bird of India in 1963.
• In 1981, then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi gifted two peafowls to the UN Office in Geneva. The latest
gesture revives this tradition of cultural diplomacy.
• Indian Peacock Scientific name: Pavo cristatus; National Bird of India since 1963; protected under
Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
• UNOG Location: Palais des Nations, Geneva, Switzerland; one of the major United Nations centres
outside the UN Headquarters in New York.
Russia Imports Gasoline from India for the First Time
• Amid domestic fuel shortages, Russia began importing gasoline from India in 2026 for the first time in its
history. The first cargo reached Russia in August 2026. Around 42,000 tonnes of gasoline was loaded at
the Vadinar Refinery in Gujarat on 18 June 2026 and transshipped through the Port of Damietta in Egypt.
• The Vadinar Refinery is operated by Nayara Energy Limited, which has ownership links with Russia’s
state-controlled Rosneft.
• While India imports large quantities of Russian crude oil, Russia’s purchase of gasoline refined in India
marks a significant shift in India–Russia energy trade.
• Disruptions to refinery operations and reduced domestic supplies have prompted Russia to procure
gasoline from markets including India and Belarus.
India–SACU Sign Terms of Reference for Preferential Trade Agreement Negotiations
• India and the Southern African Customs Union (SACU) signed the Terms of Reference (ToR) at Vanijya
Bhawan in New Delhi in August 2026 to initiate negotiations for a Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA).
It is India’s first such ToR with the African region.
• The ToR defines the scope and modalities of future negotiations aimed at providing preferential market
access and tariff concessions on selected goods. It seeks to strengthen India–SACU trade, investment and
broader economic relations.
• A PTA provides tariff concessions only on selected products and therefore has a narrower scope than a
Free Trade Agreement (FTA).
• SACU — Established: 1910; Headquarters: Windhoek, Namibia; Members: Botswana, Eswatini, Lesotho,
Namibia and South Africa; Total members: 5.
• SACU is recognised as the world’s oldest functioning customs union. Its key objectives include free
movement of goods among member states and the application of a Common External Tariff on imports
from non-member countries.

developed through historic maritime trade routes.

Trump Renames ‘Lake Ontario’ as ‘Lake America’
• US President Donald Trump signed an Executive Order on 27 August 2026 directing that Lake Ontario be
renamed Lake America for official US federal government use.
• The change applies only to US federal usage. Canada will continue to refer to it as Lake Ontario, according
to Prime Minister Mark Carney.
• Lake Ontario lies along the US–Canada border, bordered by New York State to the south and east and the
Canadian province of Ontario to the north and west.
• Earlier, Trump renamed the Gulf of Mexico as the Gulf of America for official US federal use.
• The five Great Lakes of North America are Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario. Lake Michigan
is the only one located entirely within the United States.

Nauru Officially Renamed ‘Republic of Naoero’
• The Pacific island nation of Nauru officially changed its name to the Republic of Naoero. The
constitutional amendment was approved in May 2026, and President David Adeang formally announced
the change on 31 July 2026.
• “Naoero” is the country’s indigenous name, while “Nauru” originated during the colonial period. Although
the official short name has changed to Naoero, the country code NRO remains unchanged, and its citizens
are referred to as Dei-Naoero.
• The name change was communicated to the United Nations on 26 June 2026.
• By area, it is the world’s third-smallest sovereign country after Vatican City and Monaco. It has no official
capital; Yaren District serves as its administrative centre.
• Republic of Naoero Administrative Centre: Yaren; Currency: Australian Dollar; President: David Adeang;
Official languages: Nauruan and English.
• Nauruan language Indigenous name: Dorerin Naoero; UNESCO classification: critically endangered
language.
Nauru Launches Climate-Funded Citizenship-by-Investment Programme
• Nauru launched the Nauru Economic and Climate Resilience Citizenship Program (ECRCP), a climate-
funded Citizenship-by-Investment initiative that grants citizenship to eligible foreign investors in return
for financial contributions.
• Funds raised through the programme will be used for climate-resilient infrastructure and the relocation of
communities.
• Against the threat of rising sea levels, the programme primarily aims to raise funds to relocate around 90%
of the country’s households and critical infrastructure to higher ground.
• A distinctive feature of the programme is its use of an alternative funding mechanism for climate resilience,
reducing dependence on traditional debt-based multilateral climate finance.
Venezuela Withdraws from the Rome Statute
• Venezuela formally notified the United Nations of its withdrawal from the Rome Statute, initiating the
process of ending its membership in the International Criminal Court (ICC).
• The decision was announced by the government led by interim President Delcy Eloína Rodríguez Gómez.
• Under Article 127 of the Rome Statute, withdrawal takes effect one year after notification. However, it
does not affect ICC investigations or proceedings that had already commenced.
• Venezuela became a State Party to the Rome Statute in 2002. The Statute gives the ICC jurisdiction over
genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and the crime of aggression.
• The United Nations Independent International Fact-Finding Mission stated that the decision could reduce
victims’ opportunities to obtain justice. Venezuela argued that the ICC disproportionately targets certain
regions.
• ICC Established: 2002; Headquarters: The Hague, Netherlands; Legal basis: Rome Statute, 1998.
• Venezuela Capital: Caracas; Currency: Venezuelan Bolívar; Interim President: Delcy Eloína Rodríguez
Gómez.

Parichha Weir Recognised as ICID World Heritage Irrigation Structure
• The International Commission on Irrigation and Drainage (ICID) recognised the nearly 140-year-old
Parichha Weir in Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh, as a World Heritage Irrigation Structure on 13 August 2026.
• Located on the Betwa River and linked to the Betwa Canal System, it provides irrigation to parts of Uttar
Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh.
• Captain Strachey proposed the canal system in 1855; construction began in 1881 at about ₹40 lakh and it
became operational in 1886.
• After Sukwa–Dukwan Weir received recognition in 2022, Parichha Weir became the second major ICID
heritage irrigation structure in the Jhansi region.
• World Heritage Irrigation Structure status recognises historic irrigation works of outstanding engineering,
management, cultural and historical significance.
• ICID Established: 1950; Headquarters: New Delhi; Areas: irrigation, drainage and agricultural water
management.

to national and financial security; violations may attract action under Legal Metrology provisions.

• Three Rio Conventions: UNFCCC–Climate Change; CBD–Biodiversity; UNCCD–Desertification and
Land Degradation.
• World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought — 17 June; observed to commemorate the adoption of
UNCCD on 17 June 1994.

Draft Rules Issued for Regulation of Nuclear Reactors under the SHANTI Act
• The Central Government has released draft rules relating to nuclear reactors, licensing, financial security
and civil liability under the Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy (SHANTI) Act,
2025. The draft rules were issued by the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE).
• Reactor operators must maintain insurance, financial security, or a combination of both to ensure payment
of compensation in the event of a nuclear accident.
• Financial security must be maintained until the spent nuclear fuel used in the reactor is completely removed
from the storage pool.
• The Central Government will constitute an expert committee every five years to review the civil liability
limit applicable to a reactor operator in the event of a nuclear accident.
• If a proposed nuclear reactor is designed abroad, its design must have approval from the competent nuclear
regulatory authority of the country concerned. Such a reactor must already be operational in that country
