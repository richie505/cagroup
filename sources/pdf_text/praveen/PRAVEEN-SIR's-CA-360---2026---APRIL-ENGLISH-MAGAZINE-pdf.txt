Kalpakkam PFBR Criticality

• 500 MW PFBR at Kalpakkam achieved criticality using indigenous technology; initiated self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction.

• Second stage of India’s three-stage nuclear program; second country after Russia with commercial fast breeder reactor.

• Uses uranium–plutonium mixed oxide fuel, liquid sodium cooling; U-238 blanket produces additional fuel.

• Lays foundation for third stage and thorium utilization; enhances fuel output, reduces waste, with inherent safety features.

• Three stages: PHWR (natural uranium), FBR (plutonium), Thorium-based reactors.

• Kalpakkam site established 2003; under Department of Atomic Energy; includes MAPS, IGCAR, PFBR centers.

• Major nuclear power plants: Tarapur, Rajasthan, Kalpakkam, Narora, Kakrapar, Kaiga, Kudankulam.

• Indian scientists developed bio-bitumen using crop residues with indigenous technology; serves as alternative to conventional bitumen, reduces residue burning and air pollution.

• Developed by CSIR and CSIR-CRRI; pyrolysis converts crop residues into bio-oil, used as bio-binder, reducing dependence on crude oil and lowering carbon emissions.

• Provides farmers additional income through residue sale; promotes rural employment and sustainable development.

• India generates \~60 million tonnes of crop residues annually; use in road construction promotes circular economy.

• Pyrolysis: thermochemical decomposition under oxygen-free conditions; produces bio-oil, biochar, syngas.

• Supports environmental protection; aligned with National Policy for Crop Residue Management, SATAT (2018), GoBardhan (2018).

• In India, 13% of breast cancer patients are diagnosed only after metastasis; study conducted on 76,000 women between 2009–2020.
