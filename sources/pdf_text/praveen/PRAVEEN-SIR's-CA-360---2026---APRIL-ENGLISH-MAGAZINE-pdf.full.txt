UAE Exit from OPEC and OPEC+
• The United Arab Emirates announced its exit from OPEC and OPEC+ from May 1 due to dissatisfaction
with production limits and strained relations with Saudi Arabia.
• The UAE has been an OPEC member since 1967, aiming to be a reliable global energy supplier and
achieve long-term strategic and economic benefits.
• OPEC+ includes 12 OPEC and 10 non-OPEC countries, contributing about 59% of global crude
production, with monthly decisions to stabilize the market.
• UAE’s exit may increase oil production and impact global prices; production declined from 3.60 to 2.34
million barrels in UAE and from 10.40 to 7.80 million barrels in Saudi Arabia.
• This reflects changes in global energy geopolitics and production strategies.
• OPEC members include Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran, UAE, Kuwait, Venezuela, Nigeria, Algeria, Libya,
Gabon, Congo, and Equatorial Guinea.
• OPEC was established in 1960, headquartered in Vienna (Austria), to coordinate oil policies and stabilize
markets.
• UAE capital is Abu Dhabi, currency is UAE Dirham, and President is Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan.
Jordan Joins ISA, CDRI, and GBA
• Jordan joined the International Solar Alliance, Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure, and Global
Biofuels Alliance in April 2026 by submitting membership documents in New Delhi.
• The aim is to strengthen cooperation with India in clean energy, climate resilience, and sustainable fuels,
while boosting India’s global climate leadership.
• ISA targets 50% renewable electricity by 2030 with storage and low-interest financing; CDRI focuses on
resilient infrastructure; GBA promotes biofuels to reduce import dependence.
• India–Jordan diplomatic relations mark 75 years, with a trade target of USD 3–5 billion.
• ISA was established in 2015 (Gurugram), CDRI in 2019 (New Delhi), and GBA launched in 2023 (India).
• Jordan’s capital is Amman, currency is Jordanian Dinar, and King is Abdullah II.

• UNESCO was established in 1945, headquartered in Paris, and is a UN specialized agency.

Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023 Implementation
• The Government brought the Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023 into force with effect from April
16, 2026.
• It is a key procedural step for implementing 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative
Assemblies.
• Article 330A provides reservation in Lok Sabha and Article 332A in State Assemblies, including
representation for SC and ST women.
• Reservation seats will rotate after delimitation, and implementation depends on the next Census.
• The next Census is expected in 2027, and the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 proposes
delimitation based on 2011 data.
• The aim is to enable implementation before the 2029 general elections, with the Act passed in September
2023 and valid for 15 years.
• Delimitation Commission redraws constituency boundaries based on population, and Census is conducted
every 10 years under the Census Act, 1948.
• Women’s representation in the current Lok Sabha is about 14–15%, and the Women’s Reservation Bill
was first introduced in 1996.
Rejection of Women’s Reservation Bill (Constitution 131st Amendment Bill)
• The Women’s Reservation Bill, also called the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, was rejected in the
Lok Sabha.
• The bill aimed to fast-track implementation of women’s reservation through delimitation without waiting
for Census data.
• A total of 528 members voted, with 298 in favour and 230 against.
• The bill failed as it did not secure the required special majority of 326 votes under Article 368.
• Passage requires a majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting; state
ratification is needed if it affects representation.
• Lok Sabha was established in 1952 with a maximum strength of 552, current strength 543, and a tenure of
5 years under Article 83.
Delimitation of Constituencies
• Article 82 applies to Lok Sabha and Article 170 to State Assemblies for delimitation based on Census
data.

courts.
• Parliament set up commissions in 1952, 1963, 1973, and 2002.
• The 42nd Amendment (1976) froze seats until 2000, and the 84th Amendment (2002) extended the freeze
till 2026.

GEOGRAPHY AND ENVIRONMENT
Shekha Jheel Bird Sanctuary – India’s 99th Ramsar Site
• Shekha Jheel Bird Sanctuary near Aligarh in Uttar Pradesh was designated as India’s 99th Ramsar site on
April 22, 2026, announced by Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav.
• It is a freshwater wetland supporting groundwater recharge and irrigation, and hosts over 180 migratory
bird species in winter.
• It lies on the Central Asian Flyway and serves as a key resting and habitat site for waterbirds and wetland
species.
• Ramsar sites in India increased from 26 in 2014 to 99 in 2026, nearing the milestone of 100 sites.
• In 2026, Patna Bird Sanctuary (Uttar Pradesh) and Chhari-Dhand (Gujarat) raised the count to 98, followed
by Shekha Jheel as the 99th.
• In 2025, sites like Siliserh Lake (Rajasthan), Kopra Reservoir (Chhattisgarh), Goga Beel, Gokul Reservoir,
Udaipur Jheel (Bihar), Menar, and Khichan (Rajasthan) were added.
• On World Wetlands Day, sites such as Sakkarakottai, Thirthangal (Tamil Nadu), Khecheopalri (Sikkim),
and Udhwa Lake (Jharkhand) were included.
• The first Ramsar sites in India were Chilika Lake and Keoladeo National Park; Tamil Nadu and Uttar
Pradesh have the highest numbers, with Sundarbans as the largest and Renuka as the smallest.
• Ramsar Convention was signed in 1971, India joined on February 1, 1982, with total area about 1.4 million
hectares, ranking first in Asia and third globally.

• Yana Caves near Gokarna in Uttara Kannada district of Karnataka were declared a Geo-heritage site of

• The formations are about 2,700 million years old, located in the Western Ghats, formed due to tectonic
activity and karst processes.
• The aim is to preserve geological heritage with scientific, educational, and cultural significance.
• Geological Survey of India was established in 1851, headquartered in Kolkata, under the Ministry of
Mines, with Director General Asit Saha.
