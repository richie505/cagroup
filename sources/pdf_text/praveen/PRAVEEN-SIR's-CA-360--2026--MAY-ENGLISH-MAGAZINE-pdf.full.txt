United Arab Emirates (UAE) Visit – 15 May 2026
• During his visit to the UAE on 15 May 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi held discussions with
President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan on bilateral and regional issues.

defence cooperation and infrastructure investments.

establish a ship repair facility at Vadinar, Gujarat.

West Asian issues should be resolved through dialogue, and that attacks by Iran on the UAE were
unacceptable.

Bangladesh Approves Padma River Project
• Bangladesh approved a new project on the Padma (Ganga) River amid concerns related to Farakka Barrage
water-sharing issues.
• The decision was taken at a meeting of the National Economic Council chaired by Prime Minister Tarique
Rahman.
• The 1996 Ganga Water Sharing Treaty between India and Bangladesh is set to expire in December 2026.
• The project involves an investment of 34,497 crore Taka (approximately ₹2,677 crore), with the primary
objective of storing water within Bangladesh.
• According to Bangladesh Water Resources Minister Shahiduddin Chowdhury Annie, the project is not
connected to the 54 shared rivers between India and Bangladesh.
• The development highlights the growing importance of transboundary river-water management in South
Asia.
• The Farakka Barrage was commissioned in 1975 on the Ganga River in Murshidabad district of West
Bengal to divert water to the Hooghly River and improve navigation at Kolkata Port.
• The Ganga Water Sharing Treaty was signed between India and Bangladesh in 1996 for a period of 30
years.
• Padma is the name by which the Ganga River is known in Bangladesh.

Abraham Accords – US Initiative for Normalisation of Relations in West Asia
• The Abraham Accords are a series of agreements brokered by the United States to normalize diplomatic
relations between Israel and Muslim-majority countries.
• The accords are named after Abraham, a figure revered in Judaism, Christianity and Islam.
• Their primary objective is to strengthen diplomatic, economic, technological and security cooperation
among participating countries.
• The United States serves as the mediator, while Israel remains the principal partner in the initiative.
• The United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Morocco became the first signatories in 2020, followed later by
Kosovo, Sudan, Somaliland and Kazakhstan.
• Discussions are ongoing to bring countries such as Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, Türkiye, Egypt and
Jordan into the framework.
• The accords seek to promote regional stability, security cooperation, trade, investment, technology
partnerships, defence cooperation and economic integration.
• They also facilitate the expansion of bilateral relations with Israel without directly linking them to the
resolution of the Palestinian issue.
• Abraham Accords: Launched – 2020 | Mediator – United States.

11th Review Conference of the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT)
• The 11th Review Conference of the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), held at the United Nations
Headquarters in New York, concluded without consensus on a final declaration.
• Conducted every five years since 1975, the conference brings together nearly 190 member states to discuss
nuclear disarmament, non-proliferation, safeguards and global security issues.
• The NPT, signed in 1968, is based on three pillars: nuclear non-proliferation, nuclear disarmament and the
peaceful use of nuclear energy.
• It recognises only the United States, Russia, China, the United Kingdom and France as Nuclear Weapon
States, as they conducted nuclear tests before 1 January 1967.
• The treaty requires non-nuclear weapon states not to develop nuclear weapons and obligates nuclear
weapon states to pursue disarmament.
• The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is responsible for inspecting and monitoring the nuclear
programmes of member countries.
• NPT: Opened for Signature – 1968 | Entered into Force – 1970 | Indefinitely Extended – 1995.
• India is not a signatory to the NPT, maintaining that the treaty is discriminatory because it recognises only
five countries as nuclear weapon states.
• IAEA: Established – 1957 | Headquarters – Vienna, Austria | Director General – Rafael Mariano Grossi.

ICAR Recommends Ammonium Sulphate as Alternative to Urea
• The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) has advised states to promote ammonium sulphate
fertiliser in paddy cultivation to reduce dependence on urea.
• Ammonium sulphate is a water-soluble inorganic fertiliser containing 21% nitrogen and 24% sulphur, with
nitrogen available in ammonium form that can be readily absorbed by plants.
• It is particularly useful in alkaline soils, helping reduce soil pH while improving nutrient balance and
providing quick nutrient availability to crops.
• The fertiliser is produced by reacting ammonia with sulphuric acid and is also obtained as a by-product of
caprolactam production and coke-oven gas processing in the steel industry.
• The recommendation aims to encourage sustainable fertiliser use, as excessive urea application can
adversely affect soil quality.
• ICAR: Established – 1929 | Headquarters – New Delhi | Under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers’
Welfare.
• Major plant nutrients: Nitrogen – growth | Phosphorus – root development | Potassium – plant health.
• Paddy is India’s major food-grain crop and requires high levels of water and nutrients.
