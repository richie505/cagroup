Bangladesh Approves Padma River Project

• Bangladesh approved a new project on the Padma (Ganga) River amid concerns related to Farakka Barrage water-sharing issues.

• The decision was taken at a meeting of the National Economic Council chaired by Prime Minister Tarique Rahman.

• The 1996 Ganga Water Sharing Treaty between India and Bangladesh is set to expire in December 2026.

• The project involves an investment of 34,497 crore Taka (approximately ₹2,677 crore), with the primary objective of storing water within Bangladesh.

• According to Bangladesh Water Resources Minister Shahiduddin Chowdhury Annie, the project is not connected to the 54 shared rivers between India and Bangladesh.

• The development highlights the growing importance of transboundary river-water management in South Asia.

• The Farakka Barrage was commissioned in 1975 on the Ganga River in Murshidabad district of West Bengal to divert water to the Hooghly River and improve navigation at Kolkata Port.

• The Ganga Water Sharing Treaty was signed between India and Bangladesh in 1996 for a period of 30 years.

• Padma is the name by which the Ganga River is known in Bangladesh.

Abraham Accords – US Initiative for Normalisation of Relations in West Asia

• The Abraham Accords are a series of agreements brokered by the United States to normalize diplomatic relations between Israel and Muslim-majority countries.

• The accords are named after Abraham, a figure revered in Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

• Their primary objective is to strengthen diplomatic, economic, technological and security cooperation among participating countries.

• The United States serves as the mediator, while Israel remains the principal partner in the initiative.

• The United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Morocco became the first signatories in 2020, followed later by Kosovo, Sudan, Somaliland and Kazakhstan.

• Discussions are ongoing to bring countries such as Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, Türkiye, Egypt and Jordan into the framework.

• The accords seek to promote regional stability, security cooperation, trade, investment, technology partnerships, defence cooperation and economic integration.

• They also facilitate the expansion of bilateral relations with Israel without directly linking them to the resolution of the Palestinian issue.

National Sports Board and Tribunal Rules, 2026 Notified

• The Central Government notified the National Sports Governance (National Sports Board) Rules, 2026 and National Sports Governance (National Sports Tribunal) Rules, 2026 under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025.

• The National Sports Board will serve as the apex body for recognition of National Sports Federations and oversee governance, financial management and ethical standards.

• The Board will consist of a Chairperson and two members, appointed by the Central Government on the recommendation of a Search-cum-Selection Committee.

• Members will have a tenure of three years, subject to a maximum age of 65 years.

• They cannot hold positions in any national or international sports body during their tenure.

• The National Sports Tribunal will function as a specialised forum for resolving sports-related disputes.

• Online filing and virtual hearing facilities have been provided.

• The Tribunal Chairperson will serve for four years or up to 70 years of age, while members will serve for four years or up to 67 years of age.

• Former members cannot practise before the Tribunal after retirement and cannot undertake arbitration work while in office.

• The National Sports Governance Act, 2025 aims to strengthen transparency and accountability in sports administration.

Five-Time Olympian and Arjuna Awardee Raja Randhir Singh Passes Away

• Veteran Indian shooter, five-time Olympian and sports administrator Raja Randhir Singh passed away in New Delhi in May 2026 at the age of 79. Born on 18 October 1946 in Patiala, Punjab, he received the Arjuna Award in 1979 for his contributions to shooting.

• A specialist in Trap and Skeet shooting, he represented India at the 1968 Mexico City, 1972 Munich, 1976 Montreal, 1980 Moscow and 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, and was also selected as a reserve shooter for the 1964 Tokyo Olympics.

• He created history by winning the gold medal in Men’s Trap Shooting at the 1978 Bangkok Asian Games, becoming the first Indian shooter to win an Asian Games gold medal.

• He served as former Secretary General of the Indian Olympic Association and became the first Indian to serve as President of the Olympic Council of Asia.
