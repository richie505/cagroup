Union Budget 2026–27

• The Budget is a financial statement that addresses the country’s economic and social challenges (Annual Statement of Revenue and Expenditure / Annual Financial Statement – Article 112, Indian Constitution).

• If the Economic Survey is considered a diagnosis, the Budget can be seen as the remedy.

• This is the 9th Budget presented by Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman. It is the 6th Digital Budget of India.

• For the first time in independent India, a paperless (Digital) Budget was presented for FY 2021–22 by Nirmala Sitharaman.

• Nirmala Sitharaman is also the woman Finance Minister who has presented the Budget the highest number of times (9 times). ➢ Budget History

• The first Budget of independent India was presented on November 26, 1947, by R.K. Shanmukham Chetty.

• The first Budget of Republican India was presented by John Mathai.

• The person who presented the Budget the highest number of times (10 times) is Morarji Desai.

• Jawaharlal Nehru presented the Budget while serving as Prime Minister.

• The first woman and woman Prime Minister to present the Budget was Indira Gandhi.

• The woman Finance Minister who presented the Budget the most times is Nirmala Sitharaman. ➢ Interesting Facts about the Budget

• The Budget was presented even before India gained Independence.

• It was first presented on April 7, 1860. Scottish economist James Wilson of the East India Company presented the Budget to the British Crown. ➢ First Budget of Independent India

• The first Union Budget of independent India was presented on November 26, 1947, by the first Finance Minister R.K. Shanmukham Chetty.

• The Budget covered a period of four months, up to March 31, 1948.

• The estimated expenditure was ₹171.15 crore.

• The estimated fiscal deficit was ₹24.59 crore.

• Earlier, Liaquat Ali Khan had presented the 1947–48 Budget as a member of the Interim Government. ➢ Changes in Budget Printing

• Until 1950, the Budget documents were printed at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

• After a Budget leak in 1950, printing was shifted to Mint Road.

• In 1980, the government decided to set up a printing press in North Block for Budget printing. Since then, printing has been carried out there.

• The Halwa ceremony before Budget printing became a tradition.

• Until 1955, the Budget was presented only in English. Later, it began to be printed in both Hindi and English. ➢ Halwa Ceremony

• Every year, the Halwa ceremony is conducted before the printing of Budget documents. The Finance Minister participates in the ceremony.

• The ceremony is held on the ground floor of North Block and signifies that the Budget has been finalized and printing has begun.

• Budget matters are kept highly confidential. Officials involved in Budget preparation are not allowed to meet even their families until the Budget is presented in Parliament.

• The Finance Minister serves Halwa to the officials.

• Printing begins about ten days before the Budget presentation.

• Officials and staff involved in the process stay within the premises and remain under 24-hour Intelligence Bureau surveillance.

• They are not permitted to use phones or meet outsiders; in emergencies, communication is allowed only under security supervision.

• Budget printing continued at Rashtrapati Bhavan until 1950, then shifted to Minto Road, and later to the basement of North Block, where it continues.

➢ Prime Ministers Who Presented the Budget

• Jawaharlal Nehru, Indira Gandhi, and Rajiv Gandhi presented the Budget while serving as Prime Minister. ➢ Highest Number of Budget Presentations

• Former Prime Minister Morarji Desai presented the Budget 10 times (1962–69 as Finance Minister).

• In the leap years 1964 and 1968, he presented the Budget on his birthday, February 29.

• P. Chidambaram presented the Budget 9 times.

• Pranab Mukherjee presented it 8 times.

• Yashwant Sinha presented it 8 times.

• Manmohan Singh presented it 6 times.

• Recently, Nirmala Sitharaman presented her 9th Budget. ➢ Change in Budget Timing

• Until 1999, the Budget was presented at 5 PM on the last working day of February.

• Yashwant Sinha changed this British-era practice and began presenting it at 11 AM. ➢ Change in Budget Date

• Until 2016, the Budget was presented on the last working day of February.

• From 2017 onwards, then Finance Minister Arun Jaitley changed the date to February 1. ➢ Longest and Shortest Budget Documents

• In 1991, under Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao, Finance Minister Manmohan Singh presented the longest Budget speech/document with 18,650 words.

• In 1977, Finance Minister H.M. Patel presented the shortest Budget, containing only about 800 words. ➢ Longest Budget Speech

• Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman delivered the longest Budget speech on February 1, 2020, which lasted 2 hours and 42 minutes. ➢ Paperless Budget

• On February 1, 2021, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman presented the first paperless Budget, considering the COVID-19 pandemic situation. ➢ Merger of Railway Budget

• Before 2017, the Annual Budget and Railway Budget were presented separately.

• In 2017, they were merged. ➢ History of Railway Budget Merger

• Since 1860, the British presented a Budget for India.

• From 1924 onwards, a separate Railway Budget was introduced.

• From then until 2016, Railway and General Budgets were presented separately.

• In 2017, under the Narendra Modi government, Finance Minister Arun Jaitley merged the Railway Budget with the General Budget. ➢ Women Who Presented the Budget

• Indira Gandhi was the first woman to present the Budget (1970–71).

• In 2019, Nirmala Sitharaman became the second woman to present the Budget as Finance Minister.

• She replaced the traditional briefcase with a “Bahi-Khata” bearing the National Emblem. ➢ Evolution to Paperless Budget

• Earlier, following British tradition, Finance Ministers carried Budget documents in a red briefcase to Parliament.

• This practice continued until 2019.

• After the COVID-19 outbreak, in 2021, Nirmala Sitharaman introduced the paperless Budget.

• In recent years, the Budget has been stored digitally on tablets and distributed electronically to Members of Parliament.

Union Budget 2026–27: Key Highlights

• Union Minister for Finance and Corporate Affairs, Smt. Nirmala Sitharaman, presented the Union Budget 2026–27 in Parliament.

• This is the first Budget prepared at Kartavya Bhavan.

• The Budget is structured around three “Kartavyas” (three duties). ➢ Three Kartavyas

• First Kartavya: Accelerate economic growth by raising productivity and competitiveness, and build resilience against global economic uncertainties.

• Second Kartavya: Fulfil people’s aspirations, enhance their capabilities, and make them strong partners in India’s journey towards prosperity.

• Third Kartavya: In line with “Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas”, ensure every family, community, region, and sector has access to required resources, facilities, and opportunities. ➢ Budget Estimates

• Non-debt receipts: ₹36.5 lakh crore.

• Total expenditure: ₹53.5 lakh crore.

• Net tax receipts of the Centre: ₹28.7 lakh crore.

• Gross market borrowings: ₹17.2 lakh crore.

• Net market borrowings via dated securities: ₹11.7 lakh crore.

• RE (2025–26) non-debt receipts: ₹34 lakh crore.

• RE (2025–26) net tax receipts of the Centre: ₹26.7 lakh crore.

• RE (2025–26) total expenditure: ₹49.6 lakh crore.

• Within this, capital expenditure is about ₹11 lakh crore.

• Fiscal deficit (BE 2026–27): 4.3% of GDP.

• Fiscal deficit (RE 2025–26): 4.4% of GDP.

• Debt-to-GDP ratio: 55.6% in 2026–27 and 56.1% in 2025–26. ➢ Manufacturing, Industry, R\&D, and Strategic Sectors

• Allocation of ₹10,000 crore for 5 years under the BioPharma Shakti scheme.

• Goal: Develop India as a global BioPharma manufacturing hub.

• Set up 3 new National Institutes of Pharmaceutical Education and Research (NIPERs) and upgrade the existing 7 institutes.

• Create a network of 1,000+ accredited clinical trials centres.

• Launch India Semiconductor Mission 2.0.

• Focus on equipment, raw materials, and development of indigenous intellectual property.

• Establish industry-led R\&D and skill development centres.

• Increase allocation for the electronics components manufacturing scheme to ₹40,000 crore.

• Set up Rare Earth corridors in Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu.

• Support states to establish 3 speciality chemical parks.

• Set up two hi-tech tool rooms through CPSEs.

• Introduce a scheme for manufacturing construction and infrastructure equipment.

• Allocate over ₹10,000 crore for 5 years for a container manufacturing scheme. ➢ Textiles, Clusters, SMEs, and Local Enterprise

• National Fibre Scheme to achieve self-reliance in natural, synthetic, and advanced fibres.

• Textile expansion and employment scheme to modernise traditional clusters.

• Establish mega textile parks to add value to technical textiles.

• Mahatma Gandhi Gram Swaraj programme to strengthen khadi, handloom, and handicrafts.

• Special scheme to rejuvenate 200 legacy industrial clusters.

• ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund to create future “champion SMEs”.

• Additional ₹2,000 crore to the Atmanirbhar Bharat Fund.
