16th Finance Commission

Constitutional Background

• The Finance Commission is a constitutional body constituted under Article 280 of the Constitution of India.

• It is appointed by the President of India once every five years.

• Its primary function is to make recommendations on the distribution of revenues between the Union and the States.

Summary of the 16th Finance Commission Report

• Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman presented the 16th Finance Commission Report in Parliament along with the Union Budget on 1 February 2026, and the Government accepted the key recommendations.

• For the period 2026–2031, the Commission recommended that the States’ share in the divisible pool of central taxes be continued at 41 percent, in line with the policy followed by the 15th Finance Commission.

• The divisible pool is the amount remaining from the gross tax revenue of the Union after deducting cesses, surcharges, and the cost of collection.

• By retaining the 41 percent share, stability in fiscal transfers to States is ensured, thereby strengthening the constitutional principles of fiscal federalism.

• With regard to horizontal devolution among States, the Commission revised the weights assigned to existing criteria and introduced a new parameter, thereby recalibrating the distribution formula. 16th Finance Commission Changes – Impact on States

• Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced the acceptance of the recommendations of the 16th Finance Commission and detailed the tax devolution framework for States for the period 2026–31.

• Under vertical devolution, the States’ share in the divisible pool of central taxes has been retained at 41 percent. The Commission clarified that given the tight fiscal position of the Union, the share could not be increased.

• The divisible pool is the amount derived from the Union’s gross tax revenue after deducting cesses, surcharges, and the cost of collection.

• In horizontal devolution among States, significant changes were made by recalibrating the weights assigned to various criteria.

• The weight assigned to population has been increased to 17.5 percent, giving greater emphasis to current population.

• The weight assigned to area has been reduced to 10 percent, lowering its relative importance.

• The weight for income distance has been reduced to 42.5 percent, thereby moderating its impact.

• The weight for forest and ecology has been retained at 10 percent.

• The revised formula partly addresses concerns of southern States, which had argued that better performance in population control during earlier Finance Commission periods was leading to a relative disadvantage.

• As a result, States such as Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Telangana are likely to witness an increase in their allocations.

• The Commission identified the gradual shrinking of the divisible pool as a major concern.

• While the divisible pool constituted 89.1 percent of the Union’s gross tax revenue in 2014–15, it declined to around 74–80 percent during the 2020–24 period.

• The primary reason for this decline is the growing reliance of the Union Government on cesses and surcharges, which are not shareable with States and accrue entirely to the Centre.
