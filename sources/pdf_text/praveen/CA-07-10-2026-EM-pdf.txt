• As part of Atmanirbhar Bharat, the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) successfully conducted a flight test of the indigenously developed High-Altitude Platform System (HAPS), marking a significant advancement in surveillance and defence capabilities.

• The lighter-than-air stratospheric airship was developed by DRDO’s Aerial Delivery Research and Development Establishment (ADRDE), Agra.

• During the test, the system reached a maximum altitude of 21 km above sea level and remained stable at an altitude of 20 km for more than 30 minutes before being safely brought down and recovered.

• It was equipped with an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), GPS receiver, altitude-control systems and cameras, which transmitted live visuals and vehicle performance data to the ground control station.

• HAPS operates at high altitudes in the stratosphere as a satellite-like aerial platform. Such systems, powered by solar energy and helium, are designed to remain airborne for weeks or even months.

• Operating far above conventional commercial aircraft, HAPS can be used for persistent military Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance (ISR), Earth observation, weather monitoring, disaster management, wide- area communications and signal relay.
