11th OCTOBER 2026 UNCTAD Trade and Development Report 2026

• According to the Trade and Development Report 2026 released by the United Nations Trade and Development (UNCTAD), India accounted for 5.7% of global greenfield investment during 2020– 2025, more than twice China’s share of 2.8%.

• Global trade growth, which stood at 4.4% in 2025, is projected to slow to 4% in 2026. Global trade reached a record USD 35 trillion in 2025.

• Higher energy prices and export restrictions contributed to the rise in trade values. China–United States bilateral trade declined by more than 20% from 2024, while East Asia expanded trade with both China and North America.

• Global economic growth is projected to slow from 2.9% in 2025 to 2.6% in 2026, while growth in developing economies is expected to decline from 4.7% to 4%.

• Asia is expected to account for 59% of global economic growth in 2026, with developing economies, including India, making a major contribution.

• Governments are increasingly using industrial, trade, financial and technology policies to pursue economic and national security objectives.

• AI-related products, including advanced computing equipment used in data centres, have become major drivers of merchandise trade. Around 82% of their value addition is concentrated in four supplier segments.

• The share of strategic sectors in global greenfield investment increased from 16% to 44% during 2020–2025.

• Of the USD 845.7 billion in greenfield investment during this period, AI infrastructure and related technologies attracted the largest share at 12.4%, followed by semiconductors at 8.1% and energy-transition technologies and services at 7.8%.

• Strategic investments were concentrated in Europe (28.4%), North America (28%) and developing Asia (26.5%).

• China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore emerged as major investment destinations. Among selected Asian economies, India accounted for 5.7% of greenfield investment, followed by Malaysia at 4%, Indonesia at 3.8% and China at 2.8%.

• India implemented 1,416 industrial policy measures during 2008–2021, ranking behind China (4,201) and the United States (4,025).

• During 2022–2025, India implemented 707 industrial policy measures; the United States, China, Australia and Brazil introduced more measures over the same period.

• India is projected to remain the fastest-growing major economy, with GDP growth of 7.3% in 2026 and 6.8% in 2027.

• Strong domestic demand, manufacturing capacity and public infrastructure investment are expected to support growth despite India’s high dependence on imported crude oil.

• Household consumption growth in India is projected at 6.9% in 2026 and 5.6% in 2027. Additional Information

• United Nations Trade and Development (UNCTAD): Established in 1964; headquarters in Geneva, Switzerland.

• UNCTAD is a permanent intergovernmental body within the United Nations system that promotes inclusive trade, investment, economic cooperation and sustainable development.

• Trade and Development Report (TDR): First published in 1981 and released annually by UNCTAD.

• Other Major UNCTAD Reports: World Investment Report, Digital Economy Report and Review of Maritime Transport.

• Greenfield Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Investment by a foreign entity to establish new factories, business facilities or operations in another country.

• Brownfield Investment: Investment used to acquire, expand or modernise existing companies or facilities.

• Industrial Policy: Government measures aimed at promoting specific industries and improving productive capacity and competitiveness.

• World Trade Organization (WTO): Established on 1 January 1995; headquarters in Geneva, Switzerland.

• WTO replaced the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT), which was established in 1947.

11th OCTOBER 2026 Living Planet Report 2026: 73% Decline in Global Wildlife Populations

• The Living Planet Report 2026 was released jointly by the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) and the Zoological Society of London (ZSL) on 8 October 2026.

• The assessment is based on around 35,803 population trends covering 5,790 vertebrate species.

• According to the report, the average size of monitored wildlife populations worldwide declined by 73% between 1970 and 2022.

• The Living Planet Index (LPI) measures changes over time in the relative abundance of vertebrate populations, including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish.

• The 73% decline refers to the average reduction in the size of monitored wildlife populations; it does not mean that 73% of species have become extinct.

• By habitat, freshwater wildlife recorded the steepest decline at 85%, followed by terrestrial populations at 69% and marine populations at 59%.

• Region-wise, the largest decline was recorded in Latin America and the Caribbean at 95%, followed by Africa at 80% and the Asia-Pacific region at 49%. Additional Information

• World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF): Established in 1961; headquarters in Gland, Switzerland.

• Zoological Society of London (ZSL): Established in 1826; headquarters in London, United Kingdom.

• Living Planet Report: WWF’s flagship report, generally published once every two years.

• Living Planet Index (LPI): Developed using wildlife population data maintained by ZSL and its partner organisations.
