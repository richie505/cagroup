India Rejects Permanent Court of Arbitration Ruling on Indus Waters Treaty
• India rejected the Permanent Court of Arbitration’s ruling concerning the Indus Waters Treaty, stating that
the tribunal has no authority to adjudicate India’s sovereign decisions. India described it as an “illegally
constituted arbitral tribunal,” maintaining that it does not recognise its legitimacy and did not participate
in the proceedings.
• Following the Pahalgam terror attack on April 22, 2025, India placed the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance
on April 23. Reiterating its position that “blood and water cannot flow together,” India stated that the treaty
would remain in abeyance until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably ends its support for cross-border
terrorism. India also argues that demographic changes since 1960, clean-energy requirements, and security
conditions must be taken into account.
• Indus Waters Treaty – Signed: September 19, 1960; Countries: India and Pakistan; Brokered by: World
Bank; Place: Karachi; India represented by: Jawaharlal Nehru; Pakistan represented by: Ayub Khan.
• Eastern Rivers: Ravi, Beas and Sutlej – allocated mainly for India’s use; Western Rivers: Indus, Jhelum
and Chenab – allocated mainly to Pakistan, with certain usage rights available to India.
• Permanent Court of Arbitration – Established: 1899; Headquarters: Peace Palace, The Hague, Netherlands;
an intergovernmental organisation facilitating the resolution of international disputes; distinct from the
International Court of Justice.
• Indus River – Origin: Tibetan Plateau; Flows through: China/Tibet, India and Pakistan; Major tributaries:
Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej; empties into the Arabian Sea.

Council (UNSC).

economic, cultural and energy sectors.
• Following the decline of the Warsaw Pact, the leaders of Poland, Czechoslovakia and Hungary met at
Visegrád, Hungary, in 1991 and formed the grouping. After Czechoslovakia split into the Czech Republic
and Slovakia in 1993, it became a four-member grouping.
• United Nations Security Council – Total members: 15; Permanent members: 5 – China, France, Russia,
United Kingdom and United States; Non-permanent members: 10; Term: 2 years; Main responsibility:
maintenance of international peace and security.

supplies.

UN General Assembly Adopts Declaration on Sea-Level Rise
• The 81st United Nations General Assembly unanimously adopted a declaration addressing threats posed
by sea-level rise to the sovereignty, legal rights and survival of low-lying coastal countries and Small
Island Developing States (SIDS).
• It aims to establish global principles to protect continuity of statehood, permanent maritime rights and
human rights even when territory is submerged due to climate change.
• The declaration emerged from a high-level process initiated under UN General Assembly Decision 78/558,
with Australia and Cabo Verde serving as co-facilitators.
• It calls for increased climate adaptation and loss-and-damage financing, along with protection of displaced
populations and their cultural identities.
• Small Island Developing States (SIDS) – Small island countries facing distinctive challenges such as sea-
level rise, coastal erosion, extreme weather events and freshwater scarcity.

Grounds of Arrest Must be Given in Writing and in a Language Understood by the Accused: Supreme
Court
• The Supreme Court clarified that every arrested person must be informed of the grounds of arrest in writing
and in a language they understand; the protection under Article 22(1) applies irrespective of the nature or
gravity of the offence.
• This safeguard applies to arrests for ordinary offences as well as those under the Prevention of Money
Laundering Act (PMLA) and the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA).
• Written grounds should ordinarily be supplied at the time of arrest. In exceptional circumstances where
this is not possible, the grounds may initially be communicated orally but must subsequently be provided
in writing within a reasonable time.
• In all circumstances, written grounds must be supplied at least two hours before the arrested person is
produced before a Magistrate for remand; failure to comply renders the arrest and subsequent remand
illegal.
• If a person released due to violation of Article 22(1) is to be re-arrested, written grounds must first be
provided and permission obtained from the concerned Judicial Magistrate.
• The re-arrest application must explain why the grounds were not supplied during the initial arrest; approval
of the immediate superior of the original arresting officer must be obtained, and further investigation must
be assigned to another officer. The Court also directed departmental disciplinary action against the officer
responsible for the violation.
• Article 22(1) – Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention; right to be informed of the grounds of
arrest; right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of one’s choice.
• Section 47 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) – Requires an arrested person to be
informed of the grounds of arrest and information concerning the right to bail.

UN General Assembly Approves Equal-Area World Maps Showing True Land Proportions
• The United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution promoting “equal-area” world maps that more
accurately represent the relative land areas of countries and continents. Introduced by Togo as part of the
“Correct the Map” initiative, the resolution was supported by 164 of the 193 UN member states, including
India, while the United States voted against it.
• India welcomed the initiative to provide a more accurate representation of global geography.
• Equal-area maps preserve the relative proportions of countries and continents more accurately, increasing
their importance as an alternative to the Mercator Projection, widely used since the 16th century.
• In the Mercator map, high-latitude regions appear larger than their actual size, while areas near the Equator
appear relatively smaller. As a result, regions such as Africa and India appear smaller, while Greenland
appears disproportionately large.
• Africa is actually about 14 times larger than Greenland, although the two may appear nearly similar in size
on a Mercator map. The proposed change concerns only the visual representation of land areas and does
not alter national borders, territories or sovereignty.
• The Mercator Projection was developed by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569; it became
useful for marine navigation because constant compass bearings appear as straight lines.
• When representing the Earth’s curved surface on a two-dimensional map, area, shape, distance and
direction cannot all be preserved perfectly at the same time. Equal-area maps preserve relative area but
may distort shapes, angles or distances.
• Equator – Latitude: 0°; divides the Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
