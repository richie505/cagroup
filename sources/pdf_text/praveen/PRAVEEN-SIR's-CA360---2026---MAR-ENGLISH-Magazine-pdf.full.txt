Global Biofuel Alliance (GBA)
• The Global Biofuel Alliance (GBA) is a multilateral coalition launched by India in partnership with
governments, international organizations, and industry stakeholders.
• Its objective is to bring together major global biofuel producers and consumers on a common platform.
• It was launched during the New Delhi G20 Summit in 2023.
• The alliance aims to position biofuels as a key component in the global energy transition.
• It promotes sustainable energy usage, employment generation, and economic growth.
• It supports national biofuel programs of member countries.
United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS)
• The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) is a comprehensive international
agreement that provides the legal framework for all activities in the oceans and seas; it is often referred to
as the “Constitution of the Oceans.”
• It defines the rights and responsibilities of coastal states over
• Territorial Sea (12 nautical miles)
• Contiguous Zone (24 nautical miles)
• Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ – 200 nautical miles)
• Continental Shelf (beyond 200 nautical miles under certain conditions)
• It incorporates principles such as innocent passage, transit passage, freedom of navigation on the High
Seas, and the “common heritage of mankind.”
• Adopted on 10 December 1982 at Montego Bay, Jamaica; it entered into force on 16 November 1994.
• It has over 170 parties, including 168 UN member states and the European Union; India is a party, while
the United States has not ratified it.
• International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) – Hamburg – settles disputes related to maritime
law.
International Seabed Authority (ISA) – Kingston – regulates deep seabed resources.
• Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf (CLCS) – New York – examines claims for continental
shelf extension.
• Its primary objectives include peaceful use of the seas, equitable and efficient resource utilization,
environmental protection, and peaceful settlement of maritime disputes.
• In March 2026, the Iranian Navy frigate IRIS Dena sank in international waters south of the Sri Lankan
coast near Galle, following International Fleet Review 2026.
• Although the location was outside Sri Lanka’s 12 nautical mile territorial sea, it fell within its Search and
Rescue (SAR) region; therefore, the Sri Lankan Navy undertook rescue operations in accordance with
UNCLOS provisions.
• The reported U.S. submarine torpedo attack and subsequent admission triggered international debate
regarding warship immunity, use of force, and freedom of navigation.
• The region holds strategic importance as it is considered part of India’s maritime sphere of influence
between India, Iran, and Sri Lanka.
Gulf Countries and Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)
• The term “Gulf countries” generally refers to the six member states of the Gulf Cooperation Council
(GCC), established in 1981.
• The GCC aims to promote regional cooperation, economic integration, and security coordination.
• Member countries: United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, and Kuwait.

Kuwait and Papua New Guinea Added to FATF Grey List
• The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) has added Kuwait and Papua New Guinea to its “Increased
Monitoring List” (Grey List); the total number of Grey List countries has risen to 22.
• The decision was taken at the 5th FATF Plenary Meeting held in Mexico.
• The meeting was chaired by Elisa de Anda Madrazo (Mexico).
• Reasons include deficiencies in controlling money laundering, weaknesses in countering terrorist
financing, and gaps in addressing proliferation financing.
• FATF was established in 1989; Headquarters: Paris, France; Members: 39.
• Grey List: Countries with strategic deficiencies in their AML/CFT systems that are under increased
monitoring and required to implement reforms.
• Black List: High-risk jurisdictions subject to potential economic sanctions.
Reforms Needed in the United Nations Security Council – António Guterres
• United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres stated that structural problems in the UN Security
Council require urgent reforms.
• He noted that the misuse of veto power by permanent member states for national interests has led to the
Council’s failure in preventing wars and conflicts.
• The absence of permanent representation for Africa and Latin America undermines the legitimacy and
effectiveness of the Council.
• He emphasized that comprehensive reforms are necessary to address 21st-century challenges; expansion
of permanent membership and limitations on veto power are under discussion.
• United Nations established: 24 October 1945; Headquarters: New York; Secretary-General: António
Guterres.
• Security Council composition: 15 members (5 permanent + 10 non-permanent); Permanent members:
United States, Russia, China, United Kingdom, France; all possess veto power.

Iran Grants Permission to Five Countries Including India to Use the Strait of Hormuz
• Iran has granted permission to India, China, Russia, Iraq, and Pakistan to use the Strait of Hormuz for
commercial navigation, according to Foreign Minister Seyyed Abbas Araghchi.
• Amid rising regional tensions, Iran clarified that hostile nations and their allies would not be permitted
passage.
• The Strait of Hormuz is a strategically significant maritime route between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf
of Oman; approximately 20% of global oil and LNG trade passes through this route.
• Near-closure of the strait led to a rise in global oil and gas prices; as West Asia is a major energy supplier
to India, India initiated diplomatic efforts to ensure uninterrupted energy supply.
• India imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements; access to the strait is crucial for energy security,
trade, and economic stability.
• Iran: Capital – Tehran; Currency – Iranian Rial; President – Masoud Pezeshkian.

• United Nations established: 1945; Headquarters: New York.
• UNOPS (established 1973 – Copenhagen), UNCTAD (1964 – Geneva), IMO (1948/1959 – London), and
ICC (1919 – Paris) play key roles in international trade and maritime security.

Religious Conversion and SC Status – Supreme Court Judgment
• The Supreme Court upheld the Andhra Pradesh High Court judgment (30 April 2025) stating that a person
who converts to Christianity and practices it sincerely cannot be treated as a member of a Scheduled Caste
(SC).
• SC status is available only to persons professing Hinduism, Sikhism, or Buddhism; conversion to any other
religion results in immediate loss of SC status.
• Legal basis: Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950; Clause 3 restricts recognition to specified
religions; no exception; reservations and related benefits do not apply after conversion.
• Case background: A pastor who had converted to Christianity filed a complaint under the SC/ST
(Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989; the High Court quashed the case; the Supreme Court Bench (Justices
Prashant Kumar Mishra and Manmohan) dismissed the Special Leave Petition.
• Supreme Court established: 1950; Headquarters: New Delhi; Article 341 – power to specify SCs; relevant
Articles: 341, 342, 15(4), 16(4).
Passive Euthanasia – Supreme Court Implementation
• The Supreme Court implemented passive euthanasia for the first time in a specific case, permitting
withdrawal of life support for Harish Rana, who had been in a vegetative state for 13 years; the decision
was based on the right to live with dignity under Article 21.
• Euthanasia refers to intentionally ending life to relieve severe suffering; active euthanasia (direct action)
remains illegal in India; passive euthanasia (withdrawal of life support) is permitted under Supreme Court
guidelines.
Judicial history
• P. Rathinam (1994) – recognized right to die under Article 21.
• Gian Kaur (1996) – held that right to die is not part of Article 21.
• Aruna Shanbaug (2011) – legalized passive euthanasia under guidelines.
• Common Cause (2018) – recognized right to die with dignity as a fundamental right.
• 2023 – simplified procedure for living wills.
• Supreme Court established: 1950; Headquarters: New Delhi; Chief Justice: Justice Surya Kant; Article 21
– protection of life and personal liberty.
Uniform Civil Code (UCC) – Supreme Court Observations
• During hearing of a petition seeking repeal of provisions under the Shariat Act, 1937, the Supreme Court
observed that the time may be appropriate to implement the Uniform Civil Code (UCC); Bench: Justices
Surya Kant, J.B. Pardiwala, and R. Mahadevan.
• Repeal of Shariat inheritance provisions could create a legal vacuum, as no alternative law currently
governs Muslim inheritance.
• While acknowledging concerns of discrimination against Muslim women, the Court noted that the final
decision rests with the legislature; UCC could ensure uniform family laws regarding marriage, divorce,
inheritance, adoption, and maintenance.
• Article 44 – part of Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP); examples: Goa (Portuguese Civil Code),
Uttarakhand (UCC Act, 2024).
• Key cases: Shah Bano (1985); Shayara Bano (2017 – Triple Talaq invalidated); Personal Law refers to
religion-based family laws; Legal Vacuum refers to absence of substitute law after repeal.

Silverpit Crater – Impact Crater from Asteroid Collision
• Scientists confirmed that the Silverpit Crater in the North Sea was formed millions of years ago due to a
massive asteroid impact.
• Geological samples revealed mineral evidence associated with high-energy extraterrestrial collisions,
confirming it was not formed by ordinary geological processes.
• The discovery enhances understanding of ancient asteroid impacts and the geological evolution of the
North Sea region.
• Located about 700 meters below the seabed, approximately 80 miles off the Yorkshire coast (UK);
remained undetected for long due to thick sediment layers.
• Impact craters form from high-velocity collisions of asteroids or meteorites; notable example: Chicxulub
Crater in Mexico (66 million years old).
• The North Sea is part of the Atlantic Ocean, bordered by the UK, Norway, Denmark, Germany, Belgium,
and the Netherlands; known for oil and natural gas reserves.

DAYS AND THEMES
Zero Discrimination Day – March 1
• Every year, Zero Discrimination Day is observed worldwide on March 1; it highlights the message that
every person has the right to live with dignity and freedom.
• The day emphasizes achieving equality and justice, especially in the context of human rights being
compromised due to poverty, caste, tribe, gender, region, or health conditions.
• It calls on governments, organizations, and individuals to take action against discrimination and promote
an inclusive, equitable society.
• Theme: “People First.”
• The day was initiated by UNAIDS; the Zero Discrimination campaign started on December 1, 2013, during
World AIDS Day, and March 1 was subsequently declared the annual observance.
• UNAIDS: Established 1996; Headquarters – Geneva, Switzerland; Executive Director – Winnie
Byanyima.
World Wildlife Day – March 3
• World Wildlife Day is observed annually on March 3; it emphasizes that wildlife and plant life are essential
for environmental balance, food chains, and ecosystem stability.
• The day raises awareness about threats to species from deforestation, habitat destruction, and poaching,
highlighting the need for biodiversity conservation.
• It also warns about environmental losses caused by species extinction.
• Theme: "Medicinal and Aromatic Plants: Conserving Health, Heritage and Livelihoods."
National Safety Day – March 4
• National Safety Day is observed in India every year on March 4 to create awareness on security, safety,
public health, and environmental protection.
• The day commemorates the establishment of the National Safety Council of India (NSC) on March 4,
1966. National Safety Week is observed from March 4 to 11 to promote a safety culture.
• Theme: "Engage, Educate & Empower People to Enhance Safety."
• Key objectives: raise awareness on workplace safety, environmental protection, health regulations,
strengthen safety culture in industries and among citizens, and reduce accidents and occupational injuries.
National Pharmacy Education Day – March 6
• National Pharmacy Education Day is observed every year on March 6 in India to commemorate Professor
Mahadev Lal Shroff, honored as the “Father of Pharmacy Education in India.”
• The day highlights the role of pharmacists in public health and raises awareness about pharmacy education;
pharmacists play a key role in drug manufacturing, formulation, testing, quality control, and safe usage.
• Pharmacy integrates medical science and chemistry as a clinical health science, and Professor Shroff laid
the foundation for pharmaceutical education in India.
• The day has been observed since 2023 by the Pharmacy Council of India (PCI); PCI: Established 1948;
Headquarters – New Delhi; Current President – Pramod Yeole.

contributes to achieving the UN Sustainable Development Goals.
• 2026 Theme: “Mathematics and Hope.”
• The term Mathematics originates from the Greek words “Manthanein” (to learn) and “Techne” (art or

mathematical development.
• π is the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter; an irrational number approximately 3.14 or 22/7;
Archimedes estimated its value, and Leonhard Euler popularized the π symbol.
• UNESCO declared this day in 2019, and it was first celebrated in 2020.
