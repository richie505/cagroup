Context: Swachha Andhra Awards–2025

Why in News

Andhra Pradesh has launched the Swachha Andhra Awards–2025, the first State-level institutionalised annual awards in India to recognise excellence in cleanliness and sanitation across multiple categories.

Introduction

• On September 1, 2025, Andhra Pradesh became the first State in India to institutionalise annual awards for excellence in cleanliness and sanitation by launching the Swachha Andhra Awards–2025.

• Announced through a government order (GO-RT-916) by the Municipal Administration and Urban Development (MAUD) Department, the initiative is modelled on the Centre’s Swachh Survekshan Awards but is distinct for being a State-level, recurring recognition system.

• The awards aim to make cleanliness a sustained people’s movement, recognising

the efforts of institutions, communities, NGOs, and individuals, while strengthening the State’s ongoing Swarna Andhra– Swachha Andhra campaign.

(Image Source: Swachha Andhra Awards facebook)

Key Features of Swachha Andhra Awards– 2025

Institutional Framework

• Organised by the MAUD Department.

• First-of-its-kind at the State level in India.

• Award Numbers and Levels

• 52 State-level awards.

• 1,421 district-level awards. Categories Covered

• Municipalities, Gram Panchayats, Schools, Hospitals, Bus Stations, Rythu Bazaars, Hostels, Industries, Anganwadis, Residential Schools.

• Recognition of NGOs and sanitation workers as “Swachhata Warriors.” Ceremonial Details

• First awards ceremony on October 2, 2025 (Mahatma Gandhi’s birth anniversary).

• Chief Minister to present State-level awards.

• Linkage with Ongoing Campaigns

• Builds upon the Swarna Andhra–Swachha Andhra campaign (SASA).

• Under SASA, every third Saturday is observed as Swachha Andhra Day, with citizen participation. Objectives and Significance

Recognition & Motivation

• Goes beyond awareness campaigns to build an ecosystem of recognition and accountability.

• Encourages innovation in waste

Rare recognition on the cards for ‘Erra Matti Dibbalu’

Source: The Hindu

APPSC Relevance: Art and Culture

Context: Erra Matti Dibbalu & UNESCO Tentative Listing

Why in News?

Erra Matti Dibbalu, the red sand dunes in Visakhapatnam formed during the Last Glacial Maximum, have been added to UNESCO’s tentative list for their unique geo-archaeological significance.

Introduction

• The iconic red sand dunes known as ‘Erra Matti Dibbalu’, located beside the Visakhapatnam-Bheemunipatnam Beach Road in Andhra Pradesh, have been included in the Tentative List of UNESCO’s World Heritage Sites.

• This recognition marks an important step towards the global conservation and acknowledgment of the region’s unique geological, archaeological, and cultural heritage. About Erra Matti Dibbalu:

• Meaning & Location: ‘Erra Matti Dibbalu’ literally translates to "Red Sand Hills" in Telugu. These striking red sand dunes are situated along the 30-km coastal stretch between Visakhapatnam and Bheemunipatnam in Andhra Pradesh.

• Physical Features: The dunes rise between 10 to 30 feet above the ground and are prominent along the coastal landscape, drawing attention from motorists and visitors alike. Geological Importance:

• The Erra Matti Dibbalu are classified as one of India’s 34 National Geo-heritage Sites.

• They were primarily formed during the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM), approximately 18,500 years ago.

• During the LGM, the coastline of the Bay of Bengal was nearly 50 km inland, a

consequence of lower sea levels due to vast ice accumulation.

• Geological Process:

• Over time, winds and tides transported sand from the exposed continental shelf during low sea levels and deposited it at the current site of Erra Matti Dibbalu.

• The ferrous minerals present in the sand react with moisture, giving the dunes their characteristic rusty red color.

• The formation process possibly began about 20,000 years ago and continued through the interglacial periods. Archaeological Significance:

• The site provides crucial archaeological evidence of prehistoric human activity.

• In the mid-1990s, a research study led by Alok Rath and P. Thimma Reddy uncovered stone tools from microlithic, mesolithic, and paleolithic eras as well as pottery fragments.

Why in News?

Andhra Pradesh has launched a mission to eliminate over 1.15 crore metric tonnes of legacy waste by 2025 through composting, Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF), Waste-to-Energy plants, and promoting the Circular Economy model.

Introduction

• The Andhra Pradesh has launched an ambitious mission to eliminate over 1.15 crore metric tonnes of legacy waste by the end of 2025.

• This initiative aligns with the principles of Swachh Andhra, Swachh Bharat, and a Circular Economy, aiming to promote sustainability, resource efficiency, and optimal land utilization. Legacy Waste:

• Legacy Waste refers to the accumulated waste that has been dumped and left unmanaged over several years at landfill sites or illegal dumping areas.

• It typically includes municipal solid waste, construction & demolition debris, industrial waste, hazardous materials, and other unsegregated refuse.

Key Highlights of the Mission

1\. Legacy Waste Clearance Target

• The State plans to clear 85.90 lakh metric tonnes of old waste dumps by October 2, 2025, followed by an additional 30 lakh metric tonnes by December 2025.

• The reclaimed land will be repurposed productively, turning waste into resources. 2. Resource Utilization Strategy

• Recovered soil will be processed into compost, promoting organic farming and improving soil health.

• Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF) will be supplied to cement factories, reducing reliance on conventional fossil fuels and providing a sustainable waste-to-energy solution. Waste-to-Energy (WTE) Initiatives

• Functional WTE plants exist in Visakhapatnam and Guntur, converting municipal waste into power daily.

• Further projects are underway in Nellore, Rajahmundry, Kurnool, Kadapa, Vijayawada, and Tirupati, with the target of operationalizing six WTE plants by 2027. Integrated Solid Waste Management (ISWM) Approach

The State is developing 72 ISWM clusters supported by:

• 417 Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs) in rural areas.

• 108 urban projects for waste segregation and recycling.

• Efforts are ongoing to revive Construction & Demolition (C\&D) waste management plants in Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, and Tirupati.

• Poultry waste management systems are also in the pipeline, focusing on bio-extraction and energy generation. Sewage Treatment and Reuse

• Andhra Pradesh is setting up 142 Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) on 199 acres.

• The treated sewage water will be reused safely, promoting water sustainability and reducing environmental contamination. Policy and Governance Framework

The mission is steered by key departments such as:

• Swachh Andhra Corporation

• Panchayati Raj & Rural Development

• Municipal & Urban Development

• Pollution Control Board

• Forest Department The initiative highlights the State’s commitment to:

• Promoting cleanliness and public health.

• Enhancing environmental sustainability.

• Driving job creation in the green economy sector. Single-Use Plastic (SUP) Free Status

• The Andhra Pradesh Secretariat was declared Single-Use Plastic Free on August 15, 2025.

• All collectorates and government offices are mandated to achieve SUP-Free status by December 2025, reducing plastic pollution and promoting eco-friendly alternatives.
