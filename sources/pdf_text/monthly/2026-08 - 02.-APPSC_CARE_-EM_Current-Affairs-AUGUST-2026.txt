Why in News?

A rare pangolin, locally known as “alugu,” was spotted in the Kamalapuram municipal area of Kadapa district, Andhra Pradesh. The animal was safely rescued by forest officials and will be released into a suitable forest area in the Mydukur range after necessary checks.

About Pangolins

• Pangolins are rare, shy and mostly nocturnal mammals.

• They are locally known as “alugu” in parts of Andhra Pradesh.

• Their body is covered with hard protective scales.

• When threatened, pangolins usually curl into a ball and raise their scales.

• They have a long sticky tongue used to catch ants and termites.

• Pangolins are generally harmless and do not attack humans.

• They are mammals because they give birth to young ones and nurse them with milk.

• They play an important role in maintaining ecological balance.

Species of Pangolins

Police Custody under BNSS: Supreme Court Clarifies Remand Window and Rights of the Accused

Source: The Hindu

Relevance: Indian Polity and Governance

Important Keywords Prelims Keywords:

• Section 187 BNSS | Section 38 BNSS | Police Custody | Default Bail | Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam Mains Keywords:

Why in News?

The Supreme Court, in State of Andhra Pradesh vs Suda Suresh Veera Venkata Naga Raju, clarified the scope of police custody under Section 187(2) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita.

Case Before the Supreme Court

• The case arose from a custodial death investigation in Andhra Pradesh. The investigating agency sought police custody because the victim’s body was still untraced, original CCTV hard disks were yet to be recovered, and possible discoveries under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam were expected.

• The magistrate had granted eight days of police custody and allowed the accused to nominate two advocates, with one advocate permitted to be present during

investigation. However, the order also stated that the custody period would not be extended under any circumstances.

• The Andhra Pradesh High Court later modified some conditions related to custody and advocate presence. The State challenged these restrictions before the Supreme Court, arguing that they affected the ability of the investigating officer to conduct an effective investigation.

Supreme Court’s Key Rulings

1\. Police Custody Window is Wider under BNSS

• The Court held that Section 187(2) BNSS expands the time window during which police custody may be sought. Police remand is not restricted only to the first 15 days after arrest.

2\. Total Police Custody Still Capped at 15 Days • The Court clarified that even though the window has expanded, the total police custody cannot exceed 15 days in the whole.

3\. Custody Can Be Granted in Parts

• Police custody may be granted in different spells if investigation later reveals fresh facts, leads, recoveries or discoveries.

4\. No Absolute Non-Extendable Limit by Magistrate

• A magistrate or court cannot impose a blanket condition that police custody will

5\. Additional Custody Allowed in the Case

• The Supreme Court permitted seven additional days of police custody, ensuring that the total police custody, including earlier custody, remained within the statutory limit of 15 days.

Current Law on Police Remand under BNSS

Section 58 BNSS

• A person arrested without warrant cannot be detained by police for more than 24 hours, unless further detention is authorised by a magistrate under Section 187.

Section 187(2) BNSS

• If investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours and there are grounds to believe that the accusation is well-founded, the magistrate may authorise detention. Police custody can be granted for a total period of 15 days, either at once or in parts, during the initial 40 or 60 days of the permissible detention period.

Section 187(3) BNSS

Judicial detention beyond police custody may continue up to:

• 90 days for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment or imprisonment of 10 years or more;

• 60 days for other offences.

If investigation is not completed within the prescribed period, the accused may claim default bail.

Conclusion

The Supreme Court’s interpretation of Section 187(2) BNSS gives investigating agencies a wider window to seek police custody while keeping the total limit at 15 days. This reflects the needs of complex investigations where fresh evidence may emerge later. At the same time, the judgment makes clear that the accused’s right to meet an advocate does not extend to continuous lawyer presence during interrogation. The real test lies in implementation. Strong judicial supervision, custodial safeguards, video documentation and police accountability are essential to ensure that the expanded remand window strengthens investigation without weakening constitutional due process.

About UNESCO World Heritage Sites

• UNESCO World Heritage Sites are landmarks or areas recognised by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation for their cultural, historical or natural significance.

• They are considered to have Outstanding Universal Value, meaning their importance goes beyond national boundaries and belongs to the shared heritage of humanity.

• As of July 2026, India has 45 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including 37 cultural sites, 7 natural sites and 1 mixed site.

• Examples from India include the Taj Mahal, Ajanta Caves, Western Ghats and Sundarbans National Park.

World Heritage Convention and Selection Criteria

• The idea of World Heritage developed after World War II, when there was growing concern over the destruction of cultural and natural heritage.

• This led to the adoption of the Convention Concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage, 1972, commonly called the World Heritage Convention. It came into force in 1975, and India signed it on 14 November 1977.

• To qualify for World Heritage recognition, a site must show Outstanding Universal Value and meet at least one of the ten UNESCO criteria.

Cultural Criteria Include

▪ masterpiece of human creative genius

▪ testimony to a civilisation or cultural tradition

▪ important example of architecture, technology or landscape

▪ association with events, beliefs or ideas of universal significance

Natural Criteria Include

▪ outstanding natural beauty

▪ evidence of Earth’s evolutionary history

▪ ecological and biological processes

▪ important habitats for biodiversity conservation

Nominated cultural sites are assessed by ICOMOS, while natural sites are assessed by IUCN.

Why in News?

India’s first non-GMO, high-expansion popcorn maize hybrid seeds were launched at Musunuru in Eluru district, Andhra Pradesh.The indigenous hybrids seek to reduce India’s dependence on imported technology for high-quality popcorn maize and develop seeds suited to Indian soil and climatic conditions.

About the New Popcorn Hybrids

The two popcorn maize hybrids have been developed for tropical agricultural conditions.

They are designed to provide:

• better adaptation to Indian soils;

• suitability to local climatic conditions;

• improved disease resistance;

• higher yield performance; and

• greater kernel expansion during popping.

What Does Non-GMO Hybrid Mean?

Non-GMO

• A non-genetically modified organism is developed without directly altering its genetic material through genetic- engineering techniques.

Hybrid Seed

• A hybrid is produced by crossing selected parent varieties to combine desirable agricultural characteristics.

• Therefore, hybrid and genetically modified are not identical terms. A crop can be a hybrid while remaining non-GMO.

Seventh-Century Inscription Reveals the Antiquity of Guntur

Source: The Hindu

Relevance: GS Paper I — Indian History, Art and Culture; Andhra Pradesh History

Important Keywords:

• Epigraphy, Copper-plate inscriptions, Guntur, Eastern Chalukyas, Vishnuvardhana II, Toponymy

Why in News?

The Archaeological Survey of India’s Director of Epigraphy, Dr. K. Munirathnam Reddy, has highlighted archaeological evidence establishing the antiquity of Guntur in Andhra Pradesh. The earliest known epigraphical reference to Guntur occurs in seventh-century copper plates associated with the Eastern Chalukya dynasty.

What Does the Inscription Reveal?

Earliest Reference to Guntur

• The first known inscriptional reference to Guntur appears in the Pune copper plates issued by Eastern Chalukya king Vishnuvardhana II.

• The plates date to the ruler’s first regnal year—669 CE. They provide material evidence that the settlement was known and recorded nearly fourteen centuries ago.

Nature of the Evidence

Copper-plate inscriptions were generally used to record royal orders, grants and administrative transactions. Since they usually mention rulers, regnal years, places and beneficiaries, they serve as important primary sources for reconstructing history.

Evolution of Guntur’s Name

• The inscriptions document changes in the city’s name across centuries. Its nomenclature evolved through forms such as:

▪ Kunturu

▪ Gomturu

▪ Gunturu

• A 10th-century Ederu copper-plate inscription records the name as Gomturu. Regional inscriptions indicate that this form remained in continuous use from the 10th to the 14th century.

• These variations reveal a gradual linguistic transition from ancient forms to the modern name, Guntur.

Historical Signi icance

1\. Establishes Guntur’s Antiquity

• The 669 CE reference pushes Guntur’s documented history back to the seventh century and confirms its existence during the Eastern Chalukya period.

2\. Traces Linguistic Change

• The changing spellings demonstrate how pronunciation, language and writing conventions evolved over time.

3\. Reconstructs Regional History

• The inscriptions provide evidence about the historical geography and socio-political development of medieval Andhra Pradesh.

4\. Highlights Eastern Chalukya Influence

• The records connect Guntur with the

Eastern Chalukya dynasty and help scholars understand the dynasty’s administrative and territorial presence.

5\. Demonstrates the Value of Epigraphy

• Epigraphy—the study of inscriptions— helps establish dates, place names, political authority and linguistic developments where literary evidence may be limited.

Conclusion

The Pune copper plates provide the earliest known epigraphical reference to Guntur, dating to 669 CE. Together with later records such as the Ederu inscription, they trace the transformation of the city’s name and offer a tangible window into the political and linguistic history of medieval Andhra Pradesh.

Why in News?

Phase I of the Veligonda Project is scheduled to be inaugurated at Markapuram on August 31, 2026. The ₹10,200-crore project seeks to supply Krishna water to the drought-affected areas of Prakasam, Nellore and Kadapa districts for irrigation and drinking purposes.

Background

The foundation stone for the Veligonda Project was laid on March 5, 1996. Nearly three decades later, its first phase is set to become operational.

The project was conceived to address the major problems faced by western Prakasam and neighbouring regions, including:

• recurring crop failures;

• uncertain rainfall;

• fluoride-contaminated groundwater;

• drinking-water shortages;

• dependence on borewells; and

• migration caused by limited livelihood opportunities.

Odia Inscription of the Gajapati Era Found at Tekkali

Source: Deccan Chronicle Relevance: APPSC: History and cultural heritage of North Coastal Andhra Pradesh

Important Keywords Prelims:

• Tekkali Inscription | Gajapati Dynasty | Kapilendra Deva | Pratapa Kapileswara | Odia Script | Odia Language | Estampage | Palaeography | Kalinga Heritage Mains:

• Epigraphical Sources | Odisha–Andhra Cultural Linkages | Regional History | Political Geography | Linguistic Heritage | Historical Continuity | Heritage Documentation | Inscription Conservation

Why in News?

A centuries-old inscription associated with the Gajapati period has been traced inside the Tekkali royal palace in Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh. Engraved in the Odia script and language, it contains the name “Pratapa Kapileswara,” referring to Gajapati ruler Kapilendra Deva.The

discovery provides further evidence of Tekkali’s historical connections with the Odisha kingdom.

Location and Discovery

The stone inscription was found at:

• Place: Tekkali royal palace

• District: Srikakulam

• State: Andhra Pradesh

The inscription was traced by researchers associated with an organisation that studies and documents Kalinga heritage in South India. A member of the Tekkali royal family assisted them in locating the stone.

Earlier Epigraphical Record

• The inscription was not entirely unknown to historical records. It had earlier been mentioned in the Annual Report on Indian Epigraphy for 1926–27.

• Using this reference, the researchers approached the Tekkali royal family and successfully located the stone within the palace premises.

• The discovery therefore represents the physical retracing and renewed documentation of an inscription that had been recorded approximately a century earlier.

Condition of the Inscription

The inscribed stone had been lying beneath a Tulasi Choura inside the palace.

Its condition is poor because:

• a considerable portion has been damaged;

• some parts have been lost over time;

• the irst line is completely destroyed; and

• the surviving lines do not reveal the inscription’s complete purpose. Despite the damage, researchers obtained an estampage for further examination.

What Is an Estampage?

An estampage is an impression of an inscription taken on paper. It allows researchers to examine and document engraved letters without repeatedly handling or moving the original stone.

It is especially useful when an inscription is:

• weathered;

• partly damaged;

• dif icult to move; or

• located in a vulnerable place.

About Gajapati Kapilendra Deva

The inscription refers to Gajapati ruler Kapilendra Deva through the name “Pratapa Kapileswara”.

According to the researchers:

• he ruled Odisha during the 15th century; and

• during his reign, the Odisha kingdom extended from the Ganga River in the north to the Kaveri River in the south.

The inscription’s presence at Tekkali reflects the historical reach of the Gajapati kingdom into parts of present-day North Coastal Andhra Pradesh.

Historical Importance of Tekkali

• Tekkali was formerly an ancient Odia zamindari of Ganjam.

• It later became separated from Odisha during the formation of the separate State of Odisha. Nevertheless, historical and cultural connections have continued.

• A signi icant number of Odia families still reside in Tekkali, indicating the persistence of linguistic and cultural ties.
