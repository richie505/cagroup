Why in News?

Hyderabad-based Skyroot Aerospace has successfully stacked the first stage of its Vikram-I rocket at the First Launch Pad of the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota. This is the first time a privately designed, developed and manufactured orbital-class rocket has been stacked at ISRO’s First Launch Pad.

What is Vikram-I?

• Vikram-I is Skyroot Aerospace’s maiden orbital launch vehicle.

• It is designed to carry small satellites into Low-Earth Orbit and is currently undergoing final integration and technical testing.

• The vehicle is part of India’s growing commercial launch ecosystem, which seeks to meet the rising demand for affordable and flexible small-satellite launches.

Why is the Stacking Milestone Important?

First private orbital rocket at the pad:

• The event marks the first stacking of a privately developed orbital-class launch vehicle at ISRO’s First Launch Pad.

Transition from development to launch preparation:

• Stacking indicates that individual stages are being assembled into a flight-ready vehicle.

Use of national launch infrastructure:

• The milestone demonstrates how private companies can access ISRO facilities for commercial space missions.

Early Life and Background

• Alluri Sitarama Raju was born in Pandrangi village in Visakhapatnam district. His birth is commonly associated with 4 July 1897, though some accounts place it in 1898.

• He belonged to a Telugu-speaking Kshatriya family. Over time, he came into close contact with tribal communities living in the Eastern Ghats region.

• His understanding of their hardships shaped his political consciousness and later guided his resistance against British authority.

Rampa Rebellion / Manyam Rebellion

• The Rampa Rebellion, also known as the Manyam Rebellion, took place between 1922 and 1924.

• Alluri Sitarama Raju led the rebellion in the Eastern Ghats region, mobilising tribal people against British forest policies and administrative oppression.

• The rebellion was not merely a violent outburst. It was a response to the disruption of tribal life caused by colonial control over forests, land and labour. The movement aimed to:

▪ Resist oppressive British forest laws

APPSC News Summary

▪ Defend tribal livelihood practices

▪ Protect access to forest produce

▪ Challenge forced labour and exploitation

▪ Drive British authority out of the hill region

Why Tribal People Resisted British Rule

• The tribal communities of the Eastern Ghats traditionally practised podu, or shifting cultivation.

• Under this system, patches of forest land were cleared for cultivation and later left fallow as communities moved to another area. It was closely linked with their food security, livelihood and cultural identity.

• The Madras Forest Act, 1882 imposed restrictions on tribal movement and limited their access to forests. It also affected their ability to collect minor forest produce.

• As a result, many tribal people were pushed into low-wage labour for the forest department or contractors.

• The colonial state treated forests as revenue- generating resources, while tribal communities viewed them as a source of livelihood, identity and survival. This conflict became the foundation of the rebellion.

Guerrilla Campaign against the British

• Alluri Sitarama Raju organised tribal fighters into a guerrilla force.

• Instead of fighting the British through conventional battlefield methods, he used guerrilla warfare, which involved:

▪ Ambushes

▪ Sudden raids

▪ Hit-and-run tactics

▪ Sabotage

▪ Mobility through forest

• His knowledge of the hilly and forested landscape gave the rebellion a tactical advantage.

• The guerrilla campaign challenged British authority and exposed the limits of colonial power in forest regions where local support was strong.

Death and Legacy

• Alluri Sitarama Raju was captured and killed by British forces at Koyyuru village on 7 May 1924.

• His death marked the end of the Rampa Rebellion, but his legacy continued to inspire generations. He is remembered as a symbol of:

▪ Courage

• Generate Employment: SEZs create direct and indirect employment opportunities.

• Develop Infrastructure: They support the creation of modern industrial, transport and business infrastructure.

• Increase Economic Activity: SEZs promote industrial growth, innovation and the development of specialised business clusters. Key Features

• SEZs operate under a separate regulatory and fiscal framework.

• Units receive certain tax, customs and procedural benefits.

• They provide simplified regulatory procedures and better infrastructure.

• They support manufacturing, services, information technology, warehousing and export-related activities. Importance of SEZs in India

• SEZs have strengthened India’s export-led growth.

• They encourage technological advancement and industrial expansion.

• They promote the development of local economies and supporting business ecosystems.

• They improve socio-economic conditions through employment and infrastructure development.

The Andhra Pradesh government is planning to develop Kosala Nagaram Industrial Park in Chittoor district as a major manufacturing hub. The project is aimed at attracting industrial investment, especially from automobile, textile and food processing sectors.

Core Features of the Project

• The project is being taken forward by the AP Industrial Infrastructure Corporation. APIIC already owns 2,042.13 acres at Kosala Nagaram, and the State government has directed it to acquire an additional 5,000

acres for future expansion.

• The government has also decided to earmark 500 acres for a private industrial park, while about 200 acres have already been allotted to different industries.

• This indicates that Kosala Nagaram is being planned as a large industrial cluster rather than a small industrial estate.

Why Kosala Nagaram is Strategically Important

Kosala Nagaram is located close to the Tamil Nadu border, which gives it an advantage in attracting industries from established manufacturing centres. Its major location advantages include:

• proximity to the Chittoor-Tachur National Highway

• access to Krishnapatnam and Kattupalli ports

• connectivity to Chennai and Tirupati airports

• availability of skilled manpower in the region

Proposed Industrial Pro ile

The industrial park is expected to attract multiple sectors.

1\. Automobile Sector

• Automobile manufacturers from Ambattur in Tamil Nadu have shown interest in setting up units in the area.

2\. Textile Sector

• Textile industries may use the park for labour-intensive manufacturing.

3\. Food Processing Sector

• Food processing units can benefit from Andhra Pradesh’s agricultural and horticultural base.

• Together, these sectors can create a diversified industrial ecosystem.

Infrastructure and Water Planning

• The site requires extensive land levelling before industries can begin operations. Since this involves significant cost, the government is considering private participation for developing the park and creating basic infrastructure.

• The biggest infrastructure concern is water availability. The State government has given in-principle approval for a balancing reservoir to meet future water needs. Water is proposed to be supplied from the Galeru Nagari Sujala Sravanthi project, expected to be completed by December 2028.

Development Model
