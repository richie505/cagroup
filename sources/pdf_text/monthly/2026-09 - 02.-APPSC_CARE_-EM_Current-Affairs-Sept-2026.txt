Why in News?

A seven-year-old girl died and 52 people, including 47 children, fell ill after receiving chloroquine tablets at a medical camp in Gollaguppa village of Yetapaka mandal in Polavaram district.Twenty-three children were shifted from Lakshmipuram Primary Health Centre to Bhadrachalam Area Hospital. The exact cause requires investigation, including clinical examination and chemical analysis of the administered medicine.

What Is Chloroquine?

Chloroquine is a medicine used in malaria management. Depending on medical guidelines and the parasite’s drug sensitivity, it may be administered:

• to treat malaria; or

• as a preventive or prophylactic medicine.

It is a potent drug that must be administered in the correct dose after assessing the patient’s age, weight, health and nutritional condition.

What Is Malaria?

• Malaria is a mosquito-borne disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

Why Can Chloroquine Become Harmful?

Chloroquine has a narrow therapeutic index.

A therapeutic index indicates the difference between:

• the dose that produces the intended medical benefit; and

• the dose that produces toxic effects.

A narrow therapeutic index means that the margin between an effective dose and a harmful dose is small. Therefore, even a dosing error may cause severe reactions.

• ISRO is developing the Semi-Cryogenic Propulsion Stage (SC120), powered by the 2,000 kN-class SE2000 engine.

• Payload capability refers to the amount of useful load, such as satellites, that a launch vehicle can carry to a specified orbit.

Why in News?

Andhra Pradesh has taken up 1,162.80 MW of feeder-level solarisation capacity through 228 solar power plants under Component C of the PM- KUSUM Scheme.

What is PM-KUSUM?

The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan, or PM-KUSUM, promotes the use of solar energy in agriculture.

It is implemented by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy and seeks to:

• provide reliable power for irrigation;

• reduce farmers’ dependence on diesel pumps;

• solarise grid-connected agricultural pumps;

• develop decentralised renewable-energy plants;

• reduce the agricultural power-subsidy burden on States;

• improve the financial position of DISCOMs; and reduce greenhouse-gas emissions.

The scheme originally aimed to add about 34,800 MW of solar capacity, supported by Central financial assistance of ₹34,422 crore.

Oceansat-3 Tracks El Niño’s Impact on Marine Productivity

Mains:

Why in News?

• India’s EOS-06/Oceansat-3 satellite has detected changes in ocean winds and surface chlorophyll associated with the developing El Niño in the tropical Pacific.

• ISRO’s National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC) analysed chlorophyll and wind observations for April, May and June during 2023–2026, helping connect physical ocean changes with biological responses.

What Did the Satellite Observe?

• Lower Chlorophyll-a: Surface concentrations declined markedly in June

2026, compared with neutral ENSO conditions.

• Changing Winds: Prevailing easterly winds weakened, with westerly reversals observed in the analysed region.

• Contracting Productive Zone: The area of elevated chlorophyll progressively contracted westward between April and June 2026, indicating reduced nutrient supply through equatorial upwelling.

How Does El Niño Affect Marine Productivity?

Understanding El Niño

• El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño– Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a recurring interaction between the tropical Pacific Ocean and the atmosphere. It involves unusually warm surface waters in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific, alongside changes in atmospheric circulation.

Normal Conditions: Winds Support Upwelling

• Under normal conditions, easterly trade winds help move warm surface water westward. Wind-driven movement also causes surface waters to diverge near the equator, allowing deeper water to rise.

CURRENT AFFAIRS

SEPTEMBER - 2026

APPSC News Summary

• This process, called upwelling, brings relatively cold, nutrient-rich water towards the surface. These nutrients support phytoplankton, microscopic photosynthetic organisms that form the base of many marine food webs.

El Niño Conditions: Nutrient Supply Weakens

• During El Niño, weakened trade winds and changes in the ocean’s upper layers can reduce the supply of nutrient-rich water to the surface.

The resulting relationship is:

Weaker winds and altered ocean conditions → Reduced nutrient supply → Lower phytoplankton abundance → Potential decline in marine productivity.

Why is Chlorophyll-a Important?

• Chlorophyll-a is a pigment used in photosynthesis. Its concentration helps scientists estimate phytoplankton abundance.

• However, chlorophyll is an indicator, not a direct measurement of fish stocks or the total health of a marine ecosystem. Its interpretation requires supporting information on ocean conditions.

About EOS-06/Oceansat-3

EOS-06, also called Oceansat-3, is India’s third- generation Oceansat satellite. It was launched on November 26, 2022, aboard PSLV-C54 from Sriharikota, into a polar sun-synchronous orbit.

Its instruments support complementary observations:

• Ocean Colour Monitor-3 (OCM-3): Measures ocean colour, enabling estimates of chlorophyll and other ocean parameters.

• Ku-Band Scatterometer (SCAT-3): Provides information on ocean-surface wind speed and direction.

• Sea Surface Temperature Monitor (SSTM): Measures ocean-surface temperature.

• ARGOS Payload: Supports satellite-based data collection.

For the reported El Niño study, scientists combined OCM-3 chlorophyll estimates with scatterometer wind observations.

Conclusion

Oceansat-3 demonstrates how satellite observations can connect changing winds with biological responses in the ocean. Combining ocean colour, wind and temperature data strengthens understanding of El Niño, marine productivity and climate variability.
