Bituminous Concrete is a road-surfacing material prepared by mixing:
- bitumen;
- crushed stone aggregates; and
- other binding materials.
It is generally used as the upper surface layer of roads because it provides a smooth and durable surface for vehicular movement.

## Why in News?
- As of September 2026 , India had deployed 40 supercomputers with a combined computing capacity of 68 petaFLOPS under the National Supercomputing Mission .
- India is also preparing to host ESTIC-2026 , which will cover supercomputing and other emerging technologies while promoting the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047 .

## What is a Supercomputer?
A supercomputer is a high-performance computing system designed to process large datasets and perform complex calculations at exceptionally high speeds.
- Multiple processors: It combines several processors or computing nodes that work simultaneously.
- Parallel processing: Multiple processors jointly work on a single complex calculation, reducing the time required to solve it.
- High-speed connections: Computing nodes exchange data through high-speed interconnection networks.
- Major uses: Supercomputers are used in weather forecasting, climate modelling, healthcare, space research, engineering, artificial intelligence and scientific simulations.

## How is Computing Power Measured?
The performance of a supercomputer is measured in FLOPS—Floating Point Operations Per Second .
- One teraFLOPS: One trillion calculations per second.
- One petaFLOPS: One quadrillion calculations per second.
- One exaFLOPS: One quintillion calculations per second.
- The world’s fastest supercomputer in 2026 operates at approximately 2.19 exaFLOPS .

## Why Does India Need Supercomputing Capabilities?
- Growing data economy: India generates nearly 20% of the world’s data , creating demand for advanced computing infrastructure.
- Complex scientific research: Climate, space, medicine and engineering require large-scale simulations that ordinary computers cannot perform efficiently.
- Artificial intelligence: AI, machine learning and generative AI require high computing capacity to train and operate advanced models.
- Disaster management: Supercomputers can support flood prediction, forest-fire modelling and extreme-weather forecasting.
- Healthcare applications: They accelerate genomics research, disease tracking and drug discovery.
- Agricultural planning: High-performance computing can support crop-yield estimation and climate-based agricultural decisions.
- Technological sovereignty: Indigenous computing infrastructure reduces dependence on imported hardware and software.
- Evidence-based governance: Fast data processing enables government agencies to make timely and informed decisions.

## What is the National Supercomputing Mission?
The National Supercomputing Mission (NSM) was launched in April 2015 to establish a nationally distributed and technologically self-reliant supercomputing ecosystem.
Key Features
- Financial outlay: Approximately ₹4,500 crore .
- Jointly steered by: Department of Science and Technology Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology
- Implementing institutions: Centre for Development of Advanced Computing, Pune Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru
- Target: Establish 50 supercomputers with a combined capacity exceeding 123 petaFLOPS across Indian academic and research institutions.
- Objectives: Develop indigenous supercomputers, expand access to HPC, promote scientific research and create solutions for national and societal needs.

## NSM’s Build Approach
The Mission follows a three-stage indigenous-development strategy:
- Assembly: Assemble supercomputing systems within India.
- Manufacturing: Increase domestic production of important hardware components.
- Design and manufacturing: Develop complete indigenous capabilities in system design, hardware, software, networking and cooling.

## How has India’s Supercomputing Programme Evolved?

### PARAM 8000
- PARAM 8000 , developed by C-DAC , was unveiled in 1991 .
- It operated at a speed of one gigaflop .
- It marked India’s entry into indigenous supercomputing and laid the foundation for the PARAM series.

### PARAM Yuva
- PARAM Yuva achieved a computing capacity of 54 teraFLOPS .
- It supported applications such as weather forecasting and computational fluid dynamics.

### Present Capacity under NSM
As of September 2026 , India had commissioned:
- 40 supercomputers with a combined capacity of 68 petaFLOPS ;
- 13 high-end systems with capacities above 1 petaFLOPS;
- 12 mid-range systems with capacities between 500 teraFLOPS and 1 petaFLOPS; and
- 15 systems with capacities below 500 teraFLOPS.
These systems were locally built and installed across academic and research institutions.

## What are the National Applications of Supercomputing?

### Genomics and Drug Discovery
- The platform studies large molecular datasets to identify and test potential medicines.
- During the COVID-19 pandemic , it was used to screen existing drugs and predict possible side effects, including heart-related risks.

### Urban Environment Decision Support System
- It combines detailed weather and air-pollution models.
- It helps cities forecast heavy rainfall and pollution episodes and take preventive measures.

### Flood Early-Warning System
- It uses data analysis and predictive models to forecast floods up to two days in advance .
- It is being applied in the Mahanadi River basin to protect communities and infrastructure.

### Forest-Fire Spread Model
- It combines satellite remote-sensing data with computational models to predict the spread of forest fires.
- It has been tested in regions such as the Sikkim Himalayas .

### Seismic Imaging Suite
- It uses advanced algorithms to map underground structures.
- It supports oil and gas exploration and performs at a level comparable with commercial seismic-imaging tools.

### Materials Science and Computational Chemistry
- It enables the simulation of atoms, molecules and alloys.
- Researchers can study their properties and behaviour without depending entirely on physical experiments.

### Support to Government Institutions
HPC applications developed under NSM support institutions such as:
- India Meteorological Department
- Central Water Commission
- Central Pollution Control Board
- Ministry of AYUSH

## What Indigenous Technologies have been Developed under NSM?

### Rudra Series of Servers
- Rudra servers were designed and developed indigenously by C-DAC .
- They provide processing power for complex simulations, calculations and AI workloads.
- Their technology has been transferred to Indian Electronics Manufacturing Services partners for domestic production.

### PARAM Rudra Supercomputers
- PARAM Rudra systems use indigenous Rudra servers and an Indian system-software stack.
- They support astronomy, material science, atomic physics and earth-science research.
- By September 2026, approximately 6,000 Rudra servers had been deployed, while another 1,500 servers were under production.

### High-Speed Interconnect Network
- Indigenous networks operating at 100 Gbps and 200 Gbps have been developed and tested.
- They enable faster communication and data transfer between computing nodes.

### Indigenous Cooling Technology
- India has developed and demonstrated indigenous cooling systems for high-performance computers.
- These systems maintain operating temperatures and improve energy efficiency.

### HPC System Software
- A complete indigenous HPC software stack has been developed.
- It supports the operation, integration and management of supercomputing systems.

### PARAM Shavak
- PARAM Shavak is a domestically designed and manufactured “supercomputing-in-a-box” system.
- It serves the HPC and AI requirements of students and researchers in engineering colleges and universities.

## How is NSM Building a Skilled Workforce?
As of September 2026, infrastructure created under NSM had:
- supported more than 16,000 researchers ;
- included over 2,900 PhD scholars ;

## Why in News?
- The Union Cabinet approved Green Energy Corridor Phase-III on 30 September 2026 to enable the evacuation of up to 135 GW of renewable energy across States and Union Territories.
- The scheme has an outlay of ₹1,86,405 crore and includes a dedicated 50 GWh Battery Energy Storage System component.

## What is the Green Energy Corridor?
The Green Energy Corridor is a transmission-infrastructure programme implemented by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy .
It aims to:
- transmit electricity from renewable-energy projects to demand centres;
- strengthen intra-State and inter-State transmission networks;
- reduce congestion and curtailment of renewable power;
- improve grid stability; and
- support the integration of large-scale solar and wind capacity.
The programme is being implemented through:
- Intra-State Transmission System: Carries electricity within a State.
- Inter-State Transmission System: Transfers electricity across State boundaries.

## What are the Key Features of GEC Phase-III?
Renewable-Energy Evacuation
- The scheme will strengthen the Intra-State Transmission System .
- It will facilitate the evacuation of up to 135 GW of renewable energy.
- Completion is targeted by FY 2032–33 .
Financial Outlay
The total scheme outlay is ₹1,86,405 crore :
- ₹1,36,378 crore: Development of intra-State transmission systems.
- ₹50,000 crore: Installation of 50 GWh of Battery Energy Storage Systems .
- ₹54,082 crore: Total Central Financial Support.
Battery Storage Component
For the first time under the Green Energy Corridor programme, a dedicated battery-storage component has been included.
- The scheme provides for 50 GWh of BESS capacity .
- The systems may be installed at renewable-energy developer or generator sites and other grid-important locations.
- Storage will address intermittency, transmission congestion and peak-hour curtailment.
- Stored electricity can be supplied during non-solar hours.
Consumer Benefit
Central Financial Assistance will help reduce intra-State transmission charges. Lower transmission costs can limit the final cost of renewable electricity for consumers.

## How will GEC Phase-III be Implemented?

### Greenfield Projects
- New transmission projects will be implemented through Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding .
- Transmission Service Providers will participate under the Build-Own-Operate-Maintain model .

### Brownfield Projects
- Upgradation and strengthening of existing transmission networks will be undertaken through the Cost Plus Basis .
- Under this model, the approved project cost and a regulated return are recovered through tariffs.

### Implementing Agencies
- State Transmission Utilities will serve as the overall implementing agencies.
- Transmission Service Providers will develop and operate competitively awarded projects.

## What is the Status of GEC Phase-II?
Green Energy Corridor Phase-II is being implemented in seven States:
- Gujarat
- Himachal Pradesh
- Karnataka
- Kerala
- Rajasthan
- Tamil Nadu
- Uttar Pradesh
Its targets include:
- 7,863 circuit kilometres of transmission lines; and

## Why Does India Need GEC Phase-III?

### Transmission Bottlenecks
- Renewable-energy capacity is being commissioned faster than the transmission network required to carry the electricity.
- During FY 2025–26 , approximately 6,900 GWh of clean electricity faced restrictions because of the mismatch between renewable generation and transmission infrastructure.

### Growing Grid Curtailment
During April–June 2026:
- Peak electricity demand reached a record 270 GW .
- Grid operators curtailed approximately 8,133 GWh of solar electricity because of transmission constraints and grid-security requirements.

### Intermittency of Renewable Energy
Solar and wind power are variable:
- Solar generation falls after sunset.
- Wind generation changes with weather conditions.
- Electricity production may not coincide with peak demand.
Storage and grid strengthening are therefore necessary to balance electricity supply and demand.

### Rapid Capacity Expansion
- India plans to increase renewable-energy capacity from approximately 295 GW to 500 GW by 2030 . It also aims to achieve 900 GW of installed non-fossil-fuel capacity by 2035 .
- Without corresponding transmission and storage capacity, a portion of this electricity may remain unused.

## What is Renewable-Energy Curtailment?
Curtailment occurs when grid operators direct a power-generating unit to reduce or stop electricity generation, even when the plant is capable of producing power.
It may be required because of:
- insufficient transmission capacity;
- excessive generation during low-demand periods;
- grid-security concerns;
- lack of storage facilities; or
- inability to balance variable generation.
Curtailment results in the loss of usable clean electricity and affects the financial viability of renewable-energy projects.

## What is Energy Storage?
Energy storage refers to systems that store surplus electricity when generation is high and release it when demand increases or generation declines.
The process involves:
- converting electricity into a form that can be stored;
- retaining that energy for a required period; and
- converting it back into electricity when needed.
Energy storage helps address the mismatch between the time of renewable-energy generation and the time of electricity consumption.
What are the Major Types of Energy Storage?
Pumped Hydro Storage
- Surplus electricity pumps water from a lower reservoir to a higher reservoir.
- During peak demand, water is released downhill through turbines to produce electricity.
- It is suitable for large-scale and long-duration storage where appropriate geography is available.
Battery Energy Storage Systems
- BESS stores electricity in chemical form and releases it when required.
- Lithium-ion batteries , particularly lithium iron phosphate batteries , are widely used for grid-scale storage.
- Their major advantages include declining costs, high efficiency and long operational life.
Concentrating Solar-Thermal Storage
- Mirrors focus sunlight on a receiver.
- Materials such as molten salt absorb and store heat.
- The stored heat later produces steam, which drives a turbine and generates electricity.
Compressed-Air Energy Storage
- Surplus electricity compresses air and stores it in underground caverns or tanks.
- When demand increases, the compressed air is released to operate turbines.
Flywheel Energy Storage
- Electricity is stored as rotational energy in a rapidly spinning rotor.
- Flywheels can release power almost instantly.
- They are useful for grid stability and short-term fluctuations.
Gravity Energy Storage
- Electricity is used to lift heavy weights to a higher elevation.
- When electricity is required, the weights are lowered.
Their gravitational energy is converted back into electricity through generators

## What is the Significance of GEC Phase-III?
Better Renewable-Energy Integration
The scheme will create transmission capacity to carry electricity from renewable-rich regions to demand centres.
Reduced Loss of Clean Electricity
A stronger grid and storage infrastructure will reduce the curtailment of solar and wind power.
Grid Stability
Battery systems can absorb excess electricity and release it when demand rises, thereby managing short-term fluctuations.
Support During Non-Solar Hours
Electricity stored during periods of high solar generation can be supplied in the evening or at night.
Lower Power Costs
Central assistance can reduce the transmission-cost burden and help keep electricity affordable for consumers.
Energy Security
Greater use of domestically generated renewable electricity can reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels.
Climate Benefits
The scheme will support the reduction of carbon emissions and promote ecologically sustainable growth.
Employment Generation
Transmission lines, substations and storage facilities can create direct and indirect employment in:
- power-sector services;
- equipment manufacturing;
- construction; and
- operations and maintenance.
Progress Towards National Targets
The project will support India’s targets of:
- 500 GW renewable-energy capacity by 2030 ; and
- 900 GW non-fossil-fuel capacity by 2035 .

## What Challenges Does the Renewable-Energy Sector Face?
Grid–Generation Mismatch
Transmission systems are not always completed at the same pace as renewable-energy projects, leading to stranded or curtailed generation.
Variability and Intermittency
Solar and wind generation depend on time and weather, making continuous electricity supply difficult without storage and flexible grid operations.
Transmission Congestion
Renewable-energy projects are often concentrated in resource-rich regions, while major electricity demand may be located elsewhere.
Peak-Demand Management
India must simultaneously meet record electricity demand and absorb variable renewable power without compromising grid security.
Storage Requirements
Large-scale renewable integration requires substantial energy-storage capacity to shift electricity from high-generation to high-demand periods.
Implementation Coordination
Successful implementation requires coordination among:
- MNRE;
- State governments;
- State Transmission Utilities;
- transmission providers;
- renewable-energy developers; and
- grid operators.
Financial Requirements
The programme requires investment of more than ₹1.86 lakh crore , along with timely project execution and effective use of Central assistance.

## Way Forward
- Synchronise infrastructure: Transmission lines and storage capacity should be commissioned alongside renewable-energy projects.
- Accelerate grid expansion: Both intra-State and inter-State networks must expand in renewable-rich regions.
- Adopt a storage mix: India should combine batteries, pumped hydro and other technologies according to duration and grid requirements.
- Improve forecasting: Better solar, wind and demand forecasts can reduce balancing problems and curtailment.
- Strengthen Centre–State coordination: Project approvals, financing and implementation should follow time-bound mechanisms.
- Promote domestic manufacturing: Indigenous production of batteries, grid equipment and transmission components can improve energy security.
- Ensure affordability: Competitive bidding and effective Central assistance should prevent transmission and storage costs from excessively increasing consumer tariffs.
- Improve grid flexibility: Demand-response systems and flexible power generation should complement transmission and storage expansion.

## Conclusion
India’s renewable-energy transition cannot depend on generation capacity alone. Electricity must also be transmitted, stored and delivered when consumers require it. By combining transmission expansion with 50 GWh of battery storage , GEC Phase-III addresses the critical gap between renewable-energy generation and grid readiness. Timely implementation can reduce clean-energy curtailment, strengthen energy security and support India’s transition towards a low-carbon economy.
