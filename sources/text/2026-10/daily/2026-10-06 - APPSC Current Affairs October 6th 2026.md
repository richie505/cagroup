Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0–Amarajeevi Jaladhara in Andhra Pradesh

## Why in News?
Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu launched the Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0–Amarajeevi Jaladhara programme at Proddatur in Kadapa district on 5 October 2026.
The programme involves an investment of ₹30,156 crore and aims to provide household tap-water connections across Andhra Pradesh.

## What is Amarajeevi Jaladhara?
Amarajeevi Jaladhara is a State-level rural drinking-water programme being implemented under Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 .
It seeks to:
- provide household tap-water connections;
- ensure access to safe drinking water;
- strengthen rural water-supply infrastructure; and
- expand water coverage throughout Andhra Pradesh.
The programme forms part of the State government’s wider focus on rural development and long-term water security.

## What are its Coverage and Targets?
The programme is expected to provide safe drinking water to:
- 90 lakh households ;
- approximately 48,000 rural habitations ; and
- nearly 3.30 crore people .
The stated objective is to provide tap-water connectivity to every household across Andhra Pradesh.

## What are the Expected Outcomes?
Improved Drinking-Water Access
Household tap connections can reduce dependence on distant, irregular or unsafe water sources.
Better Public Health
Regular access to safe water can help lower the risk of water-borne diseases and improve sanitation and hygiene.
Reduced Burden on Women
Rural women and girls often bear the responsibility of collecting water. Household connections can reduce this unpaid physical labour.
Improved Rural Living Standards
Reliable water supply is an essential component of housing, sanitation, healthcare and dignified living.
Strengthened Rural Infrastructure
The programme can create and upgrade pipelines, water-treatment facilities, storage systems and local distribution networks.
Employment Generation
Construction and maintenance of water infrastructure can generate employment in rural areas.

## What are Andhra Pradesh’s Long-term Water-Security Plans?
Irrigation Projects
The State government announced plans to undertake:
- 36 irrigation projects ;
- with an estimated investment of ₹35,000 crore ; and
- over the next three years .
These projects are intended to strengthen irrigation access and water security.
Polavaram Project
The government reiterated its plan to complete the Polavaram Project before the next Godavari Pushkaram .
Polavaram is central to Andhra Pradesh’s plans for irrigation, water supply and regional water management.
River Interlinking
The State also proposed pursuing the interlinking of the:
- Godavari;
- Krishna; and
- Cauvery rivers.
The proposal is intended to address drought conditions in southern India by transferring water across river basins.

## What Other Development Programmes were Announced?
Palle Panduga
- Infrastructure works worth ₹11,085 crore are being taken up under Palle Panduga .
- The programme focuses on strengthening rural infrastructure.
Housing
- The State aims to construct 19 lakh houses by 2029 .
- The stated objective is to ensure that every family has a house.
Land Titles
- Land-title distributions valued at approximately ₹52,000 crore were highlighted.
Rayalaseema Horticulture Hub
- The government announced plans for a ₹1 lakh crore global horticulture hub in Rayalaseema.
- The initiative seeks to accelerate regional growth through horticulture-related infrastructure and investment.
NTR Bharosa
- Under the NTR Bharosa pension programme , the government stated that approximately ₹76,000 crore had been disbursed to vulnerable sections.

## What is Required for Effective Implementation?
Although the programme has a large financial and physical target, its outcomes will depend on:
- timely completion of infrastructure;
- reliable availability of water at the source;
- regular testing of water quality;
- proper operation and maintenance;
- prevention of leakages;
- transparent expenditure monitoring; and
- participation of rural communities in water management.
Providing a tap connection should be accompanied by an adequate, regular and safe water supply.

## Conclusion
The ₹30,156 crore Amarajeevi Jaladhara programme represents a major expansion of Andhra Pradesh’s rural drinking-water infrastructure. Its success should be measured not merely by the number of connections installed but by the regularity, quality and sustainability of water supplied. Effective implementation can improve public health, reduce the burden on women and strengthen inclusive rural development.

## What are the Asian Games?
The Asian Games are a continental multi-sport competition involving athletes from Asian countries and regions.
- They are held once every four years .
- They are governed by the Olympic Council of Asia .
- The first Asian Games were held in New Delhi in 1951 .
- India also hosted the Games in New Delhi in 1982 .
- The Games include both Olympic disciplines and sports with strong Asian roots, such as kabaddi, kurash, wushu and sepaktakraw.

- Participating NOCs: 11
- Athletes: 489
- Sports: 12

## What is the Olympic Council of Asia?
The Olympic Council of Asia is the governing organisation of the Asian Games.

### Key Facts
- It is an independent, non-governmental and not-for-profit international sports organisation.
- It is recognised by the International Olympic Committee and the Association of National Olympic Committees .
- It represents 45 Asian National Olympic Committees .
- Its headquarters is located in Kuwait .

- The Asian Games Federation was established in New Delhi in 1949 .
- It organised the first unified Asian Games in New Delhi in 1951.
- Asian sports leaders decided to replace the Asian Games Federation with the Olympic Council of Asia in 1981 .
- The OCA headquarters was moved from India to Kuwait in 1982 . oca.asia

## Why in News?
Five sites and proposals were added to India’s UNESCO Tentative List in 2026 , raising the country’s total number of tentative properties to 78 . The additions represent Buddhist art, Gupta architecture, Gond heritage, early medieval temple architecture and the global legacy of Mahatma Gandhi’s Satyagraha. Separately, Sarnath was inscribed on the World Heritage List during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee , becoming India’s 45th World Heritage Site .

## What is UNESCO’s Tentative List?
- A Tentative List is an inventory of cultural, natural or mixed properties that a country considers suitable for future nomination to the UNESCO World Heritage List .
- A property must normally be included in the country’s Tentative List before it can be nominated for World Heritage status.
- Inclusion in the Tentative List does not automatically grant World Heritage status.
- After inclusion, the country must prepare a detailed nomination dossier demonstrating the property’s: Outstanding Universal Value (OUV); authenticity and integrity; legal protection; conservation status; and long-term management arrangements.
- In India, the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) and the Indian National Commission for Cooperation with UNESCO (INCCU) play important roles in identifying and proposing properties.

## What are the Newly Added Sites?
Bagh Cave Paintings
- Location: Madhya Pradesh
- The site consists of nine rock-cut Buddhist caves , mainly used between the 4th and 6th centuries CE .
- The caves contain important Buddhist murals, especially those in Cave 4 , known as the Rang Mahal .
- The paintings are significant examples of the development of Buddhist mural traditions after Ajanta.
- The caves are located in the Vindhyan region near the Baghni River . UNESCO World Heritage Centre
Udaigiri Caves
- Location: Madhya Pradesh
- These are a rock-cut sacred complex developed under Gupta patronage during the 4th and 5th centuries CE .
- The site comprises 20 caves , sculptures and inscriptions.
- Important sculptures include: the monumental Varaha incarnation of Vishnu in Cave 5 ; and Anantasayana Vishnu in Cave 13 .
- The site contains Vaishnava, Shaiva and Jain remains and reflects the evolution of temple art during the Gupta period. UNESCO World Heritage Centre
Group of Temples of the Chambal Valley
- Location: Morena district, Madhya Pradesh
- The proposal covers the temple complexes at Bateshwar, Padavali and Kakarmath .
- The temples were constructed between the 8th and 11th centuries CE .
- They represent an important stage in the development of regional Nagara-style temple architecture .
- Their architectural development is associated with the Gurjara-Pratihara and Kachchhapaghata dynasties. UNESCO Centre du patrimoine mondial
Ginnorgarh Fort
- Location: Within the Ratapani Wildlife Sanctuary , Madhya Pradesh
- Ginnorgarh was an important political and administrative centre of the Gond kingdom from the early 16th to the early 18th century .
- Its remains include: fortification walls and gateways; palace buildings; stepwells and reservoirs; wall paintings; and an extensive water-management system.
- Historical evidence points to a network of around 65 stepwells and four lakes .
- It represents the political and architectural legacy of an indigenous tribal kingdom. UNESCO World Heritage Centre
Sites of Satyagraha
- It is a serial transnational proposal jointly connecting India and South Africa.
- It comprises nine component properties associated with the evolution and institutionalisation of Mahatma Gandhi’s philosophy of non-violent resistance.

## What is a UNESCO World Heritage Site?
A World Heritage Site is a cultural, natural or mixed property recognised as possessing Outstanding Universal Value —significance so exceptional that it transcends national boundaries and is important to present and future generations of humanity.
Governing Convention
- World Heritage sites are governed by the Convention Concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage .
- It was adopted by UNESCO in 1972 .
- As of 2024, the Convention had 196 States Parties .
Categories of World Heritage
- Cultural Heritage: Monuments, groups of buildings and sites possessing outstanding historical, artistic or scientific value.
- Natural Heritage: Natural features or areas possessing exceptional scientific, conservation or aesthetic value.
- Mixed Heritage: Properties possessing both cultural and natural significance.

## What are UNESCO’s Selection Criteria?
A property must demonstrate Outstanding Universal Value and satisfy at least one of ten criteria . Criteria (i)–(vi) are cultural, while criteria (vii)–(x) are natural.
Cultural Criteria
- Criterion I: Masterpiece of human creative genius.
- Criterion II: Important interchange of human values.
- Criterion III: Exceptional testimony to a cultural tradition or civilisation.
- Criterion IV: Outstanding example of architecture or landscape illustrating a significant historical stage.
- Criterion V: Outstanding traditional settlement, land use or human-environment interaction.
- Criterion VI: Direct association with events, ideas, beliefs or artistic and literary works of universal significance.
Natural Criteria
- Criterion VII: Exceptional natural beauty or phenomena.
- Criterion VIII: Major stages of Earth’s geological history.
- Criterion IX: Significant ecological and biological processes.
- Criterion X: Important habitats for biodiversity conservation, including threatened species.
Until 2004, cultural and natural criteria were maintained separately. Revised Operational Guidelines combined them into a single set of ten criteria.

## How Does a Site Secure World Heritage Status?
Inclusion in the Tentative List
- The State Party first places the property on its national Tentative List. A property not included in this list normally cannot be considered for inscription.
Preliminary Assessment
- The proposal undergoes an initial assessment to determine its potential Outstanding Universal Value and whether a full nomination is justified.
Preparation of Nomination Dossier
The country prepares a comprehensive dossier containing:
- description and boundaries;
- justification of Outstanding Universal Value;
- comparative analysis;
- conservation status;
- legal protection;
- maps and documentation; and
- management and monitoring plans.
Evaluation by Advisory Bodies
- ICOMOS evaluates cultural properties.
- IUCN evaluates natural properties.
The evaluation process generally includes document review and field assessment.
Decision by the World Heritage Committee
The 21-member World Heritage Committee may:
- inscribe the property;
- refer the proposal for additional information;
- defer it for substantial revision; or
- decide not to inscribe it.
The Committee usually meets once every year.
Sarnath: India’s Latest World Heritage Site
- The Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath was inscribed during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee , held in Busan, South Korea , in July 2026.
- It became India’s 45th World Heritage Site and Uttar Pradesh’s fourth .
- The serial property includes: Chaukhandi Stupa ; and the Archaeological Remains of Sarnath .
- Sarnath is associated with the Buddha’s first sermon after enlightenment , through which he set the Dharmachakra or Wheel of Dharma in motion.
- Ancient Buddhist literature refers to the site as Rishipattan .
- Sarnath was added to India’s Tentative List on 3 July 1998 and secured World Heritage status after 28 years.
- Uttar Pradesh’s other World Heritage Sites are: Taj Mahal Agra Fort Fatehpur Sikri
Sarnath was inscribed under criteria (iii) and (vi) .

## Tentative List, World Heritage List and Danger List
| List | Meaning | Status |
| Tentative List | National inventory of potential future nominations | Does not confer World Heritage status |
| World Heritage List | Properties possessing Outstanding Universal Value and formally inscribed by the Committee | International recognition and conservation obligations |
| World Heritage in Danger | Inscribed properties facing serious actual or potential threats | Seeks urgent corrective action and international assistance |
What is the List of World Heritage in Danger?
- The list draws international attention to conditions threatening the characteristics for which a property received World Heritage status.
- Its objective is to mobilise support and encourage corrective conservation measures.
Types of Danger
- Ascertained danger: Specific, proven and imminent threats already affecting the property.
- Potential danger: Threats that could adversely affect its World Heritage values in the future.
Major Threats
- Armed conflict and war
- Earthquakes and natural disasters
- Pollution
- Poaching
- Uncontrolled urbanisation
- Unregulated tourism development
- Neglect and deterioration
Examples include the Bamiyan Valley in Afghanistan , the Historic Town of Zabid in Yemen and Bam in Iran .

## What is the Significance of the New Additions?
- Civilisational diversity: The sites cover Buddhist, Hindu, tribal and Gandhian heritage traditions.
- Regional representation: The additions highlight lesser-known heritage landscapes, especially those of Madhya Pradesh.
- Tribal heritage recognition: Ginnorgarh foregrounds the historical contribution of the Gond kingdom.
- Cultural diplomacy: The transnational Satyagraha proposal strengthens India–South Africa cultural cooperation.
- Gandhian soft power: It highlights the international influence of truth, non-violence and peaceful resistance.
- Conservation impetus: Tentative-list inclusion can encourage documentation, scientific conservation and improved management.
- Sustainable tourism: Carefully managed recognition can create local employment and strengthen regional cultural economies.

## What are the Major Challenges?
- Establishing Outstanding Universal Value through credible historical and comparative evidence.
- Maintaining the authenticity and integrity of heritage properties.
- Controlling encroachment, pollution and unplanned urban development.
- Preventing over-tourism and commercialisation.
- Coordinating conservation among multiple institutions and governments.
- Protecting local and tribal community interests.
- Mobilising adequate funds and trained conservation professionals.
- Coordinating the India–South Africa components of the Satyagraha proposal.

## Way Forward
- Prepare rigorous nomination dossiers supported by archaeological and scientific research.
- Develop site-specific conservation and disaster-management plans.
- Conduct heritage-impact assessments before permitting nearby infrastructure projects.
- Involve local communities, tribal groups and traditional knowledge holders.
- Use digital mapping and three-dimensional documentation to monitor deterioration.
- Promote regulated tourism according to the carrying capacity of each site.
- Strengthen cooperation among ASI, State governments, local authorities and UNESCO bodies .
- Establish an India–South Africa coordination mechanism for the Sites of Satyagraha proposal.

## Conclusion
The new additions broaden India’s heritage narrative by bringing together ancient art, temple architecture, tribal statecraft and the global legacy of Satyagraha. However, Tentative List inclusion is only the beginning of a demanding nomination and conservation process. Scientific preservation, community participation and sustainable tourism must accompany international recognition. Such an approach can transform heritage into a source of cultural continuity, livelihood creation and India’s soft power.
