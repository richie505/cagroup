Chloroquine-Related Illness in Andhra Pradesh: Safety Concerns in Mass Drug Administration

### Relevance: GS Paper II – Public Health and Tribal Welfare | GS Paper III – Drug Safety and Health Infrastructure

## Why in News?
A seven-year-old girl died and 52 people, including 47 children, fell ill after receiving chloroquine tablets at a medical camp in Gollaguppa village of Yetapaka mandal in Polavaram district.Twenty-three children were shifted from Lakshmipuram Primary Health Centre to Bhadrachalam Area Hospital. The exact cause requires investigation, including clinical examination and chemical analysis of the administered medicine.

## What Is Chloroquine?
Chloroquine is a medicine used in malaria management. Depending on medical guidelines and the parasite’s drug sensitivity, it may be administered:
- to treat malaria; or
- as a preventive or prophylactic medicine.
It is a potent drug that must be administered in the correct dose after assessing the patient’s age, weight, health and nutritional condition.

## What Is Malaria?
- Malaria is a mosquito-borne disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.
- The tribal regions of Andhra Pradesh have a significant presence of Plasmodium falciparum , the parasite associated with severe and cerebral malaria.
- Malaria transmission generally increases after the monsoon because stagnant water creates favourable conditions for mosquito breeding.

### Malaria Burden in Tribal Andhra Pradesh
| Area | Cases in 2024 | Cases in 2025 |
| Alluri Sitarama Raju district | About 3,690 | More than 3,500 |
| Parvathipuram Manyam district | About 2,650 | About 1,820 |
| Andhra Pradesh | About 7,800 | About 7,000 |
Historically, P. falciparum accounted for around 70–80% of Andhra Pradesh’s malaria burden . Although deaths have declined due to preventive measures, malaria remains a concern in remote tribal areas.

## Why Can Chloroquine Become Harmful?
Chloroquine has a narrow therapeutic index .
A therapeutic index indicates the difference between:
- the dose that produces the intended medical benefit; and
- the dose that produces toxic effects.
A narrow therapeutic index means that the margin between an effective dose and a harmful dose is small. Therefore, even a dosing error may cause severe reactions.

### Possible Causes of the Incident
- The reason for the mass illness has not yet been conclusively established. The investigation must examine multiple possibilities.

### Incorrect Dosage
- Children cannot be given a generalised dose. The quantity must be calculated according to age, body weight and health condition.

### Administration on an Empty Stomach
- Taking the medicine without adequate food may increase the risk of adverse reactions, particularly among children.

### Malnutrition or Dehydration
- Children suffering from malnutrition, dehydration or an underlying illness may be more vulnerable to severe drug reactions.

### Batch-Quality Problems
The authorities must verify:
- batch numbers;
- manufacturing details;
- expiry dates;
- packaging; and
- chemical composition.

### Improper Storage
- Exposure to unsuitable temperature, moisture or other poor storage conditions may cause medicine degradation or contamination.

### Inadequate Post-Dose Monitoring
- Since serious reactions can develop rapidly, patients—especially children—require observation after administration.

## Conclusion
Chloroquine can be valuable in appropriate malaria management, but incorrect dosing, vulnerable patient conditions or poor-quality medicine can make its use dangerous. The Polavaram incident requires a transparent scientific investigation and stronger safeguards for drug administration in remote tribal areas.

Pichavaram Mangroves: Valuing India’s Natural Coastal Capital

### Relevance: GS Paper III—Environment, Biodiversity, Climate Change, Disaster Management and Sustainable Development

## Why in News?
A recent study titled Economic Valuation and Blue Carbon Assessment of Ecosystem Services in the Pichavaram Mangrove Forest estimated the ecosystem’s Total Economic Value (TEV) at ₹2,485.38 crore , or approximately ₹1.83 crore per hectare .The assessment shows that mangroves are not merely forests but valuable natural infrastructure supporting coastal protection, carbon sequestration, biodiversity, fisheries, tourism and local livelihoods.

## About Pichavaram Mangrove Forest
Pichavaram is an ecologically significant coastal wetland ecosystem in Tamil Nadu. It provides interconnected ecological, economic and social benefits, including:
- protection against coastal hazards;
- conservation of biodiversity;
- support for fish, crab and shrimp populations;
- storage and sequestration of carbon;
- regulation of oxygen and nutrient cycles;
- tourism and recreation; and
- livelihood security for coastal communities.
The study used both primary and secondary information. Primary data were collected from 302 households across five mangrove-dependent villages—MGR Thittu, Kalaignar Nagar, Chinnavaikal, Patriadi and Mudasalodai.
Meaning and characteristics of mangroves

## What are Mangroves?
Mangroves are salt-tolerant trees and shrubs found in the intertidal zones of tropical and subtropical coastlines. They grow in saline or brackish water and survive tidal fluctuations, waterlogged soil, high salinity and low oxygen conditions.
They serve as an ecological bridge between terrestrial and marine ecosystems.

### Major Adaptations
- Salt tolerance: Roots filter salt, while some species remove excess salt through their leaves.
- Pneumatophores: Aerial roots rise above waterlogged soil to absorb oxygen.
- Prop roots: Provide stability against tides, waves and storm surges.
- Vivipary: Seeds germinate while attached to the parent plant, improving survival in saline environments.
- Efficient carbon storage: Mangroves store substantial carbon in vegetation and waterlogged sediments.

## Economic Valuation of Pichavaram
The study assigned monetary value to various market and non-market services provided by the ecosystem.
| Ecosystem service | Estimated value |
| Fish, crab and shrimp | ₹3.47 crore |
| Coastal protection | ₹147.17 crore |
| Nutrient cycling | ₹10.53 crore |
| Oxygen regulation | ₹181.22 crore |
| Annual carbon sequestration | ₹26.86 crore |
| Biodiversity conservation | ₹19.48 crore |
| Recreation and tourism | ₹484.25 crore |
| Carbon stock in vegetation | ₹387.77 crore |
| Soil organic carbon | ₹1,224.63 crore |
| Total Economic Value | ₹2,485.38 crore |
The valuation reveals that conventional economic assessments significantly underestimate mangroves because many of their services—such as storm protection, carbon storage and nutrient cycling—do not have direct market prices.

## Blue-Carbon Potential
Blue carbon refers to carbon captured and stored by coastal and marine ecosystems such as mangroves, salt marshes and seagrass beds.
The study found that Pichavaram’s vegetation stores, on average:
- 79.449 tonnes of carbon per hectare ; and
- carbon equivalent to approximately 291.33 tonnes of CO₂ per hectare .
Mangrove sediments contain a still larger carbon reservoir:
- average soil organic carbon stock: 251.14 tonnes per hectare ;
- economic value of soil carbon: ₹90.24 lakh per hectare ; and
- total soil organic carbon value: ₹1,224.63 crore .
The combined economic value of vegetation and soil carbon in Pichavaram was estimated at ₹1,612.40 crore .

## Why Below-Ground Carbon Matters
- A majority of mangrove carbon is stored in waterlogged soil and sediments. Low-oxygen conditions slow the decomposition of organic material, allowing carbon to remain stored for long periods.
- Therefore, conservation must protect not only visible mangrove vegetation but also the underlying hydrology and sediments. Clearing or disturbing mangrove soil can release accumulated carbon back into the atmosphere.

## Ecosystem Services of Mangroves

### 1. Provisioning Services
Mangroves provide fish, crabs, shrimp, honey, timber and other biological resources. They function as breeding and nursery grounds for several aquatic species and support local and commercial fisheries.

### 2. Regulating Services
Mangroves:
- absorb wave energy;
- reduce coastal erosion;
- moderate storm surges;
- provide protection from cyclones and tsunamis;
- store carbon;
- regulate oxygen; and
- filter pollutants, sediments and excess nutrients.
Their ability to purify coastal water has earned them the description “Kidneys of the Planet.”

### 3. Supporting Services
They assist in nutrient cycling, soil formation, biodiversity maintenance and the functioning of coastal food chains.

### 4. Cultural Services
Mangrove landscapes support recreation, education, scientific research and ecotourism. At Pichavaram, recreation and tourism were valued at ₹484.25 crore , making them a major component of its economic value.

## Importance of Mangrove Valuation

### Recognition as Natural Infrastructure
- Mangroves provide coastal protection services that would otherwise require expensive sea walls, embankments or other artificial infrastructure.

### Improved Development Decisions
- Assigning monetary value to ecosystem services enables governments to compare the ecological cost of destroying mangroves with the expected benefits of coastal projects.

### Climate Finance
- Measured carbon stocks can support blue-carbon projects, carbon-credit programmes and climate-finance mechanisms.

### Natural Capital Accounting
- Mangrove valuation can be included in Natural Capital Accounts and Green GDP , thereby reflecting environmental gains and losses alongside conventional economic growth.

### Community Welfare
- Economic valuation strengthens the case for directing conservation benefits towards communities dependent on fisheries, tourism and other mangrove resources.

## Mangrove Cover in India
According to the India State of Forest Report 2023:
- India has 4,992 sq. km of mangrove cover;
- mangroves account for around 0.15% of India’s geographical area;
- mangrove cover increased from 4,628 sq. km in 2013 to 4,992 sq. km in 2023;
- West Bengal has the largest share, followed by Gujarat and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

## Major Mangrove Regions
- Sundarbans, West Bengal: India’s largest mangrove forest and habitat of the Royal Bengal Tiger.
- Bhitarkanika, Odisha: A Ramsar site known for its estuarine crocodiles.
- Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna deltas: Important for fisheries and coastal security.
- Pichavaram, Tamil Nadu: A major mangrove complex with significant tourism and blue-carbon value.
- Gulf of Kutch, Gujarat: A distinctive mangrove ecosystem in an arid region.
- Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Important habitats supporting diverse marine and terrestrial species.

## Threats to Mangroves
The IUCN’s 2024 global assessment warned that 50% of the world’s mangrove ecosystems are at risk of collapse .

### Natural and Climate-related Threats
- cyclones and storm surges;
- sea-level rise;
- coastal erosion;
- altered tidal flows;
- changing salinity;
- increasing temperature; and
- extreme weather events.
Mangroves can protect coastlines from cyclones, but repeated severe events may also damage their vegetation and sediment structure.

### Human-induced Threats
- conversion for shrimp farming and agriculture;
- rapid coastal urbanisation;
- construction of ports and tourism infrastructure;
- industrial effluents and untreated sewage;
- plastic pollution and oil spills;
- illegal logging for fuel and timber; and
- hydrological disruption caused by embankments and land reclamation.
Habitat fragmentation can weaken natural regeneration and reduce the movement of fish and other species.

## Mangrove Conservation Initiatives

### MISHTI
- The Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats and Tangible Incomes (MISHTI) was launched in 2023 to promote mangrove plantation and restoration along coastlines and saltpan lands. It received an initial outlay of ₹100 crore.
- The programme connects ecological restoration with livelihood creation and coastal resilience.

### Coastal Regulation Zone Rules
- CRZ regulations restrict activities such as industrial construction, dumping and land reclamation in ecologically sensitive coastal areas, including mangrove habitats.

### Sustainable Aquaculture in Mangrove Ecosystems
- Sustainable integrated mangrove-aquaculture models seek to reconcile livelihood requirements with ecosystem protection instead of replacing mangroves with intensive aquaculture farms.

## Other Measures
- National Mangrove Committee;
- Conservation and Management of Mangroves and Coral Reefs programme;
- Magical Mangroves public-awareness campaign;
- Ramsar Convention on Wetlands;
- UNESCO’s Man and the Biosphere Programme;
- IUCN restoration programmes; and
- international Blue Carbon Initiative.

## Challenges
- Conversion of mangroves for aquaculture and agriculture
- Coastal urbanisation, ports and tourism infrastructure
- Industrial effluents, sewage, plastics and oil spills
- Illegal logging for fuel, timber and charcoal
- Cyclones, coastal erosion and rising sea levels
- Changes in tidal flow, salinity and sediment supply
- Weak valuation of non-market ecosystem services
- Fragmented monitoring and limited community participation
- Poorly planned restoration using unsuitable species
- Inadequate inclusion of soil carbon in climate policies

## Way Forward
- Integrate mangrove valuation into coastal planning and environmental impact assessments.
- Include mangrove assets in Natural Capital Accounting and Green GDP .
- Expand MISHTI through scientifically planned, native-species restoration.
- Strengthen enforcement of Coastal Regulation Zone rules .
- Protect natural tidal flows, sediments and below-ground carbon reserves.
- Develop blue-carbon finance, carbon credits and Payment for Ecosystem Services .
- Promote community-managed fisheries, sustainable aquaculture and ecotourism.
- Ensure fair benefit-sharing with mangrove-dependent communities.
- Control industrial discharge, untreated sewage and plastic pollution.
- Use satellites, GIS, drones and artificial intelligence for regular monitoring.
- Encourage individuals and institutions to adopt and maintain mangrove patches.
- Strengthen long-term research on biodiversity, carbon storage and climate resilience.

## Conclusion
The valuation of Pichavaram demonstrates that conserving mangroves is an economic and developmental investment, not merely an environmental obligation. Their benefits extend from fisheries and tourism to carbon storage, biodiversity conservation and disaster-risk reduction.India must integrate mangrove values into coastal planning, Green GDP, climate finance and natural-capital accounting. Scientific restoration, strict regulation and community participation can ensure that mangroves continue to protect both coastal ecology and human well-being.

Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope: Mapping the Invisible Universe

### Relevance: GS Paper III—Science and Technology, Space Technology and Awareness in Space
For Prelims: Nancy Grace Roman | Lagrange Point 2 | Wide Field Instrument | Coronagraph | Infrared Telescope | Exoplanet | Gravitational Lensing

## Why in News?
NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aboard a SpaceX rocket on August 30, 2026 .The next-generation infrared telescope will survey vast regions of space to study some of the universe’s greatest mysteries, including dark matter, dark energy and exoplanets .It is named after Nancy Grace Roman , NASA’s first Chief Astronomer, who is popularly known as the “Mother of Hubble.”

## About the Roman Space Telescope
- The Roman Space Telescope is a wide-field space observatory designed to study the structure, composition and evolution of the universe.
- Its scientific capabilities lie between those of the Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) . While Hubble and JWST can examine selected objects in great detail, Roman is designed to survey much larger areas of the sky rapidly.

### Key Facts
- Space agency: National Aeronautics and Space Administration
- Launch date: August 30, 2026
- Launch vehicle: SpaceX rocket
- Type: Infrared space telescope
- Operational location: Sun–Earth Lagrange Point 2
- Mass: Approximately 8,000 kg
- Size: Comparable to a tour bus
- Mission duration: Five years
- Daily data transmission: Around 1.4 terabytes
- Expected total data: Up to 20 petabytes
- Major instruments: Wide Field Instrument and Coronagraph

## Scientific Objectives
The telescope will seek to:
- study the distribution of dark matter;
- investigate the role of dark energy in cosmic expansion;
- discover and examine exoplanets;
- map galaxies and galaxy clusters;
- observe gravitational lensing;
- study the formation and evolution of cosmic structures; and
- image faint objects located close to bright stars.

## What Is Dark Matter?
- Dark matter is an invisible form of matter that neither emits nor reflects detectable electromagnetic radiation.
Scientists infer its existence through its gravitational effects on:
- stars;
- galaxies;
- galaxy clusters; and
- the path of light moving through space.
- Ordinary matter—such as stars, planets, gases and living organisms—is estimated to constitute only around 5% of the universe . Dark matter is believed to account for approximately 27% .
- Dark matter is often described as an invisible cosmic “glue” because its gravity helps hold galaxies together.

## How Will Roman Study Dark Matter?
The telescope will observe the distribution of galaxies and measure how their light is distorted by gravity. These observations can help scientists:
- map the location of dark matter;
- understand its distribution across the universe;
- examine its role in the formation of galaxies; and
- study its relationship with early star formation.

## What Is Dark Energy?
- Dark energy is the term used for an unknown phenomenon believed to be causing the accelerated expansion of the universe.
- It is estimated to constitute approximately 68% of the universe . Unlike gravity, which attracts matter, dark energy is understood as having a repulsive effect on the large-scale expansion of space.

## How Will Roman Study Dark Energy?
The telescope will survey large numbers of galaxies across vast cosmic distances. By observing their distribution and changes over time, scientists can examine:
- the rate at which the universe expanded in different periods;
- how dark energy may have influenced that expansion;
- whether its effects have remained constant; and
- how galaxies and galaxy clusters evolved.
Its wide-field capability will enable scientists to collect observations on a scale difficult to achieve with earlier space telescopes.

## Search for Exoplanets
- Exoplanets are planets located outside the Solar System. They generally orbit stars or stellar remnants.
- More than 6,000 exoplanets have already been identified. However, these represent only a small fraction of the planets that may exist in the Milky Way.
- The Roman telescope’s ability to examine large areas of the sky may help discover many more exoplanets and improve understanding of:
- planetary formation;
- different types of planetary systems;
- the frequency of planets in the galaxy;
- planets orbiting at large distances from their stars; and
- the possible conditions required for life.
Its observations could contribute to the broader scientific question of whether life exists elsewhere in the universe.

## What Is Gravitational Lensing?
Gravitational lensing occurs when the gravity of a massive object, such as a galaxy or galaxy cluster, bends the path of light travelling from a more distant object.
The foreground object acts like a natural lens. It can:
- distort the image of the distant object;
- magnify otherwise faint objects;
- produce multiple images; and
- reveal the presence of invisible matter.
Roman will measure subtle distortions in the shapes of galaxies caused by gravitational lensing. These measurements will help scientists map dark matter and produce a three-dimensional picture of galaxies and galaxy clusters.

## Where Will the Telescope Operate?
- The Roman Space Telescope will operate near the Sun–Earth Lagrange Point 2 (L2) , located approximately 1.2 million kilometres from Earth , according to the provided report.
- A Lagrange point is a region where the gravitational forces of two large celestial bodies and the orbital motion of a smaller object create relatively stable conditions.

### Advantages of L2
- It provides a relatively stable observing environment.
- The Sun and Earth remain broadly in the same direction.
- It supports stable optical and thermal performance.
- The telescope gets a largely unobstructed view of the universe.
- It reduces the need for frequent major orbital adjustments.
- Protective equipment can shield the telescope from solar heat and light.
The James Webb Space Telescope also operates in the L2 region.

## Major Scientific Instruments

### Wide Field Instrument
The Wide Field Instrument (WFI) is a 300-megapixel infrared camera .
It can:
- capture an area of the sky about 1.5 times larger than the apparent size of the full Moon in a single observation;
- survey large regions of the universe;
- detect distant galaxies and stars;
- support dark-matter and dark-energy studies; and
- process images significantly faster than Hubble.
The telescope is expected to process images approximately 1,000 times faster than the Hubble Space Telescope .
Its wide field of view will allow scientists to combine detailed observations with large-scale surveys.

### Coronagraph
A coronagraph blocks the intense light of a star so that astronomers can observe faint objects located nearby.
The process is similar to using a protective shield to block bright sunlight and view objects around it more clearly.
Roman’s advanced coronagraph will attempt to:
- suppress the glare of nearby stars;
- detect faint reflected light from orbiting planets;
- directly image selected exoplanets;
- study dusty disks surrounding stars; and
- test technologies required for future planet-imaging missions.
The instrument may help scientists observe older, colder and fainter objects that are otherwise hidden by bright starlight.

## Roman, Hubble and James Webb Telescopes

### Hubble Space Telescope
- Hubble has operated since 1990 and is known for detailed observations of selected astronomical objects. Its relatively narrow field of view makes it highly effective for deep, focused imaging.

### James Webb Space Telescope
- JWST, launched in 2021, is designed mainly for highly sensitive infrared observations. It examines early galaxies, star formation, planetary atmospheres and distant cosmic objects in remarkable detail.

### Roman Space Telescope
- Roman combines high-quality imaging with a much wider field of view. Its strength lies in rapidly surveying large parts of the sky and identifying patterns across enormous populations of galaxies, stars and planets.
Thus:
- Hubble provides detailed views of smaller areas;
- JWST conducts deep and sensitive infrared observations; and
- Roman performs large-scale, wide-field cosmic surveys.
These telescopes are complementary rather than direct substitutes.

## Data Collection and Transmission
Roman is expected to transmit approximately 1.4 terabytes of scientific data every day .
Ground stations located in:
- New Mexico;
- Australia; and
- Japan
will receive the data.
The telescope may produce up to 20 petabytes of information during its five-year mission.
This vast data volume will require:
- advanced storage systems;
- high-speed communication networks;
- powerful computing facilities;
- automated data processing;
- artificial intelligence-based analysis; and
- international scientific collaboration.

## Significance of the Mission

### Understanding the Universe
- Roman may improve knowledge of dark matter and dark energy, which together are believed to constitute most of the universe.

### Large-scale Cosmic Mapping
- Its wide-field surveys can create a detailed three-dimensional map of galaxies and galaxy clusters.

### Expansion of Exoplanet Science
- The mission can increase the number and diversity of known exoplanets and support the search for planetary systems resembling the Solar System.

### Technological Demonstration
- The coronagraph will test advanced methods for directly imaging faint planets near bright stars.

### Complementing Existing Telescopes
- Roman can identify scientifically important objects across wide regions. Hubble, JWST and ground-based observatories can then conduct detailed follow-up studies.

### Growth of Data-driven Astronomy
Its extensive datasets will create opportunities for scientists worldwide to conduct new studies long after the observations are collected.

## Challenges
- Complex operations: Managing a large telescope far from Earth requires precise navigation and monitoring.
- Limited servicing options: Repairing instruments near L2 is more difficult than servicing objects in low-Earth orbit.
- Large data volume: Processing and storing up to 20 petabytes will require advanced computing infrastructure.
- Image interpretation: Gravitational-lensing and dark-energy measurements demand extremely accurate data analysis.
- Coronagraph performance: Detecting a faint planet beside a bright star requires exceptional control of light and optical stability.
- Communication dependence: Continuous scientific output depends on reliable links with ground stations.
- Mission lifespan: The telescope must achieve its major objectives within its expected five-year operational period.
- Scientific uncertainty: The mission may map the effects of dark matter and dark energy without directly identifying their fundamental nature.

## Way Forward
- Coordinate Roman observations with Hubble, JWST and ground-based telescopes.
- Develop open and accessible scientific data archives.
- Strengthen global cooperation in data processing and astronomical research.
- Apply artificial intelligence and machine learning to analyse large datasets.
- Conduct systematic follow-up observations of newly discovered exoplanets.
- Use the coronagraph’s performance to improve future direct-imaging telescopes.
- Build greater computing and storage capacity for data-intensive astronomy.
- Support universities and young researchers in using mission data.
- Maintain long-term monitoring of galaxies, supernovae and gravitational-lensing events.
- Convert Roman’s findings into more precise models of cosmic expansion and structure formation.

## Conclusion
The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope represents a major advance in wide-field space astronomy. Its ability to observe vast regions rapidly will complement the detailed capabilities of Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope.By mapping galaxies, measuring gravitational lensing and discovering exoplanets, Roman may deepen scientific understanding of both the visible and invisible universe. Although it may not immediately solve every mystery, it can provide the large and precise datasets required to investigate dark matter, dark energy and the evolution of the cosmos.
