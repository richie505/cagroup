Tirumala Introduces Digital Pilgrim Centre, Revamped Museum and Electric Buses

### Relevance: APPSC Group I Paper IV: Andhra Pradesh economy and infrastructure
- Prelims: Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) , Pilgrim Experience Centre , Vaikuntham Queue Complex , Sri Venkateswara Museum , virtual reality, artificial intelligence, electric mobility, Reliance Foundation , Bharat Biotech , Annamacharya , Sri Krishnadevaraya , Tirumala Seven Hills, Brahmotsavams.
- Mains: Technology-enabled public services, sustainable pilgrimage, heritage interpretation, digital cultural preservation, green mobility, public-private partnership, responsible tourism, inclusive pilgrim management.

## Why in News?
- Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu inaugurated a Pilgrim Experience Centre and the revamped Sri Venkateswara Museum at Tirumala on September 16, 2026 .
- He also flagged off 32 electric buses , donated by the Reliance Foundation and Bharat Biotech , to strengthen sustainable transport facilities for pilgrims at the hill shrine.

## Pilgrim Experience Centre
- The Pilgrim Experience Centre has been established at the Vaikuntham Queue Complex at a cost of ₹4 crore .
- It seeks to familiarise pilgrims with the spiritual traditions of Tirumala through immersive digital technologies.
Major Features
- Three-dimensional videos explain the daily kainkaryams and puja rituals performed for Lord Venkateswara.
- Digital presentations describe the religious and cultural significance of Tirumala.
- A virtual reality-based darshan allows devotees to experience important aspects of the shrine digitally.
- The centre can engage pilgrims during the waiting period and improve their understanding of temple traditions.
The initiative represents the use of technology not as a substitute for physical worship, but as an instrument for cultural education and improved pilgrim experience.

## Electric Mobility Initiative
The Chief Minister flagged off 32 electric buses at Narayanagiri Circle to transport devotees within Tirumala.
Of these:
- Reliance Foundation donated 25 electric buses.
- Bharat Biotech contributed seven electric buses.
- The two organisations also handed over the necessary charging stations to the TTD.
- During his Tirumala visit in June 2026, Reliance Executive Director Anant Ambani had announced the donation of 25 electric buses, valued at approximately ₹27.5 crore, for the convenience of pilgrims.

## Importance of Electric Buses
- Environmental protection: Tirumala is an ecologically sensitive hill region. Electric buses can reduce tailpipe emissions and local air pollution.
- Lower noise pollution: Quieter electric vehicles are suitable for maintaining the calm atmosphere of a religious centre.
- Sustainable pilgrim transport: Clean public transport can reduce dependence on conventional fuel vehicles and support large-scale movement of devotees.
- Public-private partnership: Corporate contributions supplement the TTD’s infrastructure and demonstrate the potential of institutional cooperation in public service delivery.
- Supporting infrastructure: The provision of charging stations along with the buses is important for their continuous and reliable operation.

## Revamped Sri Venkateswara Museum
- The Chief Minister also inaugurated the revamped Sri Venkateswara Museum , which presents the historical, religious and cultural significance of Tirumala and Lord Venkateswara.

### Important Exhibits
- Models of jewellery offered to the deity on different occasions.
- Jewellery associated with Sri Krishnadevaraya , the Vijayanagara ruler and devotee of Lord Venkateswara.
- Three-dimensional replicas of crowns and ornaments worn by the deity.
- Exhibits on the temples and sculptures located across the Tirumala hills.
- Displays explaining the naturally formed geographical features of the region.
- A 3D model of the Seven Hills , designed to familiarise pilgrims with Tirumala’s physical and sacred landscape.
- Weapons surrendered by kings who visited Tirumala and later embraced a spiritual path.
- Musical instruments used by devotees and saint-composers, including Annamacharya , to praise Lord Venkateswara.
The museum also has a panoramic screen on which an AI-generated video depicting the wedding festivities of Lord Venkateswara is displayed.

## Conclusion
The new facilities at Tirumala demonstrate how technology and sustainability can strengthen the management of a major pilgrimage centre. Digital interpretation can improve cultural understanding, while electric mobility can reduce the ecological impact of pilgrim transport. Their long-term success will depend on accessibility, historical authenticity, effective maintenance and environmental safeguards. The model can offer useful lessons for sustainable and technology-enabled management of other pilgrimage destinations in India.

Bamboo Economy: From Green Stems to Green Enterprises

### Relevance: GS Paper III: Agriculture, rural economy, MSMEs, inclusive growth, environment and green technology
Prelims: Bamboo, Bambusoideae , Poaceae , Indian Forest Act, 1927 , 2017 amendment, National Bamboo Mission , NECTAR , engineered bamboo, Gadchiroli Agarbatti Project , CSIR-NEERI , Self-Help Groups.
Mains: Green economy, rural entrepreneurship, women-led development, bamboo value chain, sustainable construction, tribal livelihoods, circular economy, local value addition, bioeconomy, climate-resilient livelihoods.

## Why in News?
Bamboo has gained attention as a sustainable resource capable of supporting rural employment, tribal livelihoods, women-led enterprises, handicrafts, construction and green industries .At the SARAS Aajeevika Mela 2026 in Gurugram, bamboo products made by rural women entrepreneurs demonstrated how traditional skills, policy reforms and government support are transforming bamboo into a modern commercial resource.

## About Bamboo
Bamboo belongs to the subfamily Bambusoideae of the grass family Poaceae . It includes more than 115 genera and 1,400 species distributed mainly across tropical, subtropical and mild temperate regions.
Important Characteristics
- Bamboo is an evergreen and perennial plant .
- It has woody and usually hollow stems that develop from underground rhizomes .
- Some bamboo species can grow by nearly 30 centimetres a day under favourable conditions.
- Bamboo regenerates rapidly after harvesting, making it a renewable biological resource.
- It is generally propagated through vegetative methods such as stem cuttings because its flowering and seed production are irregular.
- It grows well in warm climates and well-drained sandy to clay soils.
- It is often called “poor man’s timber” because of its affordability, versatility and importance to rural livelihoods.
Bamboo is used to produce baskets, fishing equipment, musical instruments, furniture, incense sticks, food products, housing materials, handicrafts and modern construction components.
India’s bamboo resources are concentrated mainly in the northeastern States of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura .

## Policy Reform of 2017
- Under the Indian Forest Act, 1927 , bamboo was legally treated as a “tree”. Consequently, its harvesting, transit and transportation were subject to forestry regulations. This discouraged farmers and private enterprises from taking up bamboo cultivation outside forests.
- In 2017 , the Act was amended to exclude bamboo grown on non-forest land from the definition of a tree.

### Impact of the Reform
The amendment:
- simplified harvesting and transportation of bamboo grown outside forests;
- encouraged commercial bamboo cultivation by farmers;
- promoted investment in processing and manufacturing;
- created opportunities for artisans, Self-Help Groups and MSMEs;
- facilitated local value addition in bamboo-rich regions; and
- strengthened the potential of bamboo as an agricultural and industrial raw material.
However, bamboo located inside notified forest areas continues to be regulated under applicable forest laws.

## India’s Emerging Bamboo Economy
Bamboo is moving beyond its traditional association with handicrafts. It is increasingly used in furniture, interior decoration, processed foods, renewable energy and construction materials.
Different States illustrate this transition:
- Sikkim: Women-led enterprises produce furniture, handicrafts, incense sticks and interior decoration products.
- Tripura: Manufacturers are adopting technology to improve bamboo-product manufacturing.
- Nagaland: SHGs and local enterprises are producing bamboo food items, furniture and handicrafts.
- Mizoram: Scientific cultivation is being promoted through tissue culture and poly-house management.
- Assam: Women artisans are connecting traditional bamboo skills with urban markets and trade exhibitions.
This diversification can reduce the dependence of rural producers on the sale of untreated bamboo poles and enable them to earn more through finished products.

## National Bamboo Mission
- The National Bamboo Mission (NBM) was launched in 2006–07 . It has been implemented as a restructured Centrally Sponsored Scheme since 2018–19 .
- The Mission promotes the holistic development of the bamboo sector through a cluster-based approach . It connects farmers, processors, manufacturers and markets across the bamboo value chain.

### Major Areas of Support
- Expansion of bamboo cultivation on suitable non-forest land
- Establishment and strengthening of nurseries
- Availability of quality planting material
- Processing and value-addition facilities
- Training and capacity-building for farmers and artisans
- Development of markets for bamboo products
- Creation of linkages between farmers and industries
- Promotion of region-specific bamboo varieties and products
The Mission seeks to establish bamboo as “Green Gold” —a renewable resource that supports livelihoods, industrial growth and the green bioeconomy.

### Farm-Level Impact
- In Madhya Pradesh, farmer Vijay Patidar shifted towards bamboo cultivation after suffering losses from conventional crops. With assistance under the Mission, he planted around 4,000 bamboo saplings .
- He earned approximately ₹75,000 from selling bamboo poles within two years. The plantation also allowed him to intercrop chilli, capsicum, ginger and garlic. This shows that bamboo can function as both a long-term asset and a source of livelihood diversification.

### Technology and Value Addition
- The economic potential of bamboo depends on processing, product design, machinery, skills and market access. Without these facilities, farmers may receive only a small share of the value generated from the final product.
- The North East Centre for Technology Application and Reach (NECTAR) has supported the application of bamboo technology in:
- food and agro-processing;
- manufacturing machinery;
- construction materials;
- renewable energy;
- industrial products;
- furniture and interior design.
- These interventions are estimated to generate around 30 million man-days of employment annually . They have also increased value addition in bamboo commodities from about 10% to as high as 70% .

## Skill Development
In June 2026, CSIR–National Environmental Engineering Research Institute (CSIR-NEERI), Nagpur , organised a five-day bamboo-handicraft training programme for women SHGs.
The programme covered:
- bamboo harvesting and treatment;
- processing techniques;
- handicraft production;
- product development and design;
- entrepreneurship;
- branding and marketing.
It also demonstrated the possibility of cultivating bamboo on reclaimed fly-ash dumping sites , connecting livelihood creation with the productive restoration of degraded land.

## Engineered Bamboo
- Engineered bamboo is a composite material manufactured by binding and compressing bamboo fibres, strips or particles. It provides bamboo with greater uniformity, strength and durability for construction and industrial applications.
- More than 42 lakh square feet of engineered bamboo structures have been created for disaster relief and rehabilitation. In Chhattisgarh , 576 school classrooms made from engineered bamboo provide educational space for nearly 20,000 children .

### Importance of Engineered Bamboo
- Expands bamboo use beyond conventional handicrafts
- Provides a renewable alternative to some traditional construction materials
- Creates demand for processed bamboo
- Encourages innovation in architecture and interior design
- Supports prefabricated construction for schools and disaster relief
- Increases income opportunities across the value chain
Quality standards, fire resistance, structural testing and long-term durability must be ensured before engineered bamboo is adopted on a wider scale.

### Women-Led Bamboo Enterprises
- Women’s SHGs are becoming important participants in bamboo cultivation, processing, handicrafts and marketing.

### Gadchiroli Agarbatti Project
The Gadchiroli Agarbatti Project was started in Maharashtra in 2012 to establish bamboo-based incense-stick production centres.
By 2020:
- 32 production centres had been established;
- around 1,100 persons were employed;
- nearly 90% of the workers were women ;
- women earned approximately ₹5,000 per month ; and
- annual employment increased from around 45–60 days to more than 225 days .
The project brought employment closer to women’s homes, reduced physically strenuous work and lowered dependence on forests for subsistence income. It also strengthened their technical skills, confidence and leadership.

## Significance of the Bamboo Sector
Rural and Tribal Livelihoods
Bamboo provides income to farmers, forest-dependent communities, artisans and tribal households. Local processing can create employment within villages and reduce distress migration.
Women’s Empowerment
SHG-based enterprises allow women to acquire skills, earn independent incomes and participate in business decisions. They promote the transition from livelihood assistance to women-led entrepreneurship.
Green Industrialisation
Bamboo is a rapidly renewable biological resource. Its use in furniture, construction and industrial products can support the development of a low-carbon bioeconomy.
Agricultural Diversification
Bamboo offers a long-term source of income and can be combined with suitable intercrops during the early years of plantation. This can reduce dependence on a single agricultural commodity.
Development of Northeast India
The Northeast possesses extensive bamboo resources and traditional knowledge. Processing industries, design centres and market linkages can convert this natural advantage into employment and regional development.
Restoration of Degraded Land
Bamboo can be considered for selected degraded areas, including scientifically reclaimed industrial sites. Such plantations must be based on suitable species, local ecology and proper land assessment.

## Challenges
- Weak processing infrastructure: Many bamboo-producing regions lack treatment, storage and manufacturing facilities.
- Limited market access: Rural artisans often depend on intermediaries and have inadequate access to organised markets.
- Quality concerns: Lack of uniform standards affects the use of bamboo in construction and high-value manufacturing.
- Inadequate planting material: Farmers may not have easy access to certified, region-appropriate bamboo varieties.
- Long gestation period: Commercial returns may take time, requiring financial support during the initial years.
- Skill and design gaps: Traditional producers need training in modern design, branding, packaging and digital marketing.
- Fragmented value chain: Weak coordination among cultivators, processors, manufacturers and buyers restricts value addition.
- Ecological risks: Large monoculture plantations and the introduction of unsuitable species may affect local biodiversity and water availability.

## Way Forward
- Develop bamboo clusters near major cultivation areas with common processing and treatment facilities.
- Improve access to certified planting material and scientific cultivation advice.
- Create assured purchase arrangements between farmer groups and industries.
- Develop national standards for engineered bamboo, furniture and construction products.
- Expand credit, insurance and working-capital support for SHGs and bamboo-based MSMEs.
- Promote geographical and cultural branding of traditional bamboo products.
- Strengthen e-commerce, government procurement and participation in national exhibitions.
- Encourage collaboration among agricultural universities, design institutes, research organisations and artisans.
- Support bamboo cultivation through region-specific ecological assessments rather than uniform plantation models.
- Integrate bamboo enterprises with rural development, tribal welfare, women’s livelihood and green-skilling programmes.

## Conclusion
India’s bamboo sector is changing from a traditional livelihood activity into a diversified green-enterprise ecosystem. The 2017 regulatory reform, the National Bamboo Mission, technological innovation and women-led SHGs have expanded its commercial possibilities. Stronger processing infrastructure, quality standards, finance and market access can enable bamboo to support inclusive rural development. Its growth must remain ecologically responsible and ensure that farmers, artisans, women and tribal communities receive a fair share of the value created.

PM Vishwakarma: Strengthening India’s Traditional Artisans and Craftspeople

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper III: MSMEs, employment, inclusive growth, digital economy and skill development
Prelims: PM Vishwakarma , Central Sector Scheme, Ministry of MSME , 18 traditional trades, Udyam Assist Platform , Common Service Centres, GeM , ONDC , One Station One Product, AVSAR, TRIFED.
Mains: Artisan economy, informal sector, financial inclusion, cultural preservation, skill upgradation, enterprise formalisation, digital inclusion, market linkages, Guru-Shishya tradition, inclusive growth.

## Why in News?
PM Vishwakarma , launched on 17 September 2023 , is approaching its third anniversary. The scheme has achieved its registration target of 30 lakh beneficiaries .It has provided traditional artisans with skill training, modern toolkits, collateral-free loans, digital transaction incentives and wider market access. A concurrent evaluation by IIM Mumbai also found that a large proportion of surveyed beneficiaries reported income improvements after training.

## About PM Vishwakarma
PM Vishwakarma is a Central Sector Scheme designed to provide end-to-end support to artisans and craftspeople engaged in traditional, family-based occupations.
The scheme recognises that traditional artisans contribute to:
- India’s cultural heritage;
- the rural and urban informal economy;
- local employment;
- family-based skill transmission; and
- production of handmade goods and services.
It was launched with an outlay of ₹13,000 crore for the period from 2023–24 to 2027–28 . The scheme covers artisans working in 18 notified traditional trades .
The Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises is the nodal ministry. The scheme is implemented with support from the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship and the Department of Financial Services .

## Objectives of the Scheme
PM Vishwakarma seeks to:
- recognise artisans as Vishwakarmas ;
- improve their skills through structured training;
- provide modern and efficient tools;
- facilitate access to affordable institutional credit;
- promote digital transactions;
- strengthen branding, packaging and marketing;
- connect artisans with domestic and global supply chains;
- promote formalisation of informal micro-enterprises; and
- preserve the Guru-Shishya parampara and family-based traditional knowledge.

## Guiding Pillars
The scheme is based on three principles:
- Samman: Recognition and respect for traditional artisans
- Samarthya: Improvement of skills, productivity and enterprise capacity
- Samriddhi: Higher income, market access and long-term prosperity

## Who Is Eligible?
The scheme is open to artisans and craftspeople who:
- are at least 18 years of age ;
- work with their hands and tools;
- are engaged in one of the 18 notified traditional trades;
- work in the unorganised sector;
- are self-employed; and
- satisfy the prescribed family and credit-related conditions.

## Important Eligibility Conditions
- One beneficiary per family: Registration is limited to one member of a family. For this purpose, a family includes the husband, wife and unmarried children.
- No government employee: A person in government service and members of his or her family are not eligible.
- Credit-related restriction: Applicants should generally not have availed loans under similar government credit-based self-employment schemes during the preceding five years.
- However, beneficiaries of Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana and PM SVANidhi who have fully repaid their loans are eligible.

### Traditional Trades Covered
PM Vishwakarma covers the following 18 family-based traditional trades:
- Carpenter
- Boat maker
- Armourer
- Blacksmith or metalsmith
- Hammer and toolkit maker
- Locksmith
- Goldsmith
- Potter
- Sculptor and stone breaker
- Cobbler, shoesmith and footwear artisan
- Mason
- Basket, mat and broom maker or coir weaver
- Doll and toy maker
- Barber
- Garland maker
- Washerman
- Tailor
- Fishing-net maker

## Major Components of PM Vishwakarma

### Recognition
After registration and verification, every eligible beneficiary receives:
- a PM Vishwakarma Certificate ; and
- a PM Vishwakarma Identity Card .
The certificate provides formal recognition to artisans whose occupations have traditionally remained outside organised economic systems.

### Skill Upgradation
Training introduces artisans to modern tools, improved production techniques, innovative designs, digital payments, entrepreneurship and marketing.
- Basic Training: Five to seven days
- Advanced Training: Fifteen days or more
- Training stipend: ₹500 per day
Skill training seeks to improve product quality and productivity without removing the traditional character of the craft.

### Toolkit Incentive
Eligible beneficiaries receive a toolkit incentive of up to ₹15,000 to purchase modern tools relevant to their occupation.
Improved tools can:
- reduce physical effort;
- increase production speed;
- improve product quality; and
- strengthen workplace safety.

### Collateral-Free Credit Support
The scheme provides collateral-free Enterprise Development Loans of up to ₹3 lakh in two tranches.
First Tranche
- Loan amount: Up to ₹1 lakh
- Repayment period: 18 months
- Eligibility: Completion of Basic Training
Second Tranche
- Loan amount: Up to ₹2 lakh
- Repayment period: 30 months
- Eligibility: Timely repayment of the first loan and either adoption of digital transactions or completion of Advanced Training
The beneficiary pays a concessional interest rate of 5% . The government bears an interest subvention of up to 8% .

### Digital Transaction Incentive
Beneficiaries receive ₹1 for every eligible digital payment or receipt , subject to a maximum of 100 transactions per month .
The incentive encourages artisans to:
- create formal transaction records;
- reduce dependence on cash;
- improve financial transparency;
- establish a digital business history; and
- access formal credit more easily.

### Marketing Support
The scheme provides support through:
- branding and packaging;
- quality certification;
- trade fairs and exhibitions;
- advertising and publicity;
- e-commerce onboarding;
- Government e-Marketplace;
- buyer-seller linkages; and
- domestic and international market promotion.

## Formalisation Through Udyam Assist
- PM Vishwakarma beneficiaries are onboarded on the Udyam Assist Platform .
- The platform registers informal micro-enterprises and provides them with an Udyam Registration Number and certificate . This helps artisans enter the formal MSME ecosystem and access applicable government benefits.
- Formalisation can improve their access to banking services, markets, procurement opportunities and enterprise-development programmes.

### Progress Under PM Vishwakarma
As of 15 September 2026 , the scheme recorded the following progress:
- Registration target of 30 lakh beneficiaries achieved
- More than 24.37 lakh artisans completed Basic Training
- More than 18.16 lakh beneficiaries received toolkits
- Over 6.19 lakh loans , worth approximately ₹5,316 crore , sanctioned
- More than 8.11 lakh beneficiaries received digital incentives
- Over ₹42 crore disbursed as digital transaction incentives
- More than 12,000 artisans trained to use artificial intelligence tools
The AI training covers areas such as:
- product design;
- branding;
- packaging;
- marketing; and
- digital presentation of products.

## Market Access Initiatives

### Exhibitions and Outreach
The scheme has organised:
- more than 1,500 awareness programmes and camps ;
- over 50 workshops ;
- 82 trade fairs ;
- 37 State-level exhibitions ; and
- more than 50 flash-mob awareness activities .
The first PM Vishwakarma National Exhibition-cum-Trade Fair was held at Dilli Haat, New Delhi , from 17 to 31 January 2026 .
More than 117 artisans participated, recording sales of over ₹2 crore . The event attracted more than 60,000 visitors , including foreign diplomats and senior government officials.

### E-Commerce Integration
More than 30,000 beneficiaries have been onboarded on platforms such as:
- Government e-Marketplace
- Open Network for Digital Commerce
- Meesho
- Amazon
- Fabindia
- Sulekha
The Ministry of MSME has also signed an MoU with Amazon to facilitate the onboarding of eligible artisans.

### Market Support for Divyangjan Artisans
- The scheme uses the One Station One Product initiative to provide retail space to Divyangjan Vishwakarma artisans at selected high-footfall railway stations.
- More than 42 Divyangjan beneficiaries , representing different trades, have received stalls across 14 States and Union Territories .
- Artisan stalls have also been established at airports, including Bhopal and Chandigarh , under the AVSAR scheme of the Ministry of Civil Aviation.

### Branding and Packaging
- In collaboration with the Indian Institute of Packaging , workshops were organised across six cities. Around 1,500 artisans , with 250 participants from each city, were trained in packaging and branding.
- The Ministry has also collaborated with TRIFED to organise Tribal Artisan Melas and connect tribal PM Vishwakarma beneficiaries with platforms such as Tribes India .
- Products have also been displayed through stalls in selected hotels and resorts in States and Union Territories such as Bihar, Gujarat, Goa, Himachal Pradesh, Odisha and Puducherry.

## Significance of PM Vishwakarma
Recognition of Informal Workers
Traditional artisans have often remained outside formal databases and welfare systems. Certification, identity cards and Udyam registration give them an officially recognised economic identity.
Preservation of Cultural Heritage
The scheme supports the transmission of traditional skills through families and the Guru-Shishya tradition . It links cultural preservation with viable livelihood opportunities.
Financial Inclusion
Collateral-free loans reduce dependence on informal moneylenders. Concessional interest lowers the cost of enterprise development.
Modernisation Without Displacement
Skill training and toolkits introduce modern technology while allowing artisans to retain their traditional occupations and local identity.
Women and Social Empowerment
Many traditional occupations involve women and socially vulnerable communities. Training, formal credit and market access can improve their income and participation in household decisions.
Digital Inclusion
Digital transaction incentives, AI training and e-commerce onboarding connect traditional artisans with the expanding digital economy.
Local Employment
The scheme promotes self-employment and decentralised production. It can reduce distress migration by creating livelihood opportunities within local communities.

## Major Challenges
- Low digital literacy: Some artisans may find e-commerce, digital payments and AI tools difficult to use.
- Unequal market access: Artisans in remote regions may remain disconnected from transport, logistics and organised markets.
- Working-capital constraints: Toolkit support alone may not meet the recurring cost of raw materials and production.
- Design and quality gaps: Traditional products must meet changing consumer preferences without losing authenticity.
- Credit absorption: Some beneficiaries may hesitate to borrow because of uncertain market demand and repayment concerns.
- Intermediary dependence: Artisans may receive only a small portion of the final sale price.
- Limited social security: Credit and skills do not automatically provide health insurance, pensions or protection against livelihood shocks.
- Risk of standardisation: Excessive commercialisation may reduce regional diversity and weaken the cultural identity of traditional crafts.
- Monitoring quality: Achieving registration targets must be followed by assessment of income, productivity and enterprise sustainability.

## Way Forward
- Develop trade-specific artisan clusters with common production and storage facilities.
- Provide continuous mentoring after completion of formal training.
- Strengthen partnerships with design institutes, technical universities and packaging institutions.
- Improve transport, logistics and digital support in remote craft-producing regions.
- Facilitate access to working capital and suitable micro-insurance products.
- Promote direct-to-consumer marketing to reduce dependence on intermediaries.
- Use geographical indications and authenticity certification to protect traditional knowledge.
- Reserve suitable procurement opportunities for artisan products on GeM .
- Provide regional-language digital training and assisted e-commerce services.
- Link artisans with social-security schemes, pensions and health protection.
- Measure success through sustained income, loan repayment, market expansion and enterprise continuity—not merely registration numbers.

## Conclusion
PM Vishwakarma provides an integrated framework of recognition, skills, tools, credit, digital incentives and marketing support to traditional artisans. It seeks to transform hereditary skills into sustainable micro-enterprises without separating them from their cultural roots. Its long-term success will depend on continuous mentoring, fair market access, social security and protection of craft diversity. A strong artisan economy can support inclusive growth while preserving India’s living cultural heritage.

Q1. Consider the following statements regarding PM Vishwakarma:
- It is a Central Sector Scheme covering 18 traditional trades.
- It provides collateral-free Enterprise Development Loans in two tranches.
- Registration is directly approved by Common Service Centres without verification by local bodies.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b) 1 and 2 only
Statement-wise Explanation
Statement 1 is correct: PM Vishwakarma is a Central Sector Scheme covering 18 notified family-based traditional trades.
Statement 2 is correct: It provides collateral-free loans of up to ₹3 lakh—₹1 lakh in the first tranche and ₹2 lakh in the second.
Statement 3 is incorrect: Applications are submitted through CSCs, but verification and approval involve the Gram Panchayat or Urban Local Body, District Implementation Committee and Screening Committee.

## FAQs
1. When was PM Vishwakarma launched?
It was launched on 17 September 2023 .
2. How many traditional trades are covered?
The scheme covers 18 family-based traditional trades .
3. What is the maximum collateral-free loan available?
An eligible beneficiary can receive up to ₹3 lakh in two tranches.
4. What toolkit support is provided?
Eligible artisans receive a toolkit incentive of up to ₹15,000 .
5. How does the scheme promote formalisation?
Beneficiaries receive official recognition and are onboarded on the Udyam Assist Platform .
6. What are the three pillars of PM Vishwakarma?
The three pillars are Samman, Samarthya and Samriddhi .
