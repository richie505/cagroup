Andhra Pradesh–Australia MoUs: Clean Energy, Education and Skill Development Partnership

### Relevance: APPSC Clean Energy, Manufacturing, Skill Development, Innovation

## Why in News?
The Andhra Pradesh government and Australian institutions signed four MoUs covering clean energy, advanced manufacturing, inclusive education, student mental health and skill development . The agreements aim to strengthen Andhra Pradesh’s cooperation with Australia in research, innovation, education and workforce development .

## Key MoUs Signed
1. Centre of Excellence for Solar Manufacturing
The first MoU was signed among the Centre of Excellence for Energy Transition , IIT Tirupati and the University of New South Wales, Sydney .
It aims to establish a Centre of Excellence for Solar Manufacturing at IIT Tirupati .
Focus Areas
- Advanced solar photovoltaic manufacturing
- Solar panel recycling
- Circular economy practices
- Industry-driven research
- Faculty development
- Technology commercialisation
- Specialised training programmes
This will help Andhra Pradesh position itself as a hub for solar manufacturing and clean energy innovation .
Significance for Clean Energy
The proposed centre is important because solar energy is central to India’s energy transition. Support from the Australian institution will help in curriculum design, training and research in areas such as:
- solar cell manufacturing
- device physics
- reliability engineering
- recycling
- safety and maintenance
- ingot manufacturing
- wafering technologies
This can support both renewable energy manufacturing and green jobs in Andhra Pradesh.

## Inclusive Education MoU
The second MoU was signed among UNSW , Sri Padmavati Mahila Visvavidyalayam and Samagra Shiksha, Andhra Pradesh .
It aims to strengthen inclusive education by improving the capacity of teachers to educate children with:
- autism
- neurodiverse learning needs
- special learning requirements
The partnership will focus on specialised training, professional development and teaching resources .
This is significant for building a more inclusive school education system in Andhra Pradesh.

## Student Mental Health MoU
The third MoU was signed between the Andhra Pradesh State Council of Higher Education and Australia’s Rebound .
It will implement a technology-enabled pilot project in selected higher educational institutions.
Features
- Confidential mental health resources
- Wellness assessment tools
- Virtual counselling services
- Free-of-cost pilot programme
This is important because student well-being is becoming a major part of higher education governance.

## Skill Development MoU
The fourth MoU was signed between the Andhra Pradesh State Skill Development Corporation and Australia’s Dynamic Learning Services .
It aims to strengthen:
- vocational education
- workforce development
- industry-aligned training
- globally recognised professional certifications
- international employment pathways
This can help Andhra Pradesh youth access better domestic and global employment opportunities.

## Importance for Andhra Pradesh
1. Clean Energy Manufacturing Push
The solar manufacturing centre can help Andhra Pradesh move from renewable energy generation to renewable energy manufacturing.
2. Higher Education Internationalisation
Partnerships with Australian institutions can improve curriculum, research and faculty development.
3. Inclusive Human Capital Development
Teacher training for neurodiverse children supports equitable education and social inclusion.
4. Student Well-being
Mental health support in colleges can reduce stress, improve learning outcomes and strengthen student support systems.
5. Global Skill Pathways
Industry-aligned training and international certification can improve employability of Andhra Pradesh youth.
6. Investment-Friendly Image
These MoUs strengthen Andhra Pradesh’s image as a State open to global collaboration in education, research and innovation.

## Conclusion
The Andhra Pradesh–Australia MoUs show a shift towards knowledge-based cooperation in clean energy, education and skills. By combining solar manufacturing, inclusive education, student mental health and workforce development , Andhra Pradesh can strengthen its human capital and industrial base. The success of these partnerships will depend on implementation, industry linkage and measurable outcomes for students and youth.

India’s Solar Growth Enters a New Era: Policy Push for Clean Energy Transition

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper III: Renewable Energy, Energy Security, Climate Change, Infrastructure, Manufacturing

#### For Prelims:
- PM Surya Ghar | PM-KUSUM | PM Surya Sarovar Yojana | Green Energy Corridor | International Solar Alliance

#### For Mains:
- Clean energy transition | Energy security | Net-zero by 2070 | Domestic solar manufacturing | Grid integration and storage

## Why in News?
India’s solar sector has entered a new phase with the approval of the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana on 31 July 2026 . The scheme aims to develop 5,000 MW of floating solar photovoltaic projects with co-located energy storage systems, strengthening India’s clean energy transition.

## India’s Solar Rise: Capacity Expansion
- India’s solar capacity has expanded from around 3 GW in 2014 to 162.15 GW by 30 June 2026 . This rapid rise has made solar energy one of the strongest pillars of India’s renewable energy sector.
- Solar expansion has been driven by policy support, infrastructure creation, competitive bidding, rooftop solar adoption, agricultural solarisation and domestic manufacturing .

### Solar Capacity Composition
| Segment | Installed Capacity |
| Large solar projects | 118.79 GW |
| Grid-connected rooftop solar | 27.88 GW |
| Hybrid and off-grid solar | Remaining capacity |
Major solar States include Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra .

## India’s Solar Journey: Chronology
| Year | Key Development |
| 2010 | National Solar Mission launched |
| 2014 | Solar capacity around 3 GW ; Solar Parks Scheme launched |
| 2015 | Solar target revised to 100 GW |
| 2017 | Solar tariff fell to ₹2.44/unit at Bhadla |
| 2021 | Panchamrit goals announced at COP26 |
| 2024 | PM Surya Ghar launched |
| 2025 | India became the second-largest solar growth market |
| 2026 | Solar capacity reached 162.15 GW |

## Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana
The Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana is the latest major step in India’s solar journey.
Key Features
- Approved on 31 July 2026
- Supports 5,000 MW of Floating Solar Photovoltaic projects
- Includes co-located Energy Storage Systems
- Minimum storage capacity: 2 hours / 10,000 MWh
- Outlay: ₹5,070 crore
- Implementation period: FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31
- Uses reservoirs and industrial ponds
- All States and Union Territories are eligible
Significance
The scheme reduces pressure on scarce land, improves grid reliability and helps States manage peak power demand. It is based on the National Institute of Solar Energy assessment, which estimates India’s floating solar potential at about 102.18 GWp .
It is expected to reduce nearly 10 million tonnes of CO₂ emissions annually , generate 16,000–17,000 full-time equivalent jobs , and promote domestic manufacturing of floatation systems, PV cells, modules and energy storage systems .

## Major Solar Schemes and Policy Architecture
India’s solar expansion is supported by five major policy pillars.

### 1. Vision: Panchamrit and Mission LiFE
At COP26, India announced the Panchamrit goals , including 500 GW non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030 and net-zero emissions by 2070 . Mission LiFE promotes sustainable consumption and citizen participation in climate action.

### 2. Demand Creation: National Solar Mission and RPOs
The National Solar Mission created long-term policy direction for solar deployment. Renewable Purchase Obligations require obligated entities to procure a fixed share of electricity from renewable sources. The RPO target for 2026–27 is 35.95% .

### 3. Cost Reduction: Competitive Bidding
Transparent competitive bidding and e-reverse auctions reduced solar tariffs from around ₹18/unit in 2010 to ₹2.44/unit at Bhadla in 2017, making India one of the world’s lowest-cost solar markets.

### 4. Infrastructure Support
Solar parks and the Green Energy Corridor provide land, transmission and evacuation infrastructure for large-scale solar deployment.

### 5 . Manufacturing Support
The PLI Scheme for High-Efficiency Solar PV Modules and ALMM strengthen domestic manufacturing and quality assurance.

## Solar Parks, Green Energy Corridor and Infrastructure Support
- The Solar Parks Scheme was launched in December 2014 with an initial target of 20 GW , later enhanced to 40 GW .
- As of February 2026, 54 solar parks with sanctioned capacity of 39,188 MW have been approved.
Examples
- Bhadla Solar Park, Rajasthan — 2,245 MW , one of the world’s largest solar complexes
- Pavagada Solar Park, Karnataka
- Rewa Ultra Mega Solar Park, Madhya Pradesh
Andhra Pradesh
Andhra Pradesh has 5 solar parks with sanctioned capacity of 4,200 MW , making it an important solar State.
Green Energy Corridor
The Green Energy Corridor Programme supports transmission of renewable energy from generation centres to demand centres. It has enabled evacuation of 24,567.8 MW of renewable energy and is planned to support evacuation of around 44 GW across ten States.

## PLI, ALMM and Domestic Solar Manufacturing

### Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Solar PV
The PLI Scheme for High-Efficiency Solar PV Modules aims to promote domestic manufacturing and reduce import dependence.
Key Facts
- Total outlay: ₹24,000 crore
- Tranche I: ₹4,500 crore , approved in 2021
- Tranche II: ₹19,500 crore , approved in 2022
- Letters of Award issued for 48,337 MW manufacturing capacity
- Incentives linked to sales, efficiency and local content
As of September 2025, the scheme had attracted around ₹52,900 crore investment and generated about 44,400 jobs .

## Approved List of Models and Manufacturers
- ALMM , introduced in 2019 , is a quality assurance framework for solar PV modules and cells. It ensures that only approved models and manufacturers are used in eligible solar projects.
- Together, PLI and ALMM strengthen India’s domestic solar ecosystem, reduce import dependence and improve quality confidence for developers and consumers.

## Solar for Households and Farmers

### PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana
Launched on 13 February 2024 , PM Surya Ghar is the world’s largest domestic rooftop solar programme.
Key Features
- Outlay: ₹75,021 crore
- Target: one crore households
- Up to 300 units of free electricity per month
- Subsidy support for rooftop solar
- Collateral-free loans
- Dedicated national portal for registration, installation and subsidy disbursement

### PM-KUSUM Scheme
The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan , launched in 2019 , promotes solar energy use in agriculture.
Components
- Component A: Decentralised solar power plants on barren or fallow land
- Component B: Standalone solar agriculture pumps
- Component C: Solarisation of grid-connected agricultural pumps
Key Achievements
- Nearly 11.49 lakh off-grid solar pumps installed
- More than 15.89 lakh agricultural pumps covered through feeder-level solarisation
- Around 1,726 MW decentralised solar capacity commissioned on farmers’ land
- Over 21.77 lakh farmers benefited
PM-KUSUM provides reliable daytime power, reduces diesel dependence and allows farmers to earn by selling surplus power where eligible.

### Distributed Solar Growth
India’s solar transition is not limited to large solar parks. Rooftop solar and agricultural solarisation are becoming important.
In FY 2025–26 , distributed solar solutions contributed 16.3 GW , or 36% of the 44.61 GW solar capacity added during the year.
Of this:
- 7.6 GW came from PM-KUSUM
- 8.7 GW came from rooftop solar
This shows that households and farmers are becoming active energy producers.

## India’s Global Solar Leadership

### International Solar Alliance
The International Solar Alliance was co-founded by India and France at COP21 in Paris in 2015 . It promotes solar deployment, finance, technology sharing and capacity-building among member countries.

### 8th ISA Assembly
The 8th Assembly of ISA was held in New Delhi in October 2025 . It brought together ministers and delegates from more than 125 countries and focused on solar finance, resilient supply chains, inclusive access and technology cooperation.

### One Sun One World One Grid
The One Sun One World One Grid initiative was proposed by India in 2018 . It aims to create an interconnected global renewable energy grid. At COP26, India and the United Kingdom launched the Green Grids Initiative–OSOWOG to promote cross-border renewable energy trade.
India also helped build consensus during its G20 Presidency on tripling global renewable energy capacity by 2030.

## Significance of India’s Solar Push
1. Energy Security
Solar power reduces dependence on imported fossil fuels and strengthens domestic energy availability.
2. Climate Action
Solar expansion supports India’s NDCs, Panchamrit commitments and net-zero target .
3. Manufacturing and Jobs
PLI, ALMM and PM Surya Sarovar promote domestic manufacturing, value addition and clean energy employment.
4. Rural Transformation
PM-KUSUM supports farmers through reliable power, reduced diesel use and additional income opportunities.
5. Household Savings
PM Surya Ghar reduces electricity bills and encourages rooftop solar adoption.
6. Global Leadership
ISA and OSOWOG strengthen India’s role in global clean energy diplomacy.

## Challenges
1. Grid Integration
Large solar capacity requires stronger transmission, storage and balancing systems.
2. Storage Cost
Solar is intermittent, so affordable batteries and energy storage systems are essential.
3. Land Pressure
Ground-mounted solar projects need large land parcels. Floating solar can help reduce this pressure.
4. Import Dependence
India must strengthen domestic production of wafers, cells, modules and storage technologies.
5. DISCOM Readiness
Rooftop solar and RPO implementation require strong cooperation from distribution companies.

## Way Forward
1. Scale Floating Solar with Storage
PM Surya Sarovar should be implemented effectively to reduce land pressure and improve grid reliability.
2. Strengthen Green Energy Corridors
Transmission networks must grow in line with renewable energy capacity.
3. Deepen Domestic Manufacturing
PLI and ALMM should be used to build a complete solar value chain.
4. Expand Rooftop Solar Access
Subsidies, loans and digital portals must remain simple and citizen-friendly.
5. Solarise Agriculture Sustainably
PM-KUSUM should be linked with efficient irrigation, feeder management and farmer income.
6. Promote Global Solar Cooperation
India should use ISA and OSOWOG to expand solar finance, technology transfer and Global South cooperation.

## Conclusion
India’s solar growth shows how coordinated policy can transform an emerging technology into a mainstream energy source. From solar parks and rooftop systems to floating solar, agricultural pumps, domestic manufacturing and global alliances , India is building a complete solar ecosystem. The next phase must focus on storage, grid reliability, local manufacturing and inclusive access , making solar power central to India’s clean energy transition.

Government School Closures in India: Rationalisation, Exclusion and the Right to Education

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Education, Governance, Social Justice, Welfare of Vulnerable Sections

#### For Prelims:
- RTE Act | Neighbourhood School | UDISE+ | NITI Aayog | School Management Committee

#### For Mains:
- School rationalisation | Equity in education | Public education access | Gendered dropout risk | Centre-State accountability

## Why in News?
A recent NITI Aayog report stated that around 94,000 government schools were closed over the past decade, averaging nearly 25 schools a day . This has contributed to a decline of 2.26 crore students in government school enrolment. The share of government schools in total enrolment has fallen from 71% in 2005 to 49.24% in 2024-25 .

## Why are Government Schools Shutting Down?
- Government schools are being closed or merged across States mainly in the name of school rationalisation .
- School rationalisation generally means merging schools with low enrolment and shifting students to another nearby school. The National Education Policy 2020 supports rationalisation, but it also cautions that the idea of neighbourhood schools should not be endangered.
- The problem is that these closures cannot be explained only through demographic change, migration or urbanisation. In several States, closures appear as coordinated administrative decisions affecting children who have the least capacity to travel longer distances or shift to private schools.

## National Trend in Government School Closures
Government data tabled in the Lok Sabha shows a clear decline in public schooling infrastructure.
| Indicator | Earlier Position | Later Position |
| Government schools nationally | 11.07 lakh in 2014-15 | 10.17 lakh in 2023-24 |
| Approximate decline |  | Nearly 89,000 schools |
| Private schools nationally | 2.88 lakh | 3.31 lakh |
| Government school enrolment share | 71% in 2005 | 49.24% in 2024-25 |
This indicates that while government schools declined, private schools expanded during the same period.

## Worst-Affected States

### 1. Uttar Pradesh
Uttar Pradesh witnessed one of the sharpest declines. Government schools fell from around 1.63 lakh to 1.38 lakh in a single year, a drop of more than 25,000 schools .

### 2. Madhya Pradesh
Madhya Pradesh’s government schools fell from around 1.22 lakh in 2018-19 to 99,411 the next year, a decline of nearly 22,600 schools in twelve months.
The State’s own School Education Department found serious exclusion indicators:
- 7.37 lakh children had no school records.
- 11.38 lakh children were in the “Drop Box” category, meaning at imminent risk of dropping out.
- 1.23 lakh children were already recorded as dropouts.
- 1.55 lakh children had left school due to family displacement or migration.
- More than 3,500 government schools recorded zero new admissions.
- Another 6,500 schools recorded fewer than 10 admissions.
- In Jhabua and Shivpuri , around 29,000 children were in the Drop Box category.
This shows that school closure is closely linked with invisibilisation of children from the education system.

### 3. Jammu and Kashmir
In Jammu and Kashmir, more than 4,300 schools were closed or merged under rationalisation. UDISE+ figures show the State’s school count falling from 23,167 in 2020-21 to 18,785 by 2022-23 .
Reports also noted nearly 13,000 vacant teaching posts . In several areas, children now travel 4–6 km daily to reach a functioning school, weakening the neighbourhood school mandate under the Right to Education Act .

## Impact on Children’s Access to Education
Government schools are not used equally by all social classes. They are disproportionately used by children from poor households, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, minorities, backward communities and rural families .
When a nearby school shuts down, children face:
- longer travel distance
- higher transport cost
- reduced safety
- greater dropout risk
- loss of learning continuity
- dependence on unaffordable private schools
- weaker community monitoring of schools
Thus, rationalisation may appear efficient on paper, but on the ground it can reduce real access to education.

## Who Bears the Biggest Burden?

### 1. Poor Rural Children
Poor families often cannot afford transport or private schooling. For them, the nearest government school is not just one option; it is often the only accessible option.

### 2. Girls
Girls are usually the first to be withdrawn when the school is no longer nearby. Distance increases concerns around safety, mobility and household responsibilities.

### 3. SC, ST and Minority Children
A 2019 National Sample Survey-based analysis found that access to free education declined unevenly, with Muslim, Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe households disproportionately marginalised from nearby schools.

### 4. Migrant and Displaced Families
Children from families migrating for work are at higher risk of being excluded from school records and becoming dropouts.

### 5. Children in Multi-Grade Classrooms
In several merged or under-resourced schools, a single teacher may have to handle multiple classes together. This affects learning quality and violates the spirit of RTE staffing and quality norms .

## Centre-State Responsibility
- The Union government has argued that education is on the Concurrent List , and therefore States are responsible for school closures.However, this does not fully absolve the Centre.
- The Right to Education Act is a Central law, and its mandate on free, compulsory and neighbourhood schooling is a national responsibility. The Centre also influences State school education through Central funding, NEP-linked conditions and national policy frameworks.
- both Centre and States are responsible for ensuring that rationalisation does not weaken children’s access to public education.

## Why the RTE Act Has Fallen Short
The Right to Education Act, 2009 , in force since 2010 , guarantees free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years .
However, several limitations remain.

### 1. Weak Protection of Neighbourhood Schools
The Act recognises neighbourhood schooling, but large-scale school mergers have weakened this principle.

### 2. Limited Focus on Quality
The RTE creates access-based entitlements, but it does not sufficiently guarantee the quality of education inside classrooms.

### 3. Weak Enforcement Capacity
Children cannot assert their own legal rights. Poor and rural parents often lack the legal and institutional capacity to challenge school closures.

### 4. School Management Committees Remain Weak
SMCs were meant to give parents and communities a formal voice, but they are often inactive or not regularly convened.

### 5. Power Imbalance
The Act assumes that families can hold the State accountable, but poor households often lack the power to do so.

### 6. Staffing and Learning Gaps
Teacher vacancies and multi-grade teaching weaken the quality of education, even where schools formally remain open.

## Way Forward
1. Protect the Neighbourhood School Principal
Rationalisation should not be allowed if it forces young children to travel long distances.
2. Social Impact Assessment Before Closure
Before closing or merging a school, States should assess impact on girls, SC/ST children, minorities, children with disabilities and migrant families .
3. Strengthen School Mapping
Children outside school records must be identified through UDISE+, local surveys and door-to-door campaigns.
4. Ensure Safe Transport Where Mergers are Unavoidable
If a school is merged, free and safe transport must be guaranteed, especially for girls and younger children.
5. Fill Teacher Vacancies
School access without teachers cannot fulfil the RTE promise.
6. Revive School Management Committees
SMCs should be regularly convened and consulted before any school closure or merger.
7. Centre-State Monitoring Mechanism
The Centre should monitor whether State-level rationalisation violates RTE norms.
8. Treat Public Schools as Social Infrastructure
Government schools should not be judged only by enrolment numbers. Their role in social justice, gender equality and rural access must be recognised.

## Conclusion
Government school closures show that administrative rationalisation can become educational exclusion if not carefully planned. A school that appears “unviable” in official records may be the only accessible institution for a poor child, a girl, or a tribal household. India must ensure that rationalisation does not weaken the constitutional promise of education. The future of public education depends on protecting neighbourhood schools, teacher availability, social equity and accountable governance .

## UPSC PYQ
Q. Which of the following provisions of the Constitution of India have a bearing on Education?
- Directive Principles of State Policy
- Rural and Urban Local Bodies
- Fifth Schedule
- Sixth Schedule
- Seventh Schedule
Select the correct answer:
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 3, 4 and 5 only
(c) 1, 2 and 5 only
(d) 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5
Answer
(d) 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5
Explanation
1. Directive Principles of State Policy — Correct
DPSPs contain provisions related to education, especially Article 41 , Article 45 and Article 46 .
2. Rural and Urban Local Bodies — Correct
The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments gave local bodies roles in areas including education , especially through the 11th and 12th Schedules .
3. Fifth Schedule — Correct
The Fifth Schedule deals with administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes. Education of tribal communities is an important governance concern in these areas.
4. Sixth Schedule — Correct
The Sixth Schedule gives autonomous councils in tribal areas of the Northeast powers over subjects including education.
5. Seventh Schedule — Correct
Education is placed in the Concurrent List under the Seventh Schedule, allowing both the Centre and States to legislate on education.
