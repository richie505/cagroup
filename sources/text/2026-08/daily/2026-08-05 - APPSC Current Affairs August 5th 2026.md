Rare Pangolin Spotted in Kamalapuram: Wildlife Rescue, Habitat Protection and Conservation Concerns

### Relevance: APPSC: Andhra Pradesh Environment, Forest Administration, Wildlife Rescue, Biodiversity

## Why in News?
A rare pangolin , locally known as “alugu,” was spotted in the Kamalapuram municipal area of Kadapa district, Andhra Pradesh . The animal was safely rescued by forest officials and will be released into a suitable forest area in the Mydukur range after necessary checks.

## About Pangolins
- Pangolins are rare, shy and mostly nocturnal mammals .
- They are locally known as “alugu” in parts of Andhra Pradesh.
- Their body is covered with hard protective scales .
- When threatened, pangolins usually curl into a ball and raise their scales.
- They have a long sticky tongue used to catch ants and termites.
- Pangolins are generally harmless and do not attack humans.
- They are mammals because they give birth to young ones and nurse them with milk .
- They play an important role in maintaining ecological balance .

## Species of Pangolins
There are 8 species of pangolins in the world.
African Pangolins
- Black-bellied Pangolin
- White-bellied Pangolin
- Giant Ground Pangolin
- Temminck’s Ground Pangolin
Asian Pangolins
- Indian Pangolin
- Chinese Pangolin
- Sunda Pangolin
- Philippine Pangolin
The pangolin rescued in Kamalapuram is associated with the Indian range and is referred to as a rare Indian pangolin in the report.

## Habitat and Distribution
- Pangolins live in tropical forests, grasslands and agricultural areas .
- They may also be found near human settlements , especially when habitats are disturbed.
- The Indian Pangolin is found across India, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh .
- In the Kamalapuram case, forest officials suspected that the pangolin may have moved from nearby forest regions, possibly through the Kundu River basin .

## Diet and Ecological Role
- Pangolins mainly feed on ants and termites .
- They help in natural pest control by reducing insect populations.
- Their digging activity helps in soil aeration .
- By controlling insects and improving soil health, pangolins support ecosystem balance .
- Their presence indicates the importance of conserving forest, grassland and riverine habitats.

## Conservation Status
| Category | Status |
| Indian Pangolin | Endangered on the IUCN Red List |
| Chinese Pangolin | Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List |
| Indian and Chinese Pangolin in India | Protected under Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 |
| All 8 Pangolin Species | Listed under CITES Appendix I |
CITES Appendix I prohibits international commercial trade in pangolins and their parts.

## Conclusion
The rare pangolin sighting in Kamalapuram is a reminder of the rich but vulnerable biodiversity around human settlements. Pangolins are harmless, shy and ecologically valuable mammals, but they face serious threats from habitat loss and illegal wildlife trade. Their protection requires strong law enforcement, habitat conservation and community awareness. The safe rescue of the Kamalapuram pangolin shows that local people and forest officials can together play a key role in wildlife conservation.

GI Tags: Scaling Traditional Wealth into Global Brands

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Intellectual Property Rights, WTO-TRIPS, Government Policies

## Why in News?
The Government of India has highlighted the growing role of Geographical Indication (GI) tags in protecting India’s cultural heritage and converting traditional products into global brands . GI tags are being promoted as tools of rural development, export promotion, tourism integration, intellectual property protection and Aatmanirbhar Bharat .

## What is a GI Tag?
- A Geographical Indication tag is a form of intellectual property right given to products that originate from a specific geographical region and have qualities, reputation or characteristics linked to that place.
- For example, a Banarasi saree is not merely a textile product. It represents the weaving tradition, zari work and inherited craftsmanship of Banaras. A GI tag certifies that the saree is genuinely made in Banaras using traditional methods, thereby protecting both the product and the weaver’s skill.

## Why GI Tags Matter for India?

### 1. Protection of Traditional Knowledge
GI tags protect regional products from imitation, misuse and unauthorised commercialisation . This is important for crafts and agricultural products that depend on inherited local knowledge.

### 2. Better Income for Producers
By certifying origin and authenticity, GI tags improve the market value of products. As per the Ministry of Textiles, GI recognition can raise rural artisans’ incomes by 20–30% .

### 3. Consumer Trust
GI tags act as a seal of authenticity . They help consumers distinguish genuine products from fake or mass-produced imitations.

### 4. Premium Market Access
GI products can access high-value domestic and international markets because consumers increasingly value authenticity, tradition and craftsmanship .

### 5. Cultural Preservation
GI recognition helps preserve local traditions, indigenous craftsmanship and regional identity for future generations.

### 6. Rural Development
GI tags support artisans, farmers, weavers and producer groups by improving visibility, bargaining power and access to premium markets.

## Legal and Institutional Framework

### International Framework
Geographical Indications are recognised internationally under:
- Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property
- WTO Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights — TRIPS

### Indian Framework
India protects GI products through the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 .

### GI Rules and Timeline
- GI Rules were notified in 2002 .
- The legal framework became operational in 2003 .
- GI registrations started in 2004–05 .
- The GI Rules were amended in 2025 .

### GI Registry
The registration and protection of GI products are overseen by the Geographical Indications Registry, Chennai , which has all-India jurisdiction .

### 2025 Amendment
The 2025 amendment reduced the fee for GI applications and related processes by 80% . The renewal fee was reduced from ₹3,000 to ₹500 .

## Registration, Validity and Revocation
Who Can Apply?
The following can apply for GI registration:
- Association of producers
- Organisation
- Authority established by law
The applicant must be a legal entity and must represent the interests of the producers of the concerned product.

## What Cannot Be Registered as GI?
A GI cannot be registered if it:
- is a generic product name no longer protected in its country of origin;
- misleads consumers about geographical origin;
- offends religious sentiments;
- contains scandalous, offensive or obscene matter;
- violates any law in force;
- is not eligible for legal protection.

## Validity and Renewal
- A GI registration is valid for 10 years .
- It can be renewed from time to time.

## Can a GI Tag be Revoked?
Under Section 27 of the GI Act , a GI registration may be revoked, cancelled or varied.
Grounds for Revocation
A GI may be cancelled if:
- it was obtained through fraud or misrepresentation;
- it is no longer protected in its country of origin;
- it has fallen into disuse;
- its use is likely to deceive consumers;
- it violates any law;
- it contains scandalous or obscene matter.

## GI Tags as Rural Economic Assets

### 1. Banarasi Saree
GI certification helps genuine Banarasi sarees gain recognition, prevents imitation and improves returns for weavers.

### 2. Channapatna Toys
Channapatna toys received GI recognition in 2006 . The tag applies only to wooden toys made in Karnataka’s Channapatna region using traditional lacquerware art.

### 3. Muzaffarnagar Jaggery
The GI tag strengthened the identity of Muzaffarnagar Jaggery as a premium product. In 2025 , Brijnandan Agro Farmer Producer Company exported 30 metric tonnes of jaggery to Bangladesh. This improved export opportunities and income prospects for its 545 members .

### 2. One Station One Product

### 3. GI Trails and Tourism Integration
GI clusters are being integrated into tourism circuits. This promotes experiential tourism and direct rural retail.

Under the IPR component of the MSME Innovative Scheme , eligible applicants can get reimbursement for GI registration.

## Challenges in Scaling GI Products

### 1. Low Awareness Among Producers
Many artisans and farmers are unaware of GI registration, authorised user status and legal benefits.

### 2. Imitation and Misuse
Fake GI-like products reduce consumer trust and hurt genuine producers.

### 3. Weak Branding and Packaging
Many GI products lack modern packaging, digital marketing and brand identity.

### 4. Limited Authorised Users
Even genuine producers may not be formally registered as authorised users, reducing direct benefits.

### 5. Quality Standardisation
Export markets require consistent quality, traceability and certification.

### 6. Market Access Gaps
GI recognition alone cannot increase income unless linked with retail platforms, e-commerce, tourism and exports .

## Way Forward

### 1. Expand GI Registration
India should identify more traditional, tribal, agricultural and craft-based products for GI registration.

### 2. Increase Authorised Users
More genuine producers must be registered as authorised users so that benefits reach local communities.

### 3. Strengthen Enforcement
Strict action is needed against fake branding, imitation and unauthorised commercial use.

### 4. Improve Branding and Packaging
GI products need better design, packaging, storytelling, quality certification and digital promotion.

### 5. Link GI with Exports
APEDA, MSME Ministry and State agencies should connect GI products with global buyers.

### 6. Promote GI Tourism
GI Trails can convert craft clusters and agricultural regions into tourism and rural retail hubs.

### 7. Use FTAs Strategically
India should secure GI protection in trade agreements, especially with the EU, New Zealand and other premium markets .

## Conclusion
GI tags are not merely legal certificates; they are instruments of heritage protection, rural development, cultural branding and export-led growth . By protecting regional identity and empowering artisans, farmers and producer groups, GI tags can transform traditional products into global brands. With a target of 10,000 GI registrations by 2030 , India has an opportunity to build a strong heritage economy that connects tradition with markets, culture with livelihoods and local products with global value chains .

## Why in News?
The Environmental Performance Index 2026 was recently released. India ranked 176th out of 177 countries , while Estonia topped the index. The report has again brought attention to India’s environmental performance, climate governance, sustainability indicators and the debate over global environmental rankings.

## What is the Environmental Performance Index?
The Environmental Performance Index is a biennial global index that ranks countries based on their environmental performance.
It acts as a data-driven scorecard to assess how well countries are performing in areas such as:
- air quality
- water quality
- biodiversity
- sustainable forestry
- greenhouse gas reduction
- climate change action
- ecosystem vitality
- environmental public health
The index helps policymakers track sustainability progress, identify ecological vulnerabilities and improve environmental policy.

### Institutions Behind EPI
The EPI is developed by:
- Yale Center for Environmental Law & Policy
- Columbia University’s Center for International Earth Science Information Network
- Yale Center for Geospatial Solutions
- Supported by the McCall MacBain Foundation

## Methodology of EPI 2026
The EPI 2026 assessed:
- 177 countries

- 12 issue categories
- 3 broad policy objectives

## Significance for India
1. Environmental Governance Alert
India’s low EPI rank highlights the need to strengthen environmental regulation, pollution control and ecological conservation.
2. Climate Vulnerability Concern
India’s CRI ranking shows that the country remains highly vulnerable to floods, cyclones, heatwaves and extreme rainfall events.
3. Development-Environment Balance
India must pursue growth while reducing pollution, protecting forests and improving resource efficiency.
4. Global Climate Negotiations
These indices influence global perceptions of India’s environmental performance and climate commitments.
5. Policy Course Correction
They help identify areas needing improvement, such as air quality, biodiversity protection, waste management and emissions reduction.

## Concerns and Limitations
1. Methodological Disputes
India has questioned EPI methodology, especially the use of assumptions and extrapolated indicators.
2. Wealth Bias
EPI scores are strongly correlated with country wealth, which may disadvantage developing countries with large populations and developmental needs.
3. Different Indices, Different Outcomes
India ranks poorly in EPI but performs relatively better in CCPI. This shows that indices measure different aspects of sustainability.
4. Data Quality Issues
Environmental data collection and reporting vary across countries, affecting comparability.
5. Limited Reflection of Equity
Indices may not fully capture principles such as Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and historical emissions.

## Way Forward
1. Improve Air and Water Quality
India must strengthen pollution control, urban planning, clean fuel transition and river conservation.
2. Protect Biodiversity and Forests
Tree cover loss in key biodiversity areas must be reduced through stronger conservation and monitoring.
3. Accelerate Clean Energy Transition
Renewable energy, green hydrogen, electric mobility and energy efficiency should be scaled up.
4. Improve Climate Adaptation
India needs better disaster preparedness, climate-resilient infrastructure and early warning systems.
5. Strengthen Environmental Data Systems
Transparent, scientific and real-time environmental data can improve both policy and global reporting.
6. Engage with Global Index Makers
India should constructively engage with global ranking institutions to ensure fair methodology and accurate data.
7. Promote Sustainable Development
Environmental policy must integrate livelihood security, energy access and ecological protection.

## Conclusion
The Environmental Performance Index 2026 places India near the bottom, raising concerns about environmental quality and sustainability. However, India’s performance must be understood with methodological debates, development challenges and climate equity concerns. The larger lesson is clear: India needs stronger environmental governance, credible data systems, faster clean energy transition and climate-resilient development. Global rankings should not be treated as final judgments, but as tools for policy reflection and improvement.
