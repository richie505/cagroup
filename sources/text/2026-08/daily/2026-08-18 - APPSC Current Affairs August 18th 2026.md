Amaravati Educational Academy: Strengthening Social Infrastructure in the Capital Region

### Relevance: Amaravati Capital Region, Education Infrastructure, Social Infrastructure, Andhra Pradesh Development

#### For Prelims:

## Why in News?
The foundation has been laid for a modern educational and cultural academy at Abburaajupalem in the Amaravati capital region . The project is estimated to cost ₹25 crore and is expected to strengthen the educational and social infrastructure of the developing capital city.

## About the Educational Academy
- The proposed academy will come up on a three-acre site near the E3–N14 Junction in Abburaajupalem, Amaravati.
- The school building is expected to have a built-up area of nearly one lakh square feet . Once fully developed, the institution is expected to accommodate around 2,500 students .
- The academy is planned as a modern educational institution offering education up to Grade 12 in the future.

## Key Features of the Project

### 1. Affordable Quality Education
The academy aims to provide quality education at affordable fees , making it relevant for families in and around the Amaravati capital region.

### 2. Phased Academic Expansion
At present, the academy is conducting classes from nursery to Grade 5 . Higher classes will be introduced in phases from the coming academic years.

### 3. Modern Campus Infrastructure
The proposed campus will include modern educational facilities for students, with a large built-up area and planned capacity for future expansion.

### 4. Cultural and Educational Focus
The project is described as an educational and cultural academy, indicating that it may support both formal schooling and broader cultural development.

### 5. Capital Region Location
Its location in Amaravati is significant because the area is witnessing residential, institutional and infrastructure development.

## Role of Community Participation
- The project is being developed under the DAV Amaravati Rythu Vidyalaya Vedantam Group .
- It has been formed with the involvement of local farmers and residents of Thullur Mandal . AECA and other donors are also expected to contribute to the construction of the new campus.
- This community-based model is important because it reflects local participation in building educational infrastructure. It also shows how residents, farmers and donors can contribute to long-term social development in a new capital region.

## Conclusion
The proposed ₹25-crore educational academy at Abburaajupalem is an important addition to Amaravati’s growing social infrastructure. With a planned capacity of 2,500 students , affordable education goals and community participation, the project can support balanced development in the capital region. Its success will depend on timely execution, quality teaching, affordability and inclusive access for local communities.

Forest Fires in India: Himalayan Vulnerability, Monitoring Gaps and Disaster Preparedness

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper III: Disaster Management, Environment, Climate Change, Forest Conservation

#### For Prelims:

## Why in News?
A Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment, Forest and Climate Change has flagged major gaps in forest fire monitoring and management in the Himalayan region.
The committee recommended the launch of a geostationary satellite for round-the-clock forest fire monitoring, regional climate-fire studies, dedicated NDRF response centres for Himalayan forest fires, and a National Pine-Needle Utilisation Policy .

## What are Forest Fires?
- The National Institute of Disaster Management defines a forest fire as an unclosed and freely spreading fire that consumes natural fuels such as vegetation, litter and organic matter.
- When such fires burn completely out of control, they are called wildfires .
- Forest fires may be natural or human-induced. While low-intensity fires may help nutrient cycling in some ecosystems, uncontrolled and high-intensity fires can cause serious ecological, economic and health damage.

## Types of Forest Fires
1. Ground Fires
These fires burn organic matter present in the soil. They usually spread slowly beneath the vegetation layer.
2. Surface Fires
These are caused by the burning of dry leaves, branches and ground-level vegetation. They spread quickly and are commonly seen in India.
3. Crown Fires
These fires spread from one tree top to another. They generate intense heat and large flames. Crown fires are comparatively rare in India.

## Forest Fire Vulnerability in India
India’s total forest and tree cover is around 8.27 lakh sq km , nearly 24% of the country’s geographical area.
According to the Forest Survey of India:
- about 54.40% of forest area is vulnerable to occasional fires
- 7.49% faces moderately frequent fires
- 2.40% experiences high fire incidence
- around 35.71% is largely unaffected
The forest fire season in India generally lasts from November to June , with higher incidents in March, April and May due to dry biomass, rising temperatures and summer conditions.

## Causes of Forest Fires
Most forest fires in India are believed to be human-induced .
Major causes include:
- burning forests for fresh grass growth
- covering up illegal tree cutting
- poaching-related fires
- shifting cultivation practices
- Mohua leaf and tendu patta collection
- accumulation of dry pine needles
- sparks from electricity cables
- lightning
- rising temperatures and prolonged dry spells
Setting a forest on fire is a punishable offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 .

## Why Himalayan Forest Fires Need Special Attention
The Himalayan region is highly vulnerable because of steep slopes, dry biomass, fragile ecosystems and climate variability.
The parliamentary committee noted that the Western and Eastern Himalayas together make up around 17% of India’s landmass , but account for nearly 57% of India’s forest fire incidents .
In the Western Himalayas , fires are driven mainly by:
- extreme pre-monsoon heat
- pine-needle fuel load
- human ignition near settlements
In the Eastern Himalayas , fires are linked to atmospheric dryness and cultivation-related burning practices.

### Why Pine Trees are Fire-Prone
Chir pine trees shed dry needles locally known as Pirul . These needles contain resin and become highly inflammable during summer. Once accumulated on the forest floor, even a small spark can trigger a large fire.

## Monitoring and Management in India

### Forest Survey of India
The Forest Survey of India monitors forest fires and sends satellite-based alerts to State Forest Departments and registered users.
At present, India relies on foreign polar-orbiting satellites such as AQUA and Suomi-NPP . These satellites observe India only a limited number of times in a day, creating monitoring gaps.

### Role of States and Centre
Forest fire prevention and management is primarily the responsibility of State Forest Departments , as forests are managed by States.
The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change is the nodal ministry for disaster management related to forest fire, water pollution and hazardous substances.
Financial and institutional support is provided through:
- Forest Fire Prevention and Management Scheme
- Development of Wildlife Habitats
- Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority

## Methods Used to Prevent and Control Forest Fires
Important methods include:
- construction of watch towers
- early detection systems
- fire watchers
- water conservation structures
- local community involvement
- fire lines
- satellite-based monitoring
- GIS and remote sensing tools

### Fire Lines
- Kachha fire lines reduce fuel load by removing undergrowth and shrubs while retaining trees.
- Pucca fire lines are clear-felled strips that separate forest blocks and help stop fire spread.

## Parliamentary Committee Recommendations
The committee made several important recommendations for better fire management.

### 1. Geostationary Satellite
India should prioritise a satellite that can provide continuous forest fire monitoring, especially in vulnerable regions.

### 2. Climate-Fire Study
A region-specific study should examine the empirical link between climate change and fire frequency by analysing temperature, rainfall and dry spells.

### 3. NDRF Response Centres
At least two regional NDRF response centres should be set up for forest fires in the Himalayan region.

### 4. Pine-Needle Utilisation Policy
A National Pine-Needle Utilisation Policy should identify collection zones and provide fiscal and market incentives for pine needle use.

### 5. Better Preparedness
The committee emphasised stronger regulation, monitoring and mitigation systems for Himalayan forest fires.

## Impact of Forest Fires

### 1. Climate Impact
Intense fires release carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, methane, nitrous oxide and other gases, contributing to global warming.

### 2. Carbon Flipping
Forests can shift from being carbon sinks to carbon sources when stored carbon is released through repeated fires.

### 3. Biodiversity Loss
High-intensity fires destroy vegetation, wildlife habitats, flora and fauna.

### 4. Soil Degradation
Burning of vegetation and organic matter reduces soil quality and increases erosion, especially on Himalayan slopes.

### 5. Human Health Effects
Smoke and particulate matter cause respiratory problems, eye irritation and poor air quality.

### 6. Economic Losses
Forest fires affect agriculture, tourism, infrastructure, local livelihoods and forest-based communities.

## National Action Plan on Forest Fire
The National Action Plan on Forest Fire was prepared by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
Its objectives are to:
- reduce forest fires by informing, enabling and empowering forest-fringe communities
- incentivise communities to work with State Forest Departments
- reduce vulnerability of forests to fire hazards
- improve the capacity of forest personnel and institutions
- speed up recovery after fire events
A Central Monitoring Committee has also been constituted to oversee the implementation of the plan.

## Challenges
India faces several challenges in forest fire management. Monitoring still depends on satellites that do not provide continuous coverage. Fire response capacity is uneven across States. Many fires occur in remote and difficult terrain, especially in the Himalayas.
Human-induced fires are hard to regulate because many offenders remain unidentified. Local communities may not always have incentives to prevent fires. Climate change is increasing dry spells and heat stress, while pine needle accumulation remains a serious fuel-load problem in Himalayan forests.

## Way Forward

### 1. Launch Dedicated Fire-Monitoring Satellite
A geostationary satellite can improve real-time detection and response.

### 2. Strengthen Himalayan Fire Response
Dedicated NDRF centres, trained forest teams and rapid-response units should be created.

### 3. Use Pine Needles Productively
Pine needles can be used for biomass, briquettes, composting and livelihood-linked enterprises.

### 4. Improve Community Participation
Forest-fringe communities should be incentivised for prevention, reporting and early response.

### 5. Link Climate Science with Fire Planning
Temperature, rainfall, dry spells and vegetation data should be used for fire-risk prediction.

### 6. Promote Controlled Fuel Management
Removal of dry biomass, maintenance of fire lines and controlled clearing can reduce fire intensity.

### 7. Strengthen Legal and Institutional Accountability
Illegal burning should be tracked and penalised, while State Forest Departments must improve preparedness.

## Conclusion
Forest fires are no longer only a seasonal forest-management issue; they are a climate, disaster-management and livelihood challenge. The Himalayan region requires special attention because of high fire incidence, fragile slopes, pine-needle fuel load and growing climate stress. India needs better satellite monitoring, stronger State capacity, community participation, scientific fuel management and region-specific fire response systems. A modern forest fire strategy must combine technology, ecology and local livelihoods.

Bharat Taxi: India’s First Cooperative-Based Ride-Hailing Platform

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Governance, Cooperatives, Welfare of Workers, Digital Public Infrastructure

#### For Prelims:
- Bharat Taxi | Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002 | Sahkar se Samriddhi | e-Shram Portal | Digital India

#### For Mains:
- Cooperative urban mobility | Platform economy reform | Worker ownership | Social security | Inclusive digital economy

## Why in News?
Bharat Taxi , India’s first cooperative-led ride-hailing platform, has gained attention as a new model of urban mobility that combines digital technology , driver ownership and cooperative governance .As of 25 July 2026 , Bharat Taxi had nearly 8 lakh registered Sarathis and around 41 lakh registered customers across several cities. The platform aims to expand across India by 2029 .

## About Bharat Taxi
- Bharat Taxi is India’s first cooperative-based ride-hailing platform . It was established on 6 June 2025 under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002 .
- The platform is based on the idea of “Saarthi Hi Maalik” , meaning the driver is not merely a service provider but also a stakeholder in the platform.
- It reflects the broader vision of “Sahkar se Samriddhi” , which seeks to use cooperatives for economic participation, employment generation and inclusive growth.

## Foundation and Cooperative Ownership Model
- Bharat Taxi was formed by eight national-level cooperative institutions:
- National Cooperative Development Corporation
- IFFCO
- NABARD
- KRIBHCO
- Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation / Amul
- NAFED
- National Dairy Development Board
- National Cooperative Export Limited
- The platform places drivers, called Sarathis , within a cooperative framework. Sarathis can become member-owners by subscribing to the cooperative’s share capital.
- Shares are valued at ₹100 each , and a Sarathi can obtain ownership rights by purchasing shares worth ₹500 .
- The cooperative model also provides representation to Sarathi members in the Board of Directors , ensuring that drivers’ interests are reflected in decision-making.

## Zero-Commission Financial Model
- Bharat Taxi follows a zero-commission and surge-free pricing model .
- Unlike conventional ride-hailing platforms, it does not charge drivers a commission on every ride. Instead, it follows a subscription-based cooperative model, allowing drivers to retain full fare earnings.
- Passengers pay the applicable fare based on distance, time and market conditions. The app does not charge additional convenience fees, platform fees or surge pricing.
Under the scheme:
- 20% of cooperative earnings are credited to Bharat Taxi as Sarathis’ capital
- 80% is distributed among Sarathis based on kilometres travelled
- 7% service charge is levied only at Bharat Taxi-operated airport prepaid booths
This model aims to create a fairer earnings structure for drivers while keeping fares transparent for passengers.

## Growing Footprint Across India
As of 25 July 2026 , Bharat Taxi had:
- nearly 8 lakh registered Sarathis
- around 41 lakh registered customers
The service is operational in:
- Delhi-NCR
- Gujarat
- Lucknow
- Chandigarh
- Mumbai
- Jaipur
- Kanpur
- Pune
Operations are planned in Ranchi, Patna, Guwahati, Bhopal, Kolkata, Indore and Nagpur in the coming months. The platform aims for nationwide expansion by 2029 .

## Services and Digital Features
- Bharat Taxi provides multiple ride options through its application.
Available ride categories include:
- Bike Taxi
- Auto
- Economy Cab
- Premium Sedan
- XL Cab / SUV
- Rentals
- Out-station travel
- Scheduled rides
- Passengers can book rides through the app, while drivers can register as Sarathis through the app or website by submitting required documents.
- The platform also operates prepaid booths at selected airports for direct ride booking.

## Women-Centric Mobility and Safety
- Bharat Taxi has introduced the Sarathi Didi feature to improve safety and accessibility for women commuters. It allows women passengers to choose rides operated by women drivers.
- The platform has also launched the Bike Didi initiative to encourage women’s participation in the transport sector. As of February 2026, more than 150 women drivers had joined under this initiative.
- These features support both women’s safety and women’s livelihood generation in the urban mobility sector.

### Safety and Secure Travel
Bharat Taxi includes several safety features:
- police integration
- control-room monitoring
- KYC verification of drivers
- periodic background checks
- safety training
- live ride tracking
- SOS support
- driver ratings
Safety ratings are regularly reviewed to ensure that only trained and verified drivers remain active on the platform.

## Digital Integration and Partnerships
Bharat Taxi has signed an MoU with the National e-Governance Division under the Digital India framework.
This enables integration with national digital platforms such as:
- DigiLocker
- UMANG
- API Setu
These integrations support paperless onboarding, secure digital verification, interoperable digital operations and cashless payments.
Bharat Taxi has also partnered with institutions such as Delhi Traffic Police, Delhi Metro Rail Corporation, Airport Authority, IFFCO Tokio Insurance, State Bank of India and Paytm .

## Social Security for Sarathis
Sarathis are facilitated to register on the e-Shram portal , which creates a national database of unorganised workers.
After registration, Sarathis and their families can become eligible for free medical treatment up to ₹5 lakh under Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana .
Additional welfare support includes:
- personal accident insurance of ₹5 lakh through IFFCO Tokio at nominal rates
- advisory support for long-term driver welfare
- group health insurance support through Paytm partnership
This is significant because platform workers often face income insecurity and lack of formal social protection.

## Significance of Bharat Taxi
1. Cooperative Alternative to Platform Capitalism
Bharat Taxi offers a driver-owned model in a sector generally dominated by private app-based platforms.
2. Better Earnings for Drivers
The zero-commission model allows Sarathis to retain full ride earnings, improving income security.
3. Democratic Governance
Drivers participate as cooperative members and have representation in the Board of Directors.
4. Transparent Pricing for Passengers
Surge-free pricing and advance fare estimates improve predictability and consumer trust.
5. Social Security for Gig Workers
Linkage with e-Shram, AB-PMJAY and insurance support can improve welfare of urban platform workers.
6. Women’s Participation
Sarathi Didi and Bike Didi promote safe mobility and employment opportunities for women.
7. Digital Public Infrastructure Use
Integration with DigiLocker, UMANG and API Setu shows how public digital systems can support cooperative business models.

## Challenges
Bharat Taxi’s success will depend on service reliability, technology performance, driver onboarding, passenger trust and competition with established ride-hailing platforms.
Maintaining zero-commission sustainability while ensuring app quality, customer support, safety monitoring and insurance benefits will require strong financial planning.
The platform must also ensure that cooperative governance remains transparent and participatory as it expands nationally.

## Way Forward
1. Strengthen Driver Participation
Sarathis should be actively involved in local-level governance and feedback mechanisms.
2. Ensure Reliable Service Quality
Timely rides, fair pricing, grievance redressal and passenger safety must remain central.
3. Expand Women-Led Mobility
Sarathi Didi and Bike Didi should be scaled with training, safety support and financial assistance.
4. Deepen Social Security Coverage
All registered Sarathis should be linked with e-Shram, health insurance, accident insurance and welfare schemes.
5. Improve Digital Infrastructure
App performance, payment systems, live tracking and control-room integration must be strengthened.
6. Build City-Level Partnerships
Partnerships with airports, metro systems, traffic police and urban local bodies can improve last-mile connectivity.

## Conclusion
Bharat Taxi represents an important experiment in India’s digital platform economy. By combining cooperative ownership , zero-commission earnings , digital public infrastructure , social security and inclusive mobility , it offers a possible alternative to conventional ride-hailing models. Its long-term success will depend on operational efficiency, transparent governance, passenger trust and sustained welfare support for Sarathis. If implemented effectively, Bharat Taxi can become a model for cooperative urban mobility under the vision of Sahkar se Samriddhi .
