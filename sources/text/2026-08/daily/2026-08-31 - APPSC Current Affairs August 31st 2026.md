Odia Inscription of the Gajapati Era Found at Tekkali

### Relevance: APPSC: History and cultural heritage of North Coastal Andhra Pradesh
- Prelims: Tekkali Inscription | Gajapati Dynasty | Kapilendra Deva | Pratapa Kapileswara | Odia Script | Odia Language | Estampage | Palaeography | Kalinga Heritage

## Why in News?
A centuries-old inscription associated with the Gajapati period has been traced inside the Tekkali royal palace in Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh. Engraved in the Odia script and language, it contains the name “Pratapa Kapileswara,” referring to Gajapati ruler Kapilendra Deva.The discovery provides further evidence of Tekkali’s historical connections with the Odisha kingdom.

## Location and Discovery
The stone inscription was found at:
- Place: Tekkali royal palace
- District: Srikakulam
- State: Andhra Pradesh
The inscription was traced by researchers associated with an organisation that studies and documents Kalinga heritage in South India. A member of the Tekkali royal family assisted them in locating the stone.

## Earlier Epigraphical Record
- The inscription was not entirely unknown to historical records. It had earlier been mentioned in the Annual Report on Indian Epigraphy for 1926–27 .
- Using this reference, the researchers approached the Tekkali royal family and successfully located the stone within the palace premises.
- The discovery therefore represents the physical retracing and renewed documentation of an inscription that had been recorded approximately a century earlier.

## Condition of the Inscription
The inscribed stone had been lying beneath a Tulasi Choura inside the palace.
Its condition is poor because:
- a considerable portion has been damaged;
- some parts have been lost over time;
- the first line is completely destroyed; and
- the surviving lines do not reveal the inscription’s complete purpose.
Despite the damage, researchers obtained an estampage for further examination.

## What Is an Estampage?
An estampage is an impression of an inscription taken on paper. It allows researchers to examine and document engraved letters without repeatedly handling or moving the original stone.
It is especially useful when an inscription is:
- weathered;
- partly damaged;
- difficult to move; or
- located in a vulnerable place.
Script and Language
The inscription is engraved in:
- Script: Odia
- Language: Odia
Its script and surviving words connect it with the Gajapati political and cultural tradition of Odisha.

## What Does the Inscription Contain?
The surviving inscription consists of at least four identifiable lines.
| Line | Surviving information |
| First line | Completely damaged |
| Second line | Contains part of the ruler’s name |
| Third line | Contains “Pratapa Kapileswara” |
| Fourth line | Contains the expression “Bijeraije” |
The name Pratapa Kapileswara refers to Gajapati Kapilendra Deva.
However, the damaged condition prevents researchers from establishing:
- the exact regnal year;
- the precise date;
- the complete nature of the record; and
- the purpose for which it was issued.

## Probable Date of the Inscription
Kapilendra Deva ruled Odisha during the 15th century .
Based on:
- palaeographic features; and
- the historical context,
the inscription is also likely to belong to the 15th century.
However, its exact date has not yet been confirmed and requires further scholarly investigation.

## About Gajapati Kapilendra Deva
The inscription refers to Gajapati ruler Kapilendra Deva through the name “Pratapa Kapileswara”.
According to the researchers:
- he ruled Odisha during the 15th century; and
- during his reign, the Odisha kingdom extended from the Ganga River in the north to the Kaveri River in the south .
The inscription’s presence at Tekkali reflects the historical reach of the Gajapati kingdom into parts of present-day North Coastal Andhra Pradesh.

## Historical Importance of Tekkali
- Tekkali was formerly an ancient Odia zamindari of Ganjam .
- It later became separated from Odisha during the formation of the separate State of Odisha. Nevertheless, historical and cultural connections have continued.
- A significant number of Odia families still reside in Tekkali, indicating the persistence of linguistic and cultural ties.

## Conclusion
The Tekkali inscription is an important epigraphical record of the Gajapati era. Its Odia script, Odia language and reference to “Pratapa Kapileswara” connect the region with the 15th-century rule of Kapilendra Deva.Although its exact date and purpose remain uncertain, the inscription provides valuable evidence of the political, linguistic and cultural links between Tekkali and the historical Odisha kingdom

India–Uzbekistan Relations Elevated to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership

### Relevance: GS Paper II: India’s bilateral relations and engagement with Central Asia
Prelims: Uzbekistan | Comprehensive Strategic Partnership | UPI–UZQR | Exercise Dustlik | Uranium Supply | Fayaz Tepa | Kara Tepa | Aral Sea | SCO | ITEC
Mains: Connect Central Asia Policy | Critical-Mineral Security | Civil Nuclear Cooperation | Defence Co-development | Multimodal Connectivity | Digital Public Infrastructure | Counterterrorism | Global South

## Why in News?
During the Indian Prime Minister’s State visit to Uzbekistan on August 29–30, 2026 , India and Uzbekistan elevated their relations to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership .The two countries signed 11 agreements, set a target of $5 billion in annual trade by 2030 , and agreed to establish a framework for the long-term supply of uranium from Uzbekistan to India.

## Strategic Upgrade
India and Uzbekistan marked the 15th anniversary of their strategic partnership by expanding cooperation into a wider range of strategic and developmental areas.
The Comprehensive Strategic Partnership covers:
- trade, investment and industrial cooperation;
- energy, uranium and critical minerals;
- defence, counterterrorism and cybersecurity;
- transport and logistics connectivity;
- digital technology, artificial intelligence and space; and
- agriculture, health, education, culture and tourism.
Uzbekistan’s location, resources and regional influence place it at the centre of India’s engagement with Central Asia.

## Major Outcomes
| Area | Outcome |
| Strategic relations | Comprehensive Strategic Partnership |
| Trade | $5-billion annual target by 2030 |
| Uranium | Long-term supply framework |
| Critical minerals | Joint exploration, mining and processing |
| Defence | Co-development and co-production |
| Digital payments | Indian UPI applications to scan UZQR |
| Environment | $1-million grant for the Aral Sea region |
| Heritage | Restoration of Fayaz Tepa and Kara Tepa |
| Coordination | Foreign Ministers-led mechanism |

## Institutional Mechanisms

### Foreign Ministers-led Coordination Mechanism
The two countries will establish a high-level mechanism led by their Foreign Ministers to:
- oversee the implementation of agreements;
- coordinate different areas of bilateral cooperation; and
- ensure timely completion of priority projects.

### Joint Commission
The existing joint commission will be elevated from the secretary level to the ministerial level , giving economic and technical cooperation stronger political supervision.
The two sides also agreed to expand parliamentary contacts and review existing bilateral legal instruments to reflect current priorities.

### Trade and Investment Cooperation
The current volume of bilateral trade exceeds $1 billion . India and Uzbekistan aim to raise it to $5 billion by 2030.
To achieve this objective, they will:
- diversify the range of traded goods;
- remove non-tariff and investment barriers;
- digitalise trade procedures;
- improve mutual market access; and
- encourage business-to-business partnerships.
A Joint Working Group will address non-tariff issues and investment barriers. Electrical goods, engineering products, automobiles, pharmaceuticals, jewellery and agriculture have been identified as priority sectors.
A joint feasibility study on an India–Uzbekistan Preferential Trade Agreement has been completed. The concerned ministries will examine the next steps on a time-bound basis.
Invest India and Uzbekistan’s Investment Promotion Agency will promote investment through business forums, roadshows, seminars and stakeholder meetings. India also reaffirmed support for Uzbekistan’s accession to the World Trade Organization.

### Uranium and Civil Nuclear Cooperation
India and Uzbekistan agreed to establish a framework for the long-term supply of uranium from Uzbekistan to India .
The arrangement is significant because it can:
- diversify India’s nuclear-fuel sources;
- improve the reliability of uranium supplies;
- support peaceful nuclear-energy cooperation; and
- contribute to India’s wider energy requirements.
Both countries also expressed interest in expanding cooperation in the peaceful applications of nuclear energy.

### Critical Minerals and Mining
Critical-mineral cooperation will cover the complete mineral value chain rather than being limited to the purchase of raw materials.
The two countries will explore joint projects in:
- geological exploration and mining;
- mineral processing and recycling;
- production of value-added goods; and
- development of integrated supply chains.
Indian companies may participate in developing mineral deposits in Uzbekistan, while Uzbek enterprises may collaborate on projects in India.

### Renewable Energy
- The two sides agreed to explore cooperation in renewable energy, modernisation of energy infrastructure and prospective investment projects.
- This cooperation brings together Uzbekistan’s resource potential and India’s experience in energy transition and clean-energy development.

### Defence Cooperation
India and Uzbekistan will strengthen direct engagement between their defence industries.
Areas of cooperation include:
- co-development and co-production of military hardware;
- institutional exchanges and capacity-building;
- exploration of defence-industrial projects; and
- continuation of joint military activities.
Exercise Dustlik
Dustlik is the joint military exercise between India and Uzbekistan. Its latest edition was held in Uzbekistan’s Namangan Region in April 2026.

### Security and Counterterrorism
The two countries reaffirmed zero tolerance towards terrorism, including cross-border terrorism.
They agreed to strengthen cooperation against:
- terrorism financing and safe havens;
- violent extremism and separatism;
- organised crime and illicit drug trafficking; and
- cyber threats.
Cooperation will continue through intelligence sharing, law-enforcement coordination and the Joint Working Group on Counterterrorism.

### Transport and Logistics Connectivity
India and Uzbekistan agreed to strengthen multimodal transport routes and logistics corridors to facilitate the movement of goods and reduce freight costs.
Cooperation will also cover:
- restoration of freight transportation;
- modernisation of road infrastructure;
- exchange of road-construction technology; and
- development of energy-efficient public transport.
Improved connectivity is essential for expanding India’s economic engagement with landlocked Central Asia.

### UPI–UZQR Payment Linkage
NPCI International Payments Limited and Uzbekistan’s National Interbank Processing Centre concluded a commercial agreement on digital payments.
Under the arrangement, Indian UPI applications will be able to scan Uzbekistan’s national UZQR for merchant payments.
This can:
- simplify payments for Indian visitors;
- support tourism and business travel;
- encourage cross-border digital-payment interoperability; and
- expand the global reach of India’s digital public infrastructure.

### Information Technology and Artificial Intelligence
The first IT Park in Tashkent was established in 2019 on the Indian model with support from the Software Technology Parks of India.
The two countries will now strengthen cooperation among:
- technology parks;
- companies and start-ups;
- research and educational institutions; and
- digital-innovation ecosystems.
They also agreed to exchange experience in developing safe, secure and trustworthy artificial intelligence systems. Cybersecurity cooperation will form part of the wider digital partnership.

### Space Cooperation
India and Uzbekistan will expand cooperation in the peaceful uses of outer space through:
- space applications;
- exchange of scientific data;
- joint workshops and training; and
- capacity-building.

### Agriculture and Food Security
Agricultural cooperation will focus on:
- value-chain development and agricultural exports;
- livestock and food security;
- resource-efficient technologies;
- agricultural science; and
- conservation of plant genetic resources.
Research institutions and enterprises from both countries will work together to improve productivity and promote trade in agricultural products.

### Health and Pharmaceuticals
India and Uzbekistan will strengthen cooperation in biomedical research, medical innovation and public-health systems.
The Tashkent Pharma Park will provide a platform for:
- joint pharmaceutical projects;
- localised production;
- technology transfer;
- investment; and
- skilled workforce development.
The two sides also supported the establishment of medical centres, laboratories, research institutions and joint pharmaceutical enterprises in Uzbekistan. An MoU on traditional medicine was also concluded.

### Education and Capacity-Building
Educational cooperation will include joint research, academic exchanges, India studies in Uzbekistan and Uzbek studies in India.
Cooperation will focus particularly on medicine, pharmaceuticals, chemistry, nanotechnology and biotechnology.
India’s capacity-building contribution includes:
- over 2,700 Uzbek nationals trained under the ITEC programme;
- more than 400 Uzbek students selected for ICCR scholarships, including AYUSH scholarships; and
- announcement of the Lal Bahadur Shastri Scholarship for Hindi Language in Uzbekistan.
Indian students studying in Uzbekistan also contribute to people-to-people relations.

### Cultural Exchange Programme
A Cultural Exchange Programme for 2026–2030 was signed. It covers:
- cultural festivals and exhibitions;
- artistic tours;
- institutional exchanges; and
- cooperation in archives.

### Buddhist Heritage Cooperation
A Letter of Intent was concluded for conserving and restoring:
- Fayaz Tepa ; and
- Kara Tepa .
These ancient Buddhist monasteries in southern Uzbekistan reflect the spread of Buddhism through the Silk Road and its historical presence in Central Asia.
Heritage cooperation will also include digital documentation, three-dimensional modelling of archaeological artefacts and advanced conservation technologies.

### Sports, Yoga and Tourism
- Sports cooperation will include athlete preparation and coach training in badminton, archery and chess.
- India’s Ministry of AYUSH and Uzbekistan’s Yoga Federation will cooperate in yoga-instructor training, international Yogasana competitions and the promotion of healthy lifestyles.
- Tourism can benefit from improved payments, cultural programmes, heritage conservation and stronger connectivity.

### Environmental Cooperation
India announced a $1-million grant for afforestation and ecological rehabilitation in the Aral Sea region.
The initiative connects:
- Uzbekistan’s Yashil Makon programme; and
- India’s Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam campaign.
India will also provide technical support under its Grant-in-Aid framework.

### Regional and International Cooperation
Central Asia–India Format
Both sides reaffirmed their commitment to deeper engagement through the Central Asia–India mechanism at the level of Heads of State and other institutions.
United Nations Reform
India and Uzbekistan called for reform of the United Nations, including the Security Council. Uzbekistan reaffirmed its support for India’s candidature for permanent membership of the UN Security Council.
Multilateral Platforms
The two countries agreed to strengthen cooperation through:
- the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation;
- the United Nations;
- forums representing the Global South; and
- other international and regional bodies.
Uzbekistan congratulated India on its BRICS chairship in 2026. India supported Uzbekistan’s NAM chairship for 2027–2030. Uzbekistan also expressed interest in joining the International Solar Alliance and the International Big Cat Alliance.

## Significance for India
Strategic Presence in Central Asia
A stronger partnership with Uzbekistan expands India’s political and strategic engagement in Central Asia.
Energy and Mineral Security
Long-term uranium supplies and critical-mineral projects can reduce India’s dependence on concentrated external sources.
Regional Security
Defence cooperation, Exercise Dustlik and intelligence sharing can help address terrorism, extremism and other regional threats.
Economic Opportunities
The trade target and proposed preferential trade arrangement can expand markets for Indian pharmaceuticals, engineering products, automobiles and digital services.
Digital Diplomacy
UPI–UZQR, artificial intelligence and IT-park cooperation demonstrate the growing role of Indian digital platforms in bilateral diplomacy.
Civilisational Linkages
Restoration of Buddhist sites strengthens India’s historical and cultural relationship with the Silk Road region.

## Way Forward
- Prepare a time-bound roadmap for achieving the 2030 trade target.
- Advance the next steps towards a Preferential Trade Agreement.
- Operationalise the uranium-supply framework.
- Identify commercially viable critical-mineral projects.
- Improve multimodal freight and logistics connectivity.
- Implement UPI–UZQR interoperability.
- Promote direct defence-industry partnerships.
- Regularly review progress through the Foreign Ministers-led mechanism.

## Conclusion
The Comprehensive Strategic Partnership brings trade, uranium, critical minerals, defence, connectivity, digital technology and cultural cooperation within a single framework.
Its success will depend on converting political commitments into functioning projects. Effective implementation can strengthen India’s resource security, regional influence and long-term engagement with Central Asia.

(a) Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan (b) Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan (c) Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan (d) Uzbekistan and Tajikistan

Explanation: The Aral Sea is an inland water body located between Kazakhstan to the north and Uzbekistan to the south in Central Asia. It has drastically shrunk because the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers, which fed it, were diverted mainly for irrigation.

Aranmula Uthrattathi Boat Race

### Relevance: GS Paper I – Indian Culture, Art & Heritage
Aranmula Boat Race | Uthrattathi Vallamkali | Palliyodams | Pampa River | Parthasarathy Temple | Aranmula Kannadi | GI Tag | Kerala Culture | Onam | Cultural Tourism

## Why in News?
Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan inaugurated the Aranmula Uthrattathi boat race at Aranmula in Pathanamthitta district, Kerala , describing it as a timeless symbol of Keralam’s rich cultural heritage, deep devotion and enduring traditions .

## About Aranmula Boat Race
- The Aranmula Uthrattathi boat race is a traditional boat race held on the Pampa River in Kerala.
- It is associated with the cultural and religious traditions of Aranmula , a town closely linked with the ancient Parthasarathy Temple .

## Cultural and Religious Significance
- The race reflects Kerala’s traditional community celebrations.
- It is linked with devotion, temple culture and collective participation.
- It attracts visitors from India and abroad.
- It has helped place Aranmula on the cultural and tourism map.

## Palliyodams
Palliyodams are the traditional snake boats of Kerala used in the Aranmula boat race.

### Key Features
- They are long, decorated traditional boats.
- Each boat is powered by over 100 oarsmen .
- Oarsmen row in complete unison.
- They represent physical endurance, teamwork and craftsmanship.
Link with Aranmula
- Aranmula is located in Pathanamthitta district, Kerala.
- The town is named after the ancient Parthasarathy Temple.
- The temple is said to have a heritage of nearly 1,800 years.
Aranmula Kannadi
- Aranmula is also famous as the birthplace of Aranmula Kannadi .
- It is a traditional metal mirror .
- It has received the Geographical Indication tag .
- It represents Kerala’s traditional craftsmanship.

## CARE MCQ

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d) 1, 2 and 3
Explanation: The Aranmula boat race is held on the Pampa River . It features Palliyodams , the traditional snake boats of Kerala. Aranmula is also known for the GI-tagged Aranmula Kannadi , a traditional metal mirror.
