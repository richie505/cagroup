Geospatial technologies are tools used to collect, analyse and interpret location-based data about the Earth.
They include:
- Remote sensing
- Satellite imagery
- Geographic Information System
- Digital mapping
- Spatial data analytics
These technologies help in better decision-making for governance, development planning and environmental management.

Why Inflation is Rising in India: Fuel Costs, Food Shocks and Structural Price Pressures

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper III: Indian Economy, Inflation, Monetary Policy, Agriculture, Food Security, Fiscal Policy

#### For Prelims:
- Inflation, Wholesale Price Index, Consumer Price Index, Producer Price Index for Manufacturing, Monetary Policy Committee, Fuel and Power Inflation, Food Inflation, Cost-Push Inflation, Demand-Pull Inflation, Supply Shock, El Niño, Drought, Indirect Taxes

#### For Mains:
- Structural inflation, cost-push pressure, food supply shock, imported inflation, oil price transmission, inflation targeting, countercyclical tax policy, irrigation infrastructure, price stability, real income erosion, income redistribution

## Why in News?
India has recently witnessed a sharp rise in inflation, with Wholesale Price Index inflation hovering close to 10% in June . The rise has been mainly driven by higher fuel and power prices , manufactured goods inflation , and food price pressures arising from supply-side shocks such as weak monsoon conditions.

## What is Inflation?
- Inflation refers to the rate at which the general price level of goods and services rises over a period of time. It reduces the purchasing power of money, meaning people have to spend more to buy the same quantity of goods and services.
- According to the International Monetary Fund , inflation measures the rate of increase in prices over a given period. It may refer to overall price rise or price rise in specific goods and services.
In India, inflation is important because it directly affects:
- Cost of living
- Household consumption
- Savings and investment decisions
- Real wages
- Fiscal policy
- Monetary policy
- Poverty and inequality

## How Inflation is Measured in India
- India measures inflation primarily through the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) . In 2026, the Government also introduced a comprehensive Producer Price Index (PPI) framework to gradually replace WPI over the next five years in line with global best practices.
| Index | Meaning | Key Features |
| Consumer Price Index (CPI) | Measures changes in retail prices paid by consumers | Base Year: 2024 (current official series) ; |
| Wholesale Price Index (WPI) | Measures price changes at the wholesale (producer) level | Base Year revised to 2022–23 (effective June 2026); covers Primary Articles, Fuel & Power, and Manufactured Products ; compiled by the Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT. WPI will continue only for five years before being phased out. |
| Output Producer Price Index (OPPI) | Measures prices received by producers for goods sold | New index (Base Year: 2022–23) introduced in June 2026; reflects producer prices excluding taxes and trade margins; aligned with international standards. |
| Input Producer Price Index (IPPI – Trial) | Measures prices paid by producers for production inputs | New trial index (Base Year: 2022–23) ; compiled using purchasers’ prices to track input cost inflation. |
| Service Producer Price Index (Service PPI) | Measures producer prices for selected services | New quarterly index (Base Year: 2022–23) covering Banking, Insurance, Pension Funds, Securities Transactions, Railways, Air Passenger Transport, and Telecom . |
| Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) – MPC is a statutory body that decides India’s benchmark policy interest rate, mainly the repo rate, to achieve the inflation target. About Monetary Policy Monetary policy means the use of monetary tools by the central bank to regulate: Interest rates Money supply Availability of credit In India, monetary policy is conducted by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) . RBI’s responsibility for monetary policy is given under the RBI Act, 1934 . Primary Objective Main objective: Price stability It is pursued while keeping in mind the objective of economic growth . Price stability is considered necessary for sustainable growth . Legal Basis In May 2016 , the RBI Act, 1934 was amended. The amendment gave statutory backing to the Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework . The inflation target is fixed by the Government of India in consultation with RBI once every five years . MPC is constituted by the Central Government under Section 45ZB of the RBI Act. Function of MPC MPC fixes the benchmark policy rate , mainly the repo rate . It decides the rate needed to keep inflation within the target range. Its decision is binding on the RBI . It replaced the earlier Technical Advisory Committee system. Composition Total members: 6 RBI Governor – Chairperson RBI Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy One official nominated by the RBI Board Three members representing the Government of India External members hold office for 4 years . Decision-Making Quorum: 4 members At least one member must be: RBI Governor, or Deputy Governor in Governor’s absence Decisions are taken by majority vote . In case of a tie, the RBI Governor has a casting vote . Institutional Support RBI’s Monetary Policy Department (MPD) assists MPC in policy formulation. MPC must meet at least four times in a year . Significance Brings transparency in monetary policy. Reduces individual discretion in rate decisions. Helps control inflation expectations . Balances price stability and growth . Strengthens accountability of the RBI’s monetary policy process. |

## Recent Inflation Trend in India
Inflation in India had remained relatively low for more than a decade, except during the COVID-19 pandemic . However, the recent trend shows a renewed rise.
The WPI inflation rate was negative or close to zero until December of the previous year. It began rising sharply from March and moved close to 10% by June .
WPI has three major components:
- Primary articles: food, minerals and other basic commodities
- Fuel and power: petroleum products, electricity and related energy costs
- Manufactured products: industrial goods produced in factories
The recent rise has been mainly driven by fuel and power and manufactured products , while food prices have also contributed through supply-side stress.

## Structural Difference Between Food and Industrial Prices
A useful way to understand inflation is through the structural approach associated with economist Michal Kalecki .
Primary Commodity Prices
- Primary commodity prices, especially food prices, are strongly affected by demand-supply mismatch. Agricultural supply is often fixed in the short run because output depends on monsoon, soil, irrigation and crop cycles.
A poor monsoon or drought can reduce supply and raise prices sharply.
Manufactured Product Prices
- Industrial prices are usually cost-determined. Factories often operate below full capacity, so if demand rises, producers can increase output without immediately raising prices.
- Prices of manufactured goods rise mainly when input costs increase, such as fuel, raw materials, electricity or transport costs.
- This distinction is important because it shows that all inflation cannot be treated as demand-driven inflation.

## Major Reasons for the Recent Inflation Rise

### 1. Rise in Fuel and Power Prices
Fuel and power prices are closely linked to international crude oil prices. Since India imports a large share of its crude oil requirement, higher global oil prices create imported inflation .
Fuel affects the economy in two ways:
- It directly raises petrol, diesel and energy prices.
- It indirectly raises transport, logistics and production costs.
This cost increase is passed on to manufactured goods, creating cost-push inflation .

### 2. Manufactured Goods Inflation
- Manufactured goods inflation has risen largely because of higher input costs, especially fuel and power.
- In India, wage costs are not usually the main driver of manufactured inflation because workers have limited bargaining power. Instead, material costs such as oil, electricity, transport and industrial inputs create the inflationary impulse.Thus, when fuel prices rise, manufactured goods prices also tend to rise.

### 3. Food Price Pressures
- Food inflation has been linked to supply shocks such as inadequate monsoon conditions and the impact of El Niño on agricultural production.
- When rainfall is poor, crop output may fall. Since food supply cannot be increased immediately, prices rise quickly.
- Past drought years also show that weak monsoon conditions are often accompanied by significant food inflation. A drought may not be the only cause of food inflation, but it is a strong trigger for food price rise.

### 4. Supply-Side Nature of Inflation
The recent inflation surge does not appear to be mainly due to excess demand or overheating of the economy.
Instead, it has been driven by:
- Fuel cost rise
- Imported oil price pressure
- Higher production costs
- Food supply shocks
- Monsoon-related agricultural stress
This makes the current inflation largely cost-push and supply-shock driven .

## Policy Measures Needed
1. Strengthen Irrigation Infrastructure
Food prices should be made less dependent on monsoon fluctuations. India needs greater investment in irrigation, water storage, watershed management and climate-resilient agriculture .
2. Use Countercyclical Indirect Tax Policy
When crude oil prices rise sharply, the government can temporarily reduce customs and excise duties on fuel to prevent full pass-through to consumers and producers.
3. Review Inflation Targeting Framework
A narrow inflation-targeting approach may be less effective when inflation is driven by food and fuel supply shocks. Policy response should combine monetary, fiscal and supply-side measures.
4. Improve Agricultural Supply Chains
Better storage, cold chains, logistics and market linkages can reduce volatility in food prices.
5. Monitor Imported Inflation
Since fuel prices are externally determined, India needs a strategy to manage oil price shocks through reserves, taxation policy and energy diversification.

## Significance for Economy and Society
Impact on Consumers
Inflation reduces purchasing power, especially for poor and middle-income households.
Impact on Producers
Rising fuel and input costs increase production expenses and may reduce profit margins.
Impact on Real Wages
If nominal wages do not rise with inflation, real incomes decline.
Impact on Monetary Policy
The Reserve Bank of India uses CPI inflation for monetary policy decisions, but supply-driven inflation cannot be solved only through interest rate changes.
Impact on Competitiveness
If domestic prices rise faster than trading partners, Indian goods may become less competitive internationally.

## Conclusion
India’s recent inflation rise is mainly a supply-side and cost-push phenomenon , driven by higher fuel costs, manufactured goods inflation and food price pressures from weak monsoon conditions. Treating it only as excess demand would lead to an incomplete policy response. A more effective strategy requires irrigation investment, food supply-chain reforms, countercyclical fuel taxation, energy diversification and a broader inflation management framework . Inflation control must therefore combine price stability with protection of livelihoods and productive capacity.

- The WPI does not capture changes in the prices of services, which CPI does.
- Reserve Bank of India has now adopted WPI as its key measure of inflation and to decide on changing the key policy rates.

Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only
Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because food has a higher weightage in CPI than in WPI , as CPI reflects household consumption expenditure. Statement 2 is correct because WPI does not include services , while CPI captures price changes in goods and services consumed by people. ## About National Film Awards
- The National Film Awards were established in 1954 and are among the most prestigious film awards in India. The awards are presented by the President of India and aim to recognise excellence in Indian cinema.
- The first awards were given for the films of 1953 . Initially, they were called State Awards and included two President’s Gold Medals , two certificates of merit , and silver medals for regional films . Over time, the number and scope of awards expanded.
- Separate awards for artists and technicians were instituted in 1968 for films of 1967 . Nargis Dutt and Uttam Kumar were the first recipients of the Best Actress and Best Actor awards, then called Urvashi and Bharat awards respectively.
The awards are given in three broad sections:
- Feature Films
- Non-Feature Films
- Best Writing on Cinema
The awards aim to encourage films of aesthetic excellence , technical excellence , and social relevance , while promoting unity, integrity and appreciation of India’s cultural diversity .
