Kakinada Special Economic Zone: Renewed Push for Port-Led Industrial Growth in Andhra Pradesh

### Relevance: APPSC Andhra Pradesh Economy, Industrial Development, Port-led Growth, Infrastructure, Current Affairs

#### For Prelims:
- Kakinada Special Economic Zone, Kakinada Gateway Port, Auro Industrial City, Bharatmala Project, Annavaram Railway Station, Offshore Supply Vessel Berth, Metallurgical Coke, Green Fuels

#### For Mains:
- Port-led industrialisation, multi-modal connectivity, industrial clusters, land acquisition challenges, employment generation, coastal economic development, investment facilitation

## Why in News?
The Kakinada Special Economic Zone , after nearly two decades of slow progress, has started attracting renewed industrial interest. MoUs worth around ₹1.5 lakh crore have been signed for setting up industries in the SEZ, with an estimated potential to generate 1.5 lakh direct and indirect jobs .

## Background of Kakinada Special Economic Zone
- The Kakinada Special Economic Zone was established nearly two decades ago as a major industrial project in coastal Andhra Pradesh. However, the project faced delays due to land-related disputes, farmer agitations and slow infrastructure progress.
- Over time, the zone also came to be referred to as Auro Industrial City . The present revival is closely linked to fresh industrial MoUs, availability of large land parcels and renewed work on the Kakinada Gateway Port .

## Recent Industrial Commitments
- Fresh MoUs worth about ₹1.5 lakh crore have been signed for establishing industries in KSEZ.
- The proposed industrial activity has the potential to generate around 1.5 lakh direct and indirect jobs .
- KSEZ has already allotted around 1,200–1,500 acres to upcoming industrial units. About 10 industries have already come up, and the zone is currently supporting nearly 15,000 employees .
- During the current year, around seven MoUs have been signed in sectors such as:
- Metallurgical coke
- Speciality chemicals
- Green fuels
- Marine food processing
- Recent industrial outreach to South Korea has also opened possibilities for Korean companies, including HD Hyundai , to establish units in KSEZ and Auro Industrial City.

## Infrastructure Linkages
The future of KSEZ depends heavily on port, road and rail connectivity.

### Kakinada Gateway Port
- Work on the Kakinada Gateway Port near Butchayyapeta in Perumallapuram panchayat of Thondangi mandal has been expedited. The aim is to make the port functional within the next two years.
- An Offshore Supply Vessel berth at the port is planned for completion by December 2026 .

### Road Connectivity
- A National Highway under the Bharatmala project is being laid through the KSEZ. This will improve logistics and help industries move goods efficiently.

### Rail Connectivity
- Plans are being prepared for a new railway line from Annavaram Railway Station to the Kakinada Gateway Port . This can strengthen cargo movement and improve the SEZ’s connectivity with wider markets.

## Industrial Potential of KSEZ
KSEZ has more than 5,000 acres of land available in one place, making it suitable for developing industrial clusters.
The area is expected to benefit from:
- Port connectivity through Kakinada Gateway Port
- Road connectivity under Bharatmala
- Proposed railway link from Annavaram
- Availability of water and power support
- Large contiguous land parcel for industries
These factors can help KSEZ emerge as a major industrial hub in Kakinada district.

## Significance for Andhra Pradesh

### 1. Port-Led Industrialisation
- KSEZ can strengthen Andhra Pradesh’s coastal industrial strategy by integrating industry with port infrastructure.

### 2. Employment Creation
- The proposed investments have the potential to generate nearly 1.5 lakh direct and indirect jobs .

### 3. Industrial Cluster Development
- Large land availability can support cluster-based development in chemicals, green fuels, marine processing and related industries.

### 4. Logistics Advantage
- Port, road and proposed rail connectivity can reduce transport barriers and support export-oriented activity.

### 5. Regional Economic Growth
- Kakinada district can develop into a major industrial hub if infrastructure works and industrial commitments progress on schedule.

### 6. Foreign Investment Linkages
- Interest from Korean companies, including HD Hyundai , can strengthen international industrial linkages.

## Conclusion
The renewed momentum in Kakinada Special Economic Zone marks an important phase in Andhra Pradesh’s port-led industrial development. With MoUs worth ₹1.5 lakh crore , employment potential of 1.5 lakh jobs , and accelerated work on the Kakinada Gateway Port , the SEZ can become a major industrial hub. Its success will depend on timely infrastructure completion, transparent land management and effective conversion of investment commitments into functioning industries.

Strengthening Rural Credit for Inclusive Growth in India

### Relevance: UPSC: GS Paper III: Agriculture, Rural Economy, Inclusive Growth, Banking, Financial Inclusion

#### For Prelims:

#### For Mains:

## Why in News?
The Government highlighted the role of India’s rural credit system as a key pillar of rural development and agricultural growth. The rural credit ecosystem has expanded through institutions such as NABARD, Scheduled Commercial Banks, Regional Rural Banks, Cooperative Banks and Small Finance Banks , along with policy measures such as Priority Sector Lending, Ground Level Credit targets, Kisan Credit Card, Modified Interest Subvention Scheme, SHG-Bank Linkage Programme and digital platforms .

## India’s Rural Credit Landscape
India’s Rural Credit Landscape
- India’s rural credit system provides short-, medium- and long-term finance for agriculture, allied activities, rural enterprises, household consumption, income generation, asset creation and livelihood security.
- The system has gradually shifted from informal moneylenders to institutional credit. NABARD , the apex rural development financial institution, supports the sector through refinance, infrastructure finance, institutional development and supervision of Cooperative Banks and Regional Rural Banks.
According to NABARD’s Rural Economic Conditions and Sentiments Survey, May 2026 :
- 77.2% of rural households reported higher consumption.
- Around 51% depended only on formal credit.
- More than 27% used both formal and informal credit.
Thus, formal rural credit is expanding, though informal borrowing continues.

## Evolution of Rural Credit System
India’s rural credit system has evolved through several institutional and policy milestones.
| Year | Development | Importance |

NABARD , established in 1982, celebrated its 45th Foundation Day on 12 July 2026 . It prepares district credit plans, promotes financial inclusion and supports government initiatives for rural credit expansion.

## Institutional Architecture of Rural Credit
India’s rural credit framework rests on a wide network of banking and cooperative institutions.

### 1. Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs)
- SCBs are banks listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934 . They can borrow from RBI at the bank rate and participate in clearing houses.
- Around 120 SCBs operate in India. Their rural branches increased from 41,464 in 2014 to 56,193 by July 2025 . Rural services are provided through branches, Business Correspondents, digital platforms, PMJDY accounts and Direct Benefit Transfer systems.

### 2. Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)
- RRBs were established under the RRB Act, 1976 to provide credit to small and marginal farmers, agricultural labourers, artisans and small entrepreneurs.
- Currently, 28 RRBs operate through more than 22,000 branches in 700 districts .

### 3. Cooperative Banks
Rural cooperative credit institutions follow a multi-tier structure comprising State Cooperative Banks, District Central Cooperative Banks, Primary Agricultural Credit Societies and Agriculture and Rural Development Banks.
The network includes:
- 1,458 Urban Cooperative Banks
- 34 State Cooperative Banks
- 352 District Central Cooperative Banks
They promote banking access in rural and remote areas.

### 4. Small Finance Banks
- Small Finance Banks were introduced after the Union Budget 2014–15 and are licensed by RBI. They provide savings and credit facilities to small businesses, marginal farmers, micro-industries and unorganised-sector entities.
- Currently, 11 Small Finance Banks are operational.

## Policy Framework for Rural Credit

### SHG-Bank Linkage and DAY-NRLM
- The Self-Help Group-Bank Linkage Programme was initiated by NABARD to connect rural SHGs with the formal banking system.
- It has particularly benefited rural women who earlier lacked access to formal credit due to absence of credit history.
- The National Rural Livelihoods Mission was launched in 2010 by restructuring the Swarnajayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana . It was renamed Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission with effect from 29 March 2016 .
Key data:
- 10.05 crore rural women mobilised into more than 90.90 lakh SHGs till July 2025.
- DAY-NRLM is implemented across the country except Delhi and Chandigarh .
- As of 10 July 2026 , over 19.83 lakh SHGs were operational.
- Loan disbursement of ₹13.28 lakh crore has been recorded since inception.
- Around 50,548 Bank Sakhis have been deployed.
- Bank Sakhis helped SHGs access bank credit of over ₹12.18 lakh crore since 2013–14, as on February 2026.
Bank Sakhis assist SHG members in opening accounts, preparing loan applications, submitting documents and ensuring timely repayment. This also helps reduce NPAs.

### Primary Agricultural Credit Societies
- Primary Agricultural Credit Societies are grassroots-level institutions of the short-term cooperative credit structure.
- They directly interact with rural borrowers and provide loans. They also support repayment, distribution and marketing of agricultural inputs and produce.
- PACS form the base of the cooperative credit system and act as the final link between borrowers and higher financing institutions such as SCBs and RBI/NABARD.
- In 2023 , the Government approved a plan to establish 2 lakh new multipurpose PACS, Dairy and Fishery Cooperative Societies across all panchayats over five years.
Progress so far:
- 32,836 new societies registered as on 20 January 2026 .
- 15,793 dairy and fishery cooperatives strengthened.
- Out of 79,630 approved PACS , 61,842 PACS migrated to the Common ERP-based national software as on 10 March 2026 .
This shows the shift towards digitised and multipurpose cooperative credit institutions.

### Modified Interest Subvention Scheme
The Modified Interest Subvention Scheme is a Central Sector Scheme that provides short-term credit to farmers at affordable rates through the Kisan Credit Card .
Main features:
- Farmers receive short-term loans at 7% interest .
- Lending institutions receive 1.5% interest subvention .
- Farmers who repay promptly get an additional incentive of up to 3% .
- Effective interest rate for prompt repayment can fall to 4% .
The Union Budget 2025–26 introduced important changes:
- Loan limit under MISS through KCC increased from ₹3 lakh to ₹5 lakh .
- Lending limit for fisheries and allied activities increased from ₹2 lakh to ₹5 lakh .
- From January 2025, collateral-free short-term agricultural loan limit increased from ₹1.6 lakh to ₹2 lakh per borrower .
This aims to improve access to credit amid rising input costs and inflation.

### PM Dhan Dhanya Krishi Yojana
- PM Dhan Dhanya Krishi Yojana was approved in July 2025 to catalyse growth in 100 low-performing agricultural districts .
- It follows a saturation-based convergence of 36 Central schemes across 11 Ministries .
Key objectives include:
- Enhancing access to short-term and long-term agricultural credit
- Improving agricultural productivity
- Promoting crop diversification
- Encouraging sustainable agricultural practices
- Strengthening irrigation infrastructure
- Augmenting post-harvest storage capacity at panchayat and block levels
Committees are being formed at the District, State and National levels for planning, implementation and monitoring.
Based on cumulative output from inception to May 2026, top-performing districts are:
- Banka, Bihar
- Mahoba, Uttar Pradesh
- Charaideo, Assam
- Kishanganj, Bihar
- Tikamgarh, Madhya Pradesh

## Strengthening Rural Financial Inclusion
Financial inclusion has become a central pillar of rural credit expansion. Several schemes and digital tools have improved access to banking, credit and welfare delivery.

### Kisan Credit Card
The Kisan Credit Card scheme provides adequate and timely credit support from the banking system.
It offers:
- ATM-enabled debit card
- One-time documentation
- Provision for cost escalation
- Multiple withdrawals within the sanctioned limit
KCC loans support:
Short-term crop cultivation
Post-harvest operations
Marketing-related expenses
Household consumption needs
Working capital for farm maintenance
Investment credit for allied and non-farm activities
KCC coverage includes:
- Owner cultivators
- Tenant farmers
- Oral lessees
- Sharecroppers
- SHGs of farmers
- Joint Liability Groups
- In 2019 , KCC was extended to allied sectors such as dairy, fisheries and animal husbandry .
- The e-KCC portal introduced by NABARD enables end-to-end digitisation for RRBs and Rural Cooperative Banks. Farmers can apply digitally or through nearby Common Service Centres , with loan sanction possible in about two days .
Financial literacy on KCC is promoted through:
- Centres for Financial Literacy
- Financial Literacy Camps
- RBI’s annual Financial Literacy Week

### Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana
Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana provides universal banking access through at least one basic bank account per household.
It supports:
- Credit access
- Insurance
- Pension
- RuPay debit cards
- Direct Benefit Transfer
- JAM Trinity-based welfare delivery
- Linkage of KCC with RuPay platform

### Jan Samarth Portal
- The Jan Samarth Portal , launched in June 2022 , is a one-stop digital platform linking government-sponsored loan and subsidy schemes, including KCC.
It provides:
- Access to beneficiaries
- Support to financial institutions
- Interface for government agencies
- Scheme guidance through digital processes
- End-to-end coverage of linked schemes
The portal aims to streamline credit delivery and promote inclusive growth.

### Jan Dhan Darshak App
The Jan Dhan Darshak App helps citizens locate banking service points such as:
- Bank branches
- ATMs
- Bank Mitras
- Common Service Centres
It also helps the Government monitor banking access in villages.
As of 6 March 2025 , 99.92% of villages had a banking outlet within a 5 km radius . Villages in Dadra and Nagar Haveli achieved full coverage.

## Conclusion
India’s rural credit system has evolved from an informal and fragmented structure into a diversified, institution-led and policy-driven ecosystem. Institutions such as NABARD, Scheduled Commercial Banks, RRBs, Cooperative Banks, PACS and Small Finance Banks are expanding access to affordable and timely credit. Policy measures such as Priority Sector Lending, Ground Level Credit targets, KCC, MISS, DAY-NRLM, PMJDY, Jan Samarth Portal and PACS digitisation are strengthening financial inclusion. With deeper digitisation, wider institutional reach and targeted credit delivery, rural credit can become a powerful driver of inclusive rural development, agricultural growth and long-term economic resilience .

One Nation, One Election will not fix what is broken

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Constitution, Parliament, Federalism, Elections, Election Commission of India, Constitutional Amendments

#### For Prelims:
- One Nation, One Election, Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024, Article 82A, Articles 83 and 172, Model Code of Conduct, Election Commission of India, Constructive No-Confidence Motion, Basic Structure Doctrine, Kesavananda Bharati Case, 73rd and 74th Amendments, CAPF, EVM, VVPAT, Common Electoral Roll

#### For Mains:
- Federalism, parliamentary democracy, free and fair elections, electoral reforms, campaign finance transparency, regional representation, collective responsibility, political accountability, democratic mandate, administrative continuity

## Why in News?
The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 seeks to institutionalise One Nation, One Election by synchronising elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies through a proposed Article 82A and amendments to Articles 83 and 172 . The proposal has revived debate on whether simultaneous elections can improve governance efficiency or whether they may create constitutional, federal and democratic complications.

## What is One Nation, One Election?
- One Nation, One Election refers to the proposal to synchronise India’s electoral calendar so that elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies are held together within a common election cycle.
- In its broader political sense, ONOE is often discussed as including local body elections also. However, the Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 defines simultaneous elections mainly in relation to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies . Local body elections are not included in the present Bill, though the Kovind Committee recommended that they may be aligned within 100 days of Lok Sabha and Assembly elections.

## Historical Background
- Simultaneous elections were the normal practice in India during the early post-Independence period.
- From 1951 to 1967 , elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies were largely held together during the first four general election cycles: 1952, 1957, 1962 and 1967 .
- This synchronisation was disrupted during 1968–69 due to premature dissolution of several State Assemblies. The cycle was further broken when the 4th Lok Sabha , elected in 1967, was dissolved ahead of schedule in 1970 , leading to fresh national elections in 1971 .
- Since then, India has followed a staggered electoral calendar, with elections taking place in different States at different times.

## Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024
The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 is the main legal instrument proposed to implement ONOE.
Its key features include:
- Insertion of a new Article 82A to create a framework for simultaneous elections.
- Amendment of Article 83 , which deals with the duration of Parliament.
- Amendment of Article 172 , which deals with the duration of State Legislatures.
- The President may notify an appointed date , linked to the first sitting of a new Lok Sabha.
- The terms of State Assemblies elected after the appointed date may be adjusted to end with the Lok Sabha’s term.
- If the Lok Sabha or a State Assembly is dissolved before completing its term, fresh elections will be held only for the unexpired term .
- The Election Commission may recommend deferring a specific State election in case of localised exigencies.
- Local bodies have been strategically excluded from the current Bill.
A companion Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024 seeks to extend the synchronisation framework to legislatures of Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir .
| Committee Recommendations on ONOE The idea of simultaneous elections has been examined by several institutions. Election Commission Report, 1983: The Election Commission of India recommended a return to simultaneous elections to reduce election expenditure and administrative inconvenience. Law Commission, 170th Report, 1999: The Law Commission chaired by Justice B.P. Jeevan Reddy supported synchronised elections for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Parliamentary Standing Committee, 2015: It highlighted the burden of repeated elections and the impact of the Model Code of Conduct on governance. NITI Aayog Discussion Paper, 2017: It suggested a phase-wise synchronisation model to minimise constitutional disruption. Law Commission Draft, 2018: It proposed legal amendments, phased models, constructive no-confidence motion and fixed-term adjustments for hung legislatures. Kovind Committee, 2023–24: The High-Level Committee chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind recommended phased implementation, first aligning Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections, followed by local body elections within 100 days. |

## Arguments in Favour of ONOE

### 1. Reduction in Public Expenditure
- Repeated elections require separate spending on polling arrangements, security deployment, transport and election administration. Proponents argue that simultaneous elections can reduce duplication of election-related costs.
- According to the Centre for Media Studies , the 2024 general election cost was estimated at around ₹1.35 lakh crore , more than double the ₹60,000 crore spent in 2019.
- The Joint Parliamentary Committee has reportedly stated that simultaneous elections to Lok Sabha, Assemblies and local bodies may save nearly ₹7 lakh crore by reducing repeated expenditure on security movement, procurement and election logistics.

### 2. Reduced Model Code of Conduct Disruptions
- The Model Code of Conduct restricts governments from announcing new schemes and major policy decisions during elections. A typical MCC period lasts around 45 to 60 days .
- Supporters argue that frequent elections place different parts of the country under MCC for long periods, affecting administration and development decisions. ONOE may limit this restriction to a single predictable election window.

### 3. Administrative Continuity
Frequent elections require repeated deployment of civil servants, police forces, teachers and local administrative machinery. Simultaneous elections may allow officials to focus on routine governance and long-term implementation for a stable five-year period.

### 4. Optimised Security Deployment
Elections require large-scale deployment of Central Armed Police Forces and State police. A synchronised election cycle may allow a single coordinated security plan, reducing repeated movement and fatigue of security personnel.

### 5. Possible Economic Benefits
Supporters cite macroeconomic modelling suggesting that simultaneous elections may improve GDP growth by reducing policy uncertainty and stabilising public expenditure patterns. Some findings presented before the JPC reportedly suggest that ONOE may boost GDP growth by up to 1.6 percentage points .

### 6. Reduction in Voter Fatigue
Repeated elections may lead to voter fatigue, especially in urban areas. A common election window may increase voter interest and make it easier for migrant workers to participate in multiple elections at once.

## Constitutional and Federal Concerns

### 1. Basic Structure Doctrine
- In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973 , the Supreme Court held that parliamentary democracy and free and fair elections are part of the Constitution’s basic structure .
- Any amendment that weakens federalism, democratic accountability or free and fair elections may face constitutional scrutiny.

### 2. Collective Responsibility and Parliamentary System
- India follows a parliamentary system where the executive remains in office only as long as it enjoys majority support in the legislature.
- ONOE creates a challenge when a government loses majority mid-term. If elections are restricted to a fixed cycle, questions arise over whether the government should continue, whether President’s Rule should be imposed, or whether elections should be held only for the remaining term.
- The proposed constructive no-confidence motion, borrowed from Germany, requires a no-confidence motion to be accompanied by support for an alternative government. Critics argue that this may distort India’s parliamentary tradition and allow weak governments to remain in office.

### 3. Extension or Curtailment of Assembly Terms
- To reach the first synchronised cycle, some State Assembly terms may have to be extended or shortened.
- This raises a democratic concern because Articles 83 and 172 use the phrase “no longer” , indicating that legislative terms should not normally exceed five years. Extending a term without voter approval may violate the democratic mandate, while curtailing a term may deny voters the full tenure for which they elected a government.

### 4. Impact on Federalism and Regional Parties
- Simultaneous elections may create a wave effect , where voters choose the same party at both national and State levels.
- This may structurally benefit larger national parties and weaken regional parties. Local and regional concerns such as agrarian distress, coastal livelihoods, flood management, regional identity and State-specific governance issues may be overshadowed by national campaigns.

### 5. Local Self-Government Question
- The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments created a separate constitutional space for panchayats and municipalities . Their elections are conducted by State Election Commissions , not the Election Commission of India.
- If local body elections are included within ONOE, it may affect the autonomy of the third tier of government. If they are excluded, election-related expenditure, security deployment and governance disruption will still continue through municipal and panchayat election cycles.

## Administrative and Financial Challenges

### 1. Concentrated MCC Freeze
- Under the present system, the MCC applies in different States at different times. Under ONOE, the freeze may become a single national freeze, affecting developmental announcements across all States at the same time.
- This may concentrate governance disruption rather than eliminate it.

### 2. Short-Term Legislatures after Mid-Term Dissolution
- The proposed unexpired term rule means that if an Assembly is dissolved mid-cycle, the new Assembly may serve only the remaining period of the national cycle.
- This could create extremely short legislative terms and reduce the time available for governments to implement policies effectively.

### 3. EVM and VVPAT Requirements
Simultaneous elections would require separate voting machines for all tiers at the same time.
Official estimates indicate a need for approximately:
- 46.75 lakh Ballot Units
- 33.63 lakh Control Units
- 36.62 lakh VVPATs
The Election Commission has estimated recurring replacement and upgrading costs of around ₹10,000 crore every 15 years , given the fixed lifespan of these machines.

### 4. Security Personnel Pressure
- The current multi-phase election model allows security forces to be moved and redeployed across States. Simultaneous elections would require security coverage across all polling stations at the same time.
- This may create a shortage of trained central security personnel, particularly in sensitive regions.

## Why ONOE May Not Fix Electoral Pathologies
The strongest criticism is that ONOE reorganises the electoral calendar but does not address the deeper problems of elections.
The major pathologies include:
- Unlimited political party spending
- Cash distribution to voters
- Opaque political funding
- Criminalisation of politics
- Misuse of state machinery
- Weak enforcement of expenditure limits
- Limited autonomy of election management institutions
Government expenditure on elections is reported to be less than 0.1% of its own budget. The larger concern is unaccounted political spending. The Centre for Media Studies estimated election-related expenditure of over ₹1,00,000 crore for the 2024 Lok Sabha election alone.
Thus, ONOE may concentrate campaign spending into one cycle but may not reduce black money unless campaign finance reforms are implemented.

## Reforms Needed Beyond ONOE
Electoral reform should focus not only on the timing of elections but also on the integrity of the process.
Key reforms include:
- Mandatory disclosure of all political donations
- Enforceable expenditure limits for parties and candidates
- Stronger monitoring of cash distribution to voters
- Disqualification or stricter screening of criminally tainted candidates
- Greater autonomy and institutional capacity for the Election Commission of India
- Separate expenditure caps for Assembly and Lok Sabha candidates contesting in the same constituency
- Media and airtime regulation to protect regional parties
- Strong safeguards against misuse of state machinery
- Consensus-building with States and regional parties before major constitutional changes
A transparent legislative process and deep inter-State consensus are essential before restructuring India’s electoral calendar.

## Conclusion
The push for One Nation, One Election represents a major attempt to restructure India’s electoral system in the name of administrative efficiency, fiscal savings and governance continuity. However, the proposal raises serious concerns relating to federalism, parliamentary accountability, regional representation, local self-government, basic structure doctrine and electoral fairness . ONOE may streamline the electoral calendar, but it cannot by itself cure deeper electoral problems such as black money, criminalisation, opaque funding and misuse of state machinery. India’s reform priority should therefore combine electoral calendar debate with stronger campaign finance regulation, institutional autonomy of the Election Commission and protection of the federal democratic mandate.

Q. Were simultaneous elections held earlier in India? Yes. They were largely held together from 1951 to 1967 .
