- These include healthcare solutions driven by artificial intelligence , automation, cyber-physical systems and autonomous technologies.

Sarnath: India’s 45th UNESCO World Heritage Site

### Relevance: UPSC: GS Paper I: Indian Culture, Buddhism, Ancient Indian History, Heritage Sites

#### For Prelims:
- Sarnath, UNESCO World Heritage Site, 48th World Heritage Committee, Busan, Dharmachakra Pravartan, Dhamek Stupa, Dharmarajika Stupa, Chaukhandi Stupa, Rishipatana, Mrigadava

#### For Mains:
- Civilisational heritage, Buddhist pilgrimage, heritage conservation, cultural tourism, Outstanding Universal Value, Buddhist Circuit, living spiritual tradition

## Why in News?
The Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath in Uttar Pradesh has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee held in Busan, South Korea . With this inscription, India now has 45 World Heritage Sites .
Sarnath had been on UNESCO’s tentative list since 1998 , and after nearly 28 years, it has received full World Heritage recognition.

## Historical Importance of Sarnath
Sarnath , located near Varanasi in Uttar Pradesh, is one of the holiest sites of Buddhism. It is the place where Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon after attaining enlightenment.
This event is known as Dharmachakra Pravartan , meaning the turning of the wheel of Dharma . It marks the beginning of the spread of Buddhist teachings.
According to sacred Buddhist traditions, Buddha travelled to Sarnath in the sixth century BCE , met his first disciples, and delivered his first sermon there.

## Sarnath in Buddhist Tradition
Sarnath is counted among the major Buddhist pilgrimage centres of the world.
It is also referred to in Buddhist texts by names such as:
- Rishipatana
- Isipatana
- Mrigadava
- Deer Park
These names reflect the spiritual and historical memory of the site across different Buddhist traditions.
For nearly 2,500 years , Sarnath has remained associated with pilgrimage, Buddhist learning, monastic activity and the message of wisdom, compassion and peace . ( UNESCO )

## UNESCO World Heritage Recognition
The UNESCO World Heritage Committee inscribes sites that have Outstanding Universal Value . Such sites are considered important not only for one nation but for the shared heritage of humanity.
The inscription of Sarnath recognises its:
- religious significance
- archaeological value
- Buddhist pilgrimage tradition
- civilisational importance
- living spiritual association
The site now stands alongside India’s other globally recognised heritage properties.

## Key Monuments and Sacred Landscape
The World Heritage property preserves the architectural and archaeological remains of ancient Sarnath.
Important protected components include:

### 1. Chaukhandi Stupa
It is one of the protected archaeological sites associated with Sarnath’s sacred landscape.

### 2. Archaeological Remains of Sarnath
This includes major Buddhist structures such as:
- Dhamek Stupa
- Dharmarajika Stupa
- remains of temples, stupas and monasteries
Together, these monuments form the historic landscape widely known as Deer Park . ( UNESCO )

## Why Sarnath Deserved World Heritage Status

### 1. Birthplace of Buddhist Teaching Tradition
Sarnath is the place where Buddha gave his first sermon, making it central to the history of Buddhism.

### 2. Continuity of Pilgrimage
The site has remained a place of devotion and inspiration for Buddhists for centuries.

### 3. Archaeological Significance
Its stupas, temples and monastic remains show the growth of Buddhist architecture and religious institutions.

### 4. Civilisational Message
Sarnath represents values of compassion, harmony, wisdom and peace , which continue to have global relevance.

### 5. Integrated Cultural and Spiritual Heritage
Its identity is not limited to monuments alone. It combines sacred geography, archaeological remains and living pilgrimage tradition.

## Significance for India and Uttar Pradesh

### 1. Strengthening India’s Cultural Diplomacy
Sarnath’s inscription enhances India’s global profile as a centre of Buddhist civilisation.

### 2. Boost to Buddhist Circuit
The recognition can strengthen Uttar Pradesh’s Buddhist Circuit on the international tourism and cultural heritage map.

### 3. Heritage Tourism
World Heritage status can attract pilgrims, researchers and tourists from Buddhist countries and across the world.

### 4. Conservation Priority
The inscription brings greater attention to heritage conservation, site management and responsible tourism.

### 5. Recognition of India’s Civilisational Continuity
Sarnath reflects India’s long spiritual and intellectual traditions and its contribution to global religious history.

## Conservation and Management Concerns
World Heritage recognition also brings responsibility.

### 1. Tourism Pressure
Higher footfall may increase pressure on monuments, pathways, interpretation facilities and local infrastructure.

### 2. Authenticity of Historical Presentation
The ASI’s recent correction of site-related historical interpretation shows the need for accurate and evidence-based heritage communication.

### 3. Protection of Archaeological Remains
Stupas, monasteries and excavated remains require continuous conservation.

### 4. Balancing Pilgrimage and Preservation
Sarnath is both a sacred site and an archaeological site; management must respect both dimensions.

## Way Forward
India should prepare a strong site-management framework for Sarnath that integrates conservation, visitor management, interpretation, pilgrimage facilities and local community participation . The Buddhist Circuit should be developed through responsible tourism, not uncontrolled commercialisation. Digital interpretation, multilingual heritage guides, improved signage and strict protection of archaeological remains can enhance both visitor experience and conservation outcomes.

## Conclusion
Sarnath’s inscription on the UNESCO World Heritage List is a major recognition of India’s Buddhist and civilisational heritage. As the place where Buddha delivered his first sermon and set the Dharmachakra in motion, Sarnath represents a universal message of compassion, wisdom and peace. The challenge now is to protect its archaeological integrity while promoting it as a living centre of global Buddhist heritage.

## UPSC PYQ
Q. Which of the following is/are the correct components of the “Lion Capital” at Sarnath?
(UPSC CDS 2024)
- The shaft ( stambha-yashti ) or the tall tapering column
- An elliptical top part — the abacus, devoid of any carvings
- Overflowing inverted petals below the abacus
Select the answer using the code given below:
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b) 1 and 3 only
Explanation
Statement 1 is correct: The Lion Capital at Sarnath was part of the Ashokan pillar. The pillar had a tall, tapering shaft known as stambha-yashti .
Statement 2 is incorrect: The abacus is not elliptical and not devoid of carvings. It is circular and richly carved with animals such as the elephant, bull, horse and lion , separated by Dharmachakras .
Statement 3 is correct: Below the abacus, the capital has overflowing inverted lotus petals , which form an important decorative and symbolic component of the structure.

Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme: Catalysing Rural Economies Through Grassroots Entrepreneurship

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Welfare Schemes, SHGs, Inclusive Governance GS Paper III: Rural Economy, Employment, Entrepreneurship, Inclusive Growth

#### For Prelims:
- SVEP, DAY-NRLM, Self-Help Groups, Community Resource Persons–Enterprise Promotion, Block Resource Centres–Enterprise Promotion, Community Enterprise Fund, MUDRA Yojana, State Rural Livelihoods Missions

#### For Mains:
- Grassroots entrepreneurship, non-farm rural enterprises, community-led institutions, women-led development, poverty reduction, local employment generation, inclusive rural economy

## Why in News?
The Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme (SVEP) has emerged as an important grassroots model for poverty reduction, local employment generation and rural economic development . By June 2026, the programme had supported over 4.32 lakh rural enterprises across the country, with a strong focus on women and socially disadvantaged groups.

## What is SVEP?
The Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme is a rural entrepreneurship initiative launched in 2016 as a sub-scheme of the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Rural Livelihoods Mission .
It supports Self-Help Groups and their family members in establishing small non-farm enterprises in rural areas.
SVEP provides:
- financial assistance
- institutional support
- business management training
- soft skill development
- mentoring
- business development services
- bank linkage support
The programme aims to promote sustainable livelihoods and self-employment in rural India.

## Link with DAY-NRLM
- SVEP operates under DAY-NRLM , one of the world’s largest livelihood initiatives.
- DAY-NRLM mobilises rural poor households into Self-Help Groups , predominantly women. Its focus is on:
- sustainable livelihoods
- financial inclusion
- skill development
- institutional support
As of mid-June 2026, more than 10.29 crore rural households had been mobilised into SHGs under DAY-NRLM.

## How SVEP Works
SVEP follows a community-led enterprise promotion model .
1. Identification of Entrepreneurs
Community institutions identify potential entrepreneurs based on:
- need
- poverty status
- entrepreneurial potential
2. Enterprise Viability Assessment
Before support is provided, the proposed enterprise is assessed for viability.
3. Training and Mentoring
Entrepreneurs receive business-related training and continuous handholding support.
4. Finance and Credit Support
Financial support is provided through community institutions, bank linkages and convergence with schemes such as Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana .
5. Monitoring and Tracking
Digital tools support business planning, loan appraisal and enterprise tracking.

## Institutional Architecture
SVEP is implemented through State Rural Livelihoods Missions .
Block Resource Centres–Enterprise Promotion
- At the block level, BRC-EPs coordinate programme implementation.
Community Resource Persons–Enterprise Promotion
- CRP-EPs provide technical assistance, mentoring, training and credit facilitation support to entrepreneurs.
Important features of CRP-EPs:
- selected through testing, training and certification
- preference given to local residents
- many are selected from SHG households
- women form a major part of the cadre
- each CRP-EP mentors several enterprises
Community Enterprise Fund
Community institutions manage loans through the Community Enterprise Fund , enabling local decision-making and financial support.

## Social Inclusion under SVEP
SVEP has adopted an inclusive approach by focusing on:
- women
- marginalised communities
- persons with disabilities
- individuals with limited educational attainment
- first-generation entrepreneurs
The programme mandates that at least 60% of beneficiaries are women , while ensuring representation of SC/ST/minority communities and persons with disabilities. Around 86% of entrepreneurs under the programme belong to SC, ST, Minority and OBC communities .

## Impact on Rural Livelihoods
The Quality Council of India conducted a mid-term evaluation of SVEP for the Ministry of Rural Development. The findings indicated positive outcomes in enterprise development, income generation and social inclusion.
Key findings include:
- most SVEP-supported enterprises were profitable
- enterprises became a major source of household income
- women played a significant role in ownership and management
- entrepreneurs reported higher savings
- improved access to finance helped enterprises grow
- continuous mentoring improved business sustainability
The programme also improved confidence, livelihood security, financial skills and social standing among rural entrepreneurs.

## Significance
1. Shift from Welfare to Enterprise-Led Development
SVEP shows that rural poverty reduction can be supported through entrepreneurship, not only through welfare transfers.
2. Strengthening Non-Farm Rural Economy
By supporting small enterprises outside agriculture, the programme diversifies income sources in villages.
3. Women-Led Economic Empowerment
The SHG-based model enables rural women to become entrepreneurs, mentors and community leaders.
4. Community-Based Implementation
Funding and enterprise decisions are rooted in local institutions, making the programme more responsive to rural needs.
5. Local Employment Generation
Small enterprises create livelihood opportunities within villages and reduce dependence on migration.
6. Inclusive Start-up Ecosystem
SVEP extends India’s start-up culture beyond urban centres and builds a rural entrepreneurship base.

## Way Forward
SVEP should continue strengthening SHG federations, CRP-EP cadres, bank linkages and digital monitoring systems . Greater convergence with schemes such as MUDRA can improve access to credit. Rural entrepreneurs also need continued support in business planning, market linkages, product quality, financial literacy and enterprise scaling.

## Conclusion
The Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme has shown that entrepreneurial talent exists in rural India and can grow when supported by community institutions, finance, training and mentoring. By empowering women, marginalised communities and first-generation entrepreneurs, SVEP is laying the foundation for a vibrant rural start-up ecosystem and contributing to the vision of a self-reliant and developed India
