Kosala Nagaram Industrial Park: Andhra Pradesh’s Plan for a New Manufacturing Hub

### Relevance: APPSC Andhra Pradesh Economy, APIIC, Industrial Parks, Chittoor District, Manufacturing Sector

#### For Prelims:
- Kosala Nagaram Industrial Park, APIIC, Chittoor District, Chittoor-Tachur National Highway, Krishnapatnam Port, Kattupalli Port, GNSS Project, Sri City Model

#### For Mains:
- Manufacturing-led growth, industrial infrastructure, regional industrialisation, public-private participation, water security, employment generation, industrial corridor development

## Why in News?
The Andhra Pradesh government is planning to develop Kosala Nagaram Industrial Park in Chittoor district as a major manufacturing hub. The project is aimed at attracting industrial investment, especially from automobile, textile and food processing sectors.

## Core Features of the Project
- The project is being taken forward by the AP Industrial Infrastructure Corporation . APIIC already owns 2,042.13 acres at Kosala Nagaram, and the State government has directed it to acquire an additional 5,000 acres for future expansion.
- The government has also decided to earmark 500 acres for a private industrial park, while about 200 acres have already been allotted to different industries.
- This indicates that Kosala Nagaram is being planned as a large industrial cluster rather than a small industrial estate.

## Why Kosala Nagaram is Strategically Important
Kosala Nagaram is located close to the Tamil Nadu border , which gives it an advantage in attracting industries from established manufacturing centres.
Its major location advantages include:
- proximity to the Chittoor-Tachur National Highway
- access to Krishnapatnam and Kattupalli ports
- connectivity to Chennai and Tirupati airports
- availability of skilled manpower in the region
This combination of land, connectivity and workforce makes the area suitable for manufacturing activity.

## Proposed Industrial Profile
The industrial park is expected to attract multiple sectors.

### 1. Automobile Sector
Automobile manufacturers from Ambattur in Tamil Nadu have shown interest in setting up units in the area.

### 2. Textile Sector
Textile industries may use the park for labour-intensive manufacturing.

### 3. Food Processing Sector
Food processing units can benefit from Andhra Pradesh’s agricultural and horticultural base.
Together, these sectors can create a diversified industrial ecosystem.

## Infrastructure and Water Planning
- The site requires extensive land levelling before industries can begin operations. Since this involves significant cost, the government is considering private participation for developing the park and creating basic infrastructure.
- The biggest infrastructure concern is water availability . The State government has given in-principle approval for a balancing reservoir to meet future water needs. Water is proposed to be supplied from the Galeru Nagari Sujala Sravanthi project , expected to be completed by December 2028 .

## Development Model
The project is being planned on the lines of Sri City in Satyavedu constituency.
Sri City is considered a successful industrial model because it has attracted companies from multiple countries and created large-scale employment. Kosala Nagaram is expected to follow a similar approach by combining:
- large land bank
- external connectivity
- private participation
- sector-specific industrial planning
- ready basic infrastructure

## Conclusion
Kosala Nagaram Industrial Park has the potential to become a major manufacturing hub in Andhra Pradesh. Its success will depend not merely on land availability, but on timely infrastructure creation, assured water supply, private participation and sector-specific planning. If implemented effectively, it can emerge as another important industrial growth centre in southern Andhra Pradesh.

PM Vidyalaxmi Scheme: Making Quality Higher Education Affordable

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: Education, Welfare Schemes, Social Justice, Governance

#### For Prelims:
- PM Vidyalaxmi Scheme, Quality Higher Educational Institutions, Collateral-free Loan, Guarantor-free Loan, Interest Subvention, PM-USP CSIS, NIRF Ranking, SDG 4, NEP 2020

#### For Mains:
- Educational equity, human capital formation, financial inclusion in higher education, digital governance, merit-based access, inclusive and equitable quality education

## Why in News?
The Pradhan Mantri Vidyalaxmi Scheme is in news as the Education Ministry highlighted its role in promoting educational inclusion in India . The scheme provides collateral-free and guarantor-free education loans to meritorious students admitted to top-ranked higher education institutions.It also provides interest subvention based on family income, making higher education more affordable for students facing financial constraints.

## Need for the Scheme
India’s higher education enrolment has improved over the last decade, with the Gross Enrolment Ratio rising from 23.7% in 2014-15 to 30.0% in 2023-24 . However, many meritorious students still struggle to enter quality institutions due to high fees and loan-related barriers.
The problem is not only admission, but affordability. Students who secure seats in reputed institutions may still face difficulties due to:
- lack of collateral
- absence of guarantors
- high interest rates
- complex loan procedures
- limited awareness about support schemes
PM Vidyalaxmi addresses this gap by ensuring that financial weakness does not prevent meritorious students from studying in quality institutions .

## Key Features of PM Vidyalaxmi
The scheme was approved by the Union Cabinet on 6 November 2024 . It is designed as a mission-mode intervention for higher education financing.

### 1. Collateral-Free and Guarantor-Free Loans
Students admitted on merit to eligible institutions can get education loans without collateral or third-party guarantee.

### 2. Coverage of Quality Institutions
The scheme currently covers 1,425 Quality Higher Educational Institutions , including public and private institutions selected mainly through NIRF-based criteria .

### 3. Credit Guarantee Support
For loans up to ₹7.5 lakh , the Government provides 75% credit guarantee to banks. This reduces lending risk and encourages banks to support more students.

### 4. Interest Subvention
Students with annual family income up to ₹8 lakh are eligible for 3% interest subvention on loans up to ₹10 lakh during the moratorium period.

### 5. Wider Course Coverage
The scheme covers all degree and diploma courses in designated QHEIs.

### 6. Repayment Period
The repayment period can go up to 15 years , excluding the moratorium period of course duration plus one year.

## Eligibility Conditions
For Students
A student should:
- secure merit-based admission to a designated QHEI
- remain enrolled in the course
- maintain satisfactory academic performance
- register through Aadhaar
- not discontinue the course midway
- not be expelled for academic or disciplinary reasons
Interest subvention and credit guarantee benefits can be availed only once , either for undergraduate, postgraduate or integrated courses.

### Excluded Admissions
Students admitted through management quota or NRI quota are not eligible for the benefit.

## Eligibility of Institutions
The scheme covers institutions based on their ranking and governance category.
Eligible institutions include:
- top 100 HEIs in NIRF overall, category-specific or domain-specific rankings
- top 200 State/UT government HEIs in NIRF
- remaining HEIs under the Government of India
Indian campuses of foreign institutions, foreign campuses of Indian institutions and foreign education institutions are not covered.
This makes the scheme focused on quality higher education within India .

## PM Vidyalaxmi and PM-USP CSIS
PM Vidyalaxmi works alongside the PM-USP Central Sector Interest Subsidy Scheme .
|  | PM-USP CSIS | PM-Vidyalaxmi |
| Institutions | NAAC/ NBA accredited, INIs, technical institutes approved by regulatory bodies like Medical Commission of India, Nursing Council, Bar Council, Pharmacy Council, Dental Council | Top 100 in NIRF – Private State govt- top 200 in NIRF Remaining central govt |
| Course | Professional/ technical courses | All degree/diploma courses |
| Family income | ₹ 4.5 lakh | ₹ 8 lakh |
| Interest subvention | Full interest subvention during moratorium period | 3% interest subvention during moratorium period |
| No of slots | No limit | 1 lakh fresh slots per year |
| Loan repayment | Up to 15 years after moratorium period (course period + 1 year) |
A student receiving another Central or State scholarship, fee reimbursement or interest subvention cannot claim the same interest benefit again under these schemes.

## Digital Delivery Mechanism
PM Vidyalaxmi is implemented through a unified digital portal . The portal allows students to:
- apply for education loans
- choose banks
- track application status
- claim interest subvention
- raise grievances
- monitor processing stages
The scheme also uses the PM Vidyalaxmi Digital Rupee App / CBDC Wallet for subsidy delivery. Once the loan is sanctioned and disbursed, eligible subsidy is credited to the wallet and then transferred to the loan account.
This improves transparency, speed and accountability in benefit delivery.

## Policy Significance

### 1. Promotes Educational Equity
The scheme helps meritorious students from financially weaker backgrounds access quality higher education.

### 2. Supports NEP 2020
It follows the NEP 2020 recommendation that financial assistance should be available for studies in public and private higher education institutions.

### 3. Advances SDG 4
By improving access to inclusive and equitable quality education, it contributes to Sustainable Development Goal 4 .

### 4. Strengthens Human Capital
Affordable higher education improves skills, employability and long-term economic productivity.

### 5. Encourages Gender and Social Inclusion
Women, transgender students, EWS, OBC, SC, ST, PwD and other social groups can benefit if they meet eligibility conditions.

## Key Concerns

### 1. Awareness Gap
Students from rural and disadvantaged backgrounds may not know how to use the portal or claim benefits.

### 2. Digital Access Barrier
A fully digital system may create difficulties for students with limited internet access or low digital literacy.

### 3. Bank-Level Delays
Loan sanction and disbursement can still be affected by procedural delays at the bank level.

### 4. Academic Continuity Condition
Students must maintain satisfactory performance, so academic support is also necessary along with financial support.

### 5. Limited Subvention Slots
The 3% interest subvention under PM Vidyalaxmi is limited to one lakh fresh students per year .

## Way Forward

### 1. Strengthen Awareness Campaigns
Schools, colleges, district education offices and banks should guide students about eligibility and application steps.

### 2. Provide Handholding Support
Help desks should assist first-generation learners in filling applications and tracking loans.

### 3. Ensure Time-Bound Bank Processing
Banks should follow clear timelines for sanction, disbursement and grievance redressal.

### 4. Link Financial Aid with Academic Support
Mentoring, counselling and remedial learning can help students retain eligibility and complete courses.

### 5. Monitor Inclusion Outcomes
The scheme should track participation of women, SCs, STs, OBCs, EWS, PwD and rural students to ensure real equity.

## Conclusion
The PM Vidyalaxmi Scheme is an important step towards removing financial barriers in higher education. By combining collateral-free loans, credit guarantee, interest subvention and digital delivery , it supports meritorious students in accessing quality institutions. Its success will depend on awareness, timely bank processing, digital inclusion and strong monitoring so that educational opportunity becomes truly merit-based and not income-dependent.

Western Ghats Eco-Sensitive Area: Conservation, Federal Deadlock and Development Concerns

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper III: Environment, Biodiversity, Conservation, Environmental Governance

#### For Prelims:
- Western Ghats, Sahyadri Hills, Eco-Sensitive Area, Eco-Sensitive Zone, MoEFCC, Gadgil Committee, Kasturirangan Committee, Palghat Gap, Anamudi, UNESCO World Heritage Site

#### For Mains:
- Biodiversity hotspot, cooperative federalism, sustainable development, ecological security, livelihood-sensitive conservation, precautionary principle, environmental governance

## Why in News?
The Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change has reissued the draft notification proposing an Eco-Sensitive Area across the Western Ghats for the seventh time . The earlier draft lapsed without finalisation. The new draft keeps the matter open for public objections for 60 days . The main issue is the continuing disagreement between the Centre and the six Western Ghats States over which villages and regions should be included within the proposed eco-sensitive area.

## Key Highlights of the 7th Draft Notification on Western Ghats ESA
The 7th draft notification seeks to create a legally regulated Eco-Sensitive Area across parts of the Western Ghats while balancing conservation needs with local livelihood concerns.

### 1. Geographical Coverage
The proposed ESA covers 56,825.7 sq. km across six Western Ghats States. Karnataka has the largest proposed area, while Gujarat has the smallest.
| State | Proposed ESA Area |
| Karnataka | 20,668 sq. km |
| Maharashtra | 17,340 sq. km |
| Kerala | 9,993.7 sq. km |
| Tamil Nadu | 6,914 sq. km |
| Goa | 1,461 sq. km |
| Gujarat | 449 sq. km |

### 2. Activities Proposed to be Prohibited
The draft proposes a complete ban on high-impact and ecologically harmful activities such as commercial mining, stone quarrying, sand mining, new thermal power plants, red-category polluting industries and large construction projects .

### 3. Activities to be Regulated or Protected
Hydel power projects will not be fully banned but will be strictly regulated. The notification also clarifies that agriculture, plantation activities and day-to-day livelihood practices of local communities will remain unaffected .

### 4. Phased Implementation Option
The draft allows phased notification of the ESA on a State-wise or combined basis. This means the Centre may proceed with notification even if complete consensus among all six States is not immediately achieved.

### 5. Committee to Examine State Objections
An expert committee formed in 2022 is examining objections raised by States, especially regarding village names, boundary errors and area demarcation . Its tenure has been extended till July 2027 .

## About Western Ghats:
- The Western Ghats , also known as the Sahyadri Hills , run parallel to India’s western coast. They extend from the Satpura Range in the north through Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, ending near Kanyakumari .
- The range is called Sahyadri in northern Maharashtra, Sahya Parvatham in Kerala, and Nilgiri Malai in parts of Tamil Nadu.

### Coastal and Foothill Regions
- The northern coastal plain between the Western Ghats and Arabian Sea is called the Konkan Coast .
- The central coastal part is called Kanara .
- The southern coastal part is called the Malabar Coast .
- The eastern foothill region in Maharashtra is called Desh .
- The eastern foothill region in central Karnataka is called Malanadu .

### Geological Features
There are two major views on the geology of the Western Ghats:
- They are treated as block mountains formed due to down-warping of land towards the Arabian Sea.
- They are also described as the faulted edge of the Deccan Plateau , not true fold mountains.
Major rocks found in the region include basalt, charnockites, granite gneiss, khondalites, metamorphic gneisses, crystalline limestone, iron ore, dolerites and anorthosites .

## Important Passes in Western Ghats
Passes are important for transport, trade and connectivity between the western coastal plain and the interior plateau.
| Pass | Connectivity |
| Thal Ghat / Kasara Ghat | Connects Mumbai to Nashik |
| Bhor Ghat | Connects Mumbai to Pune via Khopoli |
| Palakkad Gap / Palghat Gap | Connects Coimbatore in Tamil Nadu to Palakkad in Kerala |
| Amba Ghat | Connects Ratnagiri district to Kolhapur |
| Naneghat Pass | Connects Pune district with Junnar |
| Amboli Ghat Pass | Connects Sawantwadi in Maharashtra to Belgaum in Karnataka |
Among these, the Palakkad Gap is especially significant because it is a major break in the Western Ghats and links Kerala with Tamil Nadu.

## Protected Area and Eco-Sensitive Zone
A Protected Area is a legally protected conservation space such as a national park, wildlife sanctuary or tiger reserve. An Eco-Sensitive Zone acts as a buffer around such protected areas to reduce ecological pressure.
| Feature | Protected Area | Eco-Sensitive Zone |
| Purpose | Strict biodiversity conservation | Buffer protection around protected areas |
| Protection Level | Highest protection | Regulated protection |
| Activities | Highly restricted | Harmful activities restricted; sustainable practices allowed |
| Livelihoods | Often more restricted | Traditional livelihoods may continue |
| Role | Core conservation area | Shock absorber around core areas |
This distinction is important because many State concerns arise from the fear that ESA rules may affect farming, plantations and local livelihoods.

## Evolution of Western Ghats ESA Proposal
The Western Ghats conservation debate began after the Western Ghats Ecology Expert Panel , headed by Madhav Gadgil , submitted its report in 2011 .

### Gadgil Committee, 2011
- The committee recommended treating the entire Western Ghats as ecologically sensitive. It suggested three graded zones: ESZ-I, ESZ-II and ESZ-III , based on ecological sensitivity. States opposed the report because it was seen as too restrictive.

### Kasturirangan Committee, 2013
- The committee proposed a more limited approach. It recommended that about 37% of the Western Ghats , around 60,000 sq. km , should be notified as an ESA.
- Since 2014 , draft notifications have been issued repeatedly, but a final notification has been delayed due to disagreements with States.

## Why States are Opposing
The six Western Ghats States have expressed concerns about the impact of ESA notification on:
- agriculture
- plantations
- housing and local construction
- infrastructure projects
- livelihood security
- village-level development planning
The main dispute is over demarcation . States have objected to the names, boundaries and areas of villages proposed for inclusion. An expert committee constituted in 2022 is examining these objections.

## Ecological and Economic Importance
The Western Ghats are recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and are among the world’s eight hottest hotspots of biological diversity .

### 1. Monsoon Regulation
The Ghats intercept moisture-laden southwest monsoon winds from the Arabian Sea and influence rainfall patterns across peninsular India.

### 2. River Systems
They form the major watershed of peninsular India.
West-flowing rivers: Periyar, Bharathappuzha, Netravati, Sharavathi, Mandovi These are short, swift and steep-gradient rivers with hydroelectric potential.
East-flowing rivers: Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Tunga, Bhadra, Bhima, Malaprabha, Ghataprabha, Hemavathi and Kabini These rivers support irrigation, agriculture and drinking water needs.

### 3. Biodiversity and Endemism
The region has rich tropical evergreen forests and is home to many endemic species such as Nilgiri Tahr and Lion-tailed Macaque .
It supports globally threatened plants, mammals, birds, amphibians, reptiles and fishes. Species such as Asian Elephant, Gaur, Tiger, Nilgiri Langur and Lion-tailed Macaque make the region ecologically significant.

### 4. Livelihood and Economy
The Western Ghats support agriculture, plantations, non-timber forest produce, hydropower, tourism and forest-dependent livelihoods. They also contain minerals such as iron ore, manganese and bauxite , which explains the pressure from extractive industries.

## Key Challenges
1. Centre-State Deadlock
The Centre seeks ecological protection, while States fear economic and livelihood restrictions.
2. Boundary Demarcation Issues
Village boundaries, satellite-based mapping and ground realities do not always match.
3. Regulatory Uncertainty
Repeated draft notifications without finalisation create confusion and weaken enforcement.
4. Development Pressure
Mining, quarrying, roads, construction and linear infrastructure continue to fragment fragile landscapes.
5. Conservation Cost
States often bear the political and economic cost of conservation without adequate compensation.

## Way Forward
1. Field-Based Demarcation
ESA boundaries should be finalised using satellite data, field verification and village-level consultation.
2. Cooperative Federalism
The Centre and States must resolve differences through transparent dialogue instead of repeated draft extensions.
3. Livelihood-Sensitive Regulation
Traditional agriculture, plantations and local livelihoods should be clearly protected, while high-impact activities must be restricted.
4. Ecological Fiscal Transfers
States conserving fragile ecosystems should receive financial incentives for protecting national ecological assets.
5. Sustainable Local Economy
Eco-tourism, agro-ecology, non-timber forest produce and community conservation can reduce opposition to ESA rules.
6. Strict Control of High-Impact Activities
Mining, quarrying, polluting industries and unregulated construction must be regulated through strong enforcement.

## Conclusion
The Western Ghats are not only a mountain chain but a critical ecological system that regulates monsoon, sustains rivers, protects biodiversity and supports millions of livelihoods. The repeated delay in finalising the ESA notification shows the difficulty of balancing conservation with development. The solution lies in a scientific, federal and livelihood-sensitive approach , where fragile ecosystems are protected without ignoring local communities.
