Kakinada Palm-Leaf Manuscript Museum Gets ₹5 Crore Funding Boost

### Relevance: APPSC Andhra Pradesh History and Culture

#### For Prelims:
- Andhra Sahitya Parishad Government Museum and Research Institute, Kakinada, Palm-leaf Manuscripts, Museum Grant Scheme, Ministry of Culture, Ayurveda, Telugu Literature, Sanskrit Literature, Buddhist Pillars.

#### For Mains:
- Cultural heritage preservation, manuscript conservation, knowledge traditions, museum infrastructure, heritage governance, State-Centre cooperation, intangible cultural heritage

## Why in News?
The Ministry of Culture has released ₹5 crore under the Museum Grant Scheme for the construction of a new building for the Andhra Sahitya Parishad Government Museum and Research Institute in Kakinada . The museum houses nearly 5,000 palm-leaf manuscripts dating back to the 17th, 18th and 19th centuries , making it an important centre for preserving Andhra Pradesh’s literary, scientific and cultural heritage.

## About the Museum
- The Andhra Sahitya Parishad Government Museum and Research Institute is located in Kakinada , Andhra Pradesh.
- It is an important heritage institution preserving rare manuscripts and historical objects. In the 1970s , the Andhra Sahitya Parishad , a literary organisation, handed over its manuscript collection to the museum for preservation.
The museum is not limited to manuscripts. It also preserves:
- Buddhist pillars
- Sculptures
- Megalithic tools
- Copper plates
- Bronze plates
- Coins
This makes the museum significant for the study of literature, religion, archaeology, history and regional culture .

## What are Palm-Leaf Manuscripts?
- Palm-leaf manuscripts are traditional written records prepared on dried and treated palm leaves. Before the widespread use of paper, palm leaves were widely used in India for recording religious, literary, scientific and administrative knowledge.
They are important because they preserve India’s older knowledge systems in fields such as:
- Medicine
- Mathematics
- Astronomy and astrology
- Literature
- Music
- Philosophy
- Ritual traditions
Since palm leaves are organic materials, they are highly vulnerable to decay, insects, humidity and improper handling.

## Importance of the Collection
- The Kakinada Museum houses nearly 5,000 palm-leaf manuscripts from the 17th, 18th and 19th centuries .
These manuscripts belong to several fields:
| Field | Importance |
| Ayurveda | Preserves traditional medical knowledge |
| Mathematics | Reflects scientific and intellectual traditions |
| Astrology | Shows traditional astronomical and predictive systems |
| Telugu Literature | Helps protect regional literary heritage |
| Sanskrit Literature | Connects regional scholarship with classical Indian knowledge |
| Classical Music | Supports the study of traditional music systems |
Of the total collection, 500 manuscripts were chemically treated for preservation in 2021 .

## Funding and New Building Plan
The new museum building is expected to improve preservation, display and research facilities.
Key Details
| Component | Details |
| Project | New building for Andhra Sahitya Parishad Government Museum and Research Institute |
| Location | Kakinada, Andhra Pradesh |
| Central funding released | ₹5 crore |

| Funding pattern | 80:20 between Ministry of Culture and State Government |
| Building plan | Ground-plus-four-floor structure |
| Expected start of work | Early August |
The authorities are yet to finalise a temporary building where the museum collection can be shifted during construction.

## Significance of the Project

### 1. Preservation of Knowledge Traditions
The manuscripts preserve India’s intellectual traditions in medicine, mathematics, literature, astrology and music .

### 2. Strengthening Andhra Pradesh’s Cultural Heritage
The project will help protect rare Telugu and Sanskrit manuscripts linked to the cultural history of Andhra Pradesh.

### 3. Better Museum Infrastructure
A new building can provide better storage, display, research and conservation facilities.

### 4. Support for Researchers
The museum can become a stronger resource centre for scholars working on literature, history, Ayurveda, musicology and archaeology .

### 5. Heritage Tourism Potential
A modern museum building can improve visitor experience and promote heritage tourism in Kakinada.

### 6. Centre-State Cooperation
The funding pattern reflects cooperation between the Ministry of Culture and the State government in heritage preservation.

## Conclusion
The ₹5 crore funding boost for the Andhra Sahitya Parishad Government Museum and Research Institute is an important step towards preserving Andhra Pradesh’s manuscript heritage. The museum’s collection of nearly 5,000 palm-leaf manuscripts reflects India’s rich knowledge traditions in literature, medicine, mathematics, astrology and music. A modern museum building, supported by conservation, digitisation and research facilities, can transform the institution into a major centre for cultural preservation and academic study.

India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership: Roadmap to 2030

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II: International Relations, Bilateral Relations, Indo-Pacific, Multilateral Cooperation

#### For Prelims:
- India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership, Roadmap to 2030, ANZAC, Commonwealth, Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative, UNCLOS, UNSC Reform, NSG, International Solar Alliance, Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure, Global Biofuels Alliance, AEO-MRA, IUU Fishing

#### For Mains:
- Rules-based Indo-Pacific, maritime security, strategic partnership, diaspora diplomacy, economic cooperation, UN reforms, disaster resilience, technology cooperation, compartmentalised convergence

## Why in News?
The Prime Ministers of India and New Zealand met in Auckland on 11 July 2026 and announced the creation of the India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership . They endorsed the roadmap to 2030 as a shared framework to guide cooperation over the next four years across political engagement, defence, maritime security, trade, agriculture, education, culture, science, technology, disaster management and the Indo-Pacific.

## Background of India–New Zealand Relations
- India and New Zealand share warm and friendly relations rooted in democratic values, Commonwealth membership, common law traditions and support for a rules-based global order .
- A historical connection goes back to World War I , when Indian soldiers fought alongside the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps forces in Gallipoli in 1915 .
- Formal diplomatic relations between the two countries were established in 1952 . Since then, the relationship has been strengthened through trade, education, tourism, sport and people-to-people exchanges.
- New Zealand identified India as a priority country through its “Opening Doors to India” policy in 2011 , reiterated in 2015 . It also launched the NZ Inc. India Strategy in 2011 to make India a core trade, economic and political partner. In 2020 , New Zealand released “India-NZ 2025 – Investing in the Relationship” , which envisioned a more enduring strategic partnership with India.
- The Indian diaspora, forming roughly 5% of New Zealand’s population , acts as a strong cultural and economic bridge between the two countries.

## Roadmap to 2030: Key Features
- The India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership: Roadmap to 2030 provides an implementation framework for deepening bilateral cooperation. It is a non-binding roadmap , meaning it creates no financial commitments and no legally binding rights or obligations.
The Roadmap is structured around six major pillars.

### Political and Diplomatic Engagement
- Both sides will promote regular meetings and reciprocal visits between Prime Ministers, Cabinet Ministers, Foreign Ministers, parliamentarians and senior officials . Annual Secretary-level meetings will help steer and coordinate the Strategic Partnership.

### Defence and Security Cooperation
- The Roadmap strengthens military exchanges, defence dialogues, naval activities, maritime cooperation, hydrography, logistics support, counter-terrorism cooperation, cyber dialogue and law enforcement cooperation.

### Trade and Economic Cooperation
- Both countries aim to double two-way trade in goods and services to NZ$7 billion , or about ₹35,000 crore , by 2030 . They will also work towards early implementation of the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement .

### People, Culture and Sport
- The Roadmap focuses on diaspora engagement, tourism, sports cooperation, cultural exchanges, traditional medicine dialogue, seafarer certificate recognition and maritime heritage cooperation.

### Education, Research, Science, Technology and Disaster Management
- The partnership will expand cooperation in education, agriculture, climate action, digital transformation, emerging technologies, solar energy, biofuels and disaster management.

### Regional and Multilateral Cooperation
- India and New Zealand will cooperate in ASEAN-led forums, Indo-Pacific maritime security, peaceful dispute resolution under UNCLOS , UN reform and India’s candidature for permanent membership of a reformed UN Security Council .

## Defence and Maritime Security Cooperation
- Defence and maritime cooperation is emerging as a core area of India–New Zealand relations. A 2025 India–New Zealand Memorandum of Understanding on Defence Cooperation has already enhanced structured defence engagement.
The Roadmap provides for continued military interaction through:
- Defence exercises
- Visits of maritime, air and land units
- Personnel exchanges
- Defence staff college exchanges
- High-level defence dialogues
- Naval activities and bilateral naval exercises
The two countries will implement the Maritime Cooperation Arrangement , cooperation in Hydrography and Nautical Cartography , and a Mutual Logistics Support Arrangement focused on the maritime domain.
- New Zealand has increasingly shown interest in India’s Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative , especially its Maritime Security pillar . An annual Maritime Security Dialogue will also be established for coordination and information exchange.
- The focus on IUU fishing , maritime domain awareness, hydrography and HADR can help deepen strategic cooperation without forcing complete convergence on all geopolitical issues.

## Counter-Terrorism, Cyber and Law Enforcement
- The Roadmap strengthens cooperation on internal security and emerging threats.
- Both countries will operationalise a Joint Working Group on Counter-Terrorism , led by the foreign ministries of both sides. They will also work towards formalising cooperation on narcotics trafficking, psychotropic substances, precursor chemicals and related matters.
- The India–New Zealand Cyber Dialogue will help both countries engage on shared cybersecurity priorities.
- Cooperation between India’s National Investigation Agency and the New Zealand Police is also proposed. This is important because India has raised concerns over separatist and extremist activities abroad, while cooperation must also respect New Zealand’s domestic legal framework and free-speech norms.

## Trade and Economic Cooperation
- Bilateral trade in goods and services reached around USD 2.4 billion in 2024 , growing by more than 50% in the preceding three years.
- India imports wool, iron and steel, fruits and nuts, and aluminium from New Zealand. India exports pharmaceuticals, machinery, textiles, precious stones and metals to New Zealand.
- The Roadmap sets an aspirational target of doubling bilateral trade to NZ$7 billion by 2030 .
- It also seeks to operationalise the 2025 Authorised Economic Operators Mutual Recognition Arrangement under the 2024 Customs Cooperation Arrangement . This will simplify customs processes and facilitate trusted trade.
- However, trade expansion will require careful handling of sensitive sectors, especially dairy , where India’s cooperative-based dairy system supports millions of small farmers, while New Zealand is a major export-oriented dairy producer.

## Agriculture, Primary Industries and Tourism
- The Roadmap expands cooperation in horticulture, forestry, animal husbandry and dairying .
- The 2025 Memorandum of Cooperation on Horticulture will support joint research, knowledge exchange, post-harvest innovation and market development.
- The 2025 Letter of Intent on Forestry Cooperation will promote policy dialogue, technical exchanges and best-practice sharing.
- The Memorandum of Cooperation on Animal Husbandry and Dairying will support technical and policy collaboration.
- Under the Agricultural Productivity Partnership , a Kiwifruit Action Plan and two Centres of Excellence in Nagaland and Uttarakhand will promote agricultural innovation, skill development and education.
- In tourism, the Roadmap supports two-way visitor flows and industry cooperation. It also encourages airlines to start direct non-stop flights under the updated Air Services Agreement .

## People, Culture, Education and Technology
- The Roadmap supports: People-to-people relations remain a major strength of the partnership. The Indian diaspora in New Zealand helps deepen cultural, educational and economic connections.
- Diaspora engagement
- Tourism cooperation
- Sport cooperation
- Traditional medicine exchanges
- Cultural cooperation
- Maritime heritage cooperation
- Local government-level engagement
- The India–New Zealand Joint Action Plan on Sport will expand collaboration beyond cricket, especially as India prepares to host the 2030 Commonwealth Games .
- In education, both sides will implement the 2025 Education Cooperation Arrangement to promote institutional partnerships and information exchange.
- Research and technology cooperation will focus on agriculture, climate, digital transformation and emerging technologies . Agreements involving the National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research and the University of Canterbury will strengthen Antarctic research. Cooperation between NIFTEM-Kundli and Massey University will promote food technology research, academic exchanges and student mobility.

## Clean Energy and Disaster Management
- New Zealand has joined the Global Biofuels Alliance , strengthening cooperation in sustainable energy transitions.
- Both sides will deepen climate action and low-emission transition through the International Solar Alliance , especially in solar deployment, financing and capacity building.
- In disaster management, cooperation between India’s National Disaster Management Authority and New Zealand’s National Emergency Management Agency will cover preparedness, emergency response and capacity building.
- This is significant because New Zealand has experience in earthquakes, coastal hazards and emergency response, while India can use this cooperation to strengthen disaster resilience domestically and in the wider Indo-Pacific.
- Cooperation through the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure can also support tsunami preparedness, coastal resilience and disaster-management capacity in Pacific Island Countries.

## Regional and Multilateral Cooperation
- India and New Zealand support a rules-based Indo-Pacific . The Roadmap calls for cooperation in ASEAN-led and other regional forums.
- Both countries support peaceful resolution of disputes in accordance with international law, especially UNCLOS .
- New Zealand supports India’s candidature for permanent membership of a reformed UN Security Council and India’s entry into the Nuclear Suppliers Group .
- New Zealand is also associated with India-led platforms such as the International Solar Alliance and the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure .
- This multilateral cooperation strengthens India’s diplomatic outreach and supports its broader Act East and Indo-Pacific strategy.

## Significance of the Strategic Partnership
Strategic Upgrade
- The elevation of ties to a Strategic Partnership gives structure and direction to the bilateral relationship.
Indo-Pacific Cooperation
- The Roadmap strengthens cooperation on maritime security, UNCLOS, IUU fishing, HADR and maritime domain awareness.
Trade Expansion
- The target of NZ$7 billion trade by 2030 can deepen economic engagement and diversify India’s trade partnerships.
Agricultural Modernisation
- New Zealand’s expertise in dairy, horticulture, animal husbandry, forestry and food technology can help India improve productivity and post-harvest systems.
Disaster Resilience
- Cooperation between NDMA and NEMA can strengthen India’s capacity in disaster preparedness, emergency response and coastal hazard mitigation.
Diaspora Diplomacy
- The Indian diaspora in New Zealand strengthens cultural, educational and business links.
Multilateral Support
- New Zealand’s support for India’s UNSC permanent membership and NSG entry has diplomatic value.

## Challenges
Dairy Sector Friction
- Agricultural India’s dairy sector supports millions of small farmers. New Zealand’s demand for wider dairy access may create resistance in India.
Technology Concerns
- Cooperation in kiwifruit, apples and honey can improve Indian productivity, but some New Zealand producers may fear future competition.
China Factor
- China accounts for a large share of New Zealand’s exports. This economic dependence can limit New Zealand’s alignment with India’s balancing approach in the Indo-Pacific.
Strategic Divergences
- New Zealand’s Five Eyes membership and its positions on governance and human rights may not always align with India’s sovereignty-focused foreign policy.
Transnational Extremism
- India’s concerns over separatist activities on New Zealand soil need careful handling through law enforcement cooperation.
Labour Mobility Constraints
- India seeks greater access for skilled professionals, but New Zealand’s immigration policies and domestic concerns may restrict mobility.
Connectivity Gap
- Limited direct air connectivity increases distance-related barriers to trade, tourism and education.

## Way Forward
Compartmentalised Convergence
- India and New Zealand should isolate contentious issues such as bulk dairy market access and geopolitical differences, while advancing cooperation in areas of clear convergence.
Resolve SPS and Market Access Issues
- Sectoral working groups should address phytosanitary concerns and gradually expand agricultural trade without harming India’s dairy ecosystem.
Operationalise Direct Flights
- Direct air connectivity should be prioritised to support tourism, education, trade and diaspora engagement.
Deepen Non-Traditional Maritime Cooperation
- Both sides should focus on IUU fishing, maritime domain awareness, HADR, hydrography and UNCLOS-based maritime governance.
Promote Niche Technology Partnerships
- Cooperation should focus on green hydrogen, agri-tech, precision farming, Antarctic research, food technology, digital transformation and biofuels.
Expand Pacific Outreach
- India and New Zealand can jointly support disaster resilience and coastal preparedness in Pacific Island Countries through NDMA-NEMA and CDRI frameworks.

## Conclusion
The India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership: Roadmap to 2030 marks a major step in transforming a friendly relationship into a structured and strategic partnership. Its success will depend on compartmentalised convergence — managing difficult issues such as dairy access and China-related differences while advancing cooperation in maritime security, trade, agriculture, science, technology, clean energy, disaster management and the Indo-Pacific. If implemented effectively, the Roadmap can become an important pillar of India’s Act East Policy , Indo-Pacific strategy and rules-based global engagement.

What is meant by ‘trial in absentia’?

### Relevance: UPSC GS Paper II – Polity, Governance, Criminal Justice Reforms, Internal Security

#### For Prelims:
- Trial in absentia, Section 356 BNSS, Section 84 BNSS, Proclaimed offender, Non-bailable warrant, NIA Court, BNS 2023, UAPA 1967, LeT, TRF, CrPC Section 82, CrPC Section 299, CrPC Section 317

#### For Mains:
- Right to fair trial, criminal justice reform, absconding accused, terrorism prosecution, procedural safeguards, national security, due process of law, speedy trial, victim justice, rule of law

## Why in News?
A Special NIA Court in Jammu issued a non-bailable warrant against Hafiz Saeed , Pakistan-based chief of the proscribed terror outfit Lashkar-e-Taiba , in connection with the Pahalgam terror attack investigation.
Since Hafiz Saeed is unlikely to appear before an Indian court, the National Investigation Agency may seek a trial in absentia under Section 356 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 , which replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 .

## What is Trial in Absentia?
Trial in absentia means a criminal trial conducted in the absence of the accused .
In ordinary criminal law, the presence of the accused is important because the accused has the right to:
- Know the charges
- Hear prosecution evidence
- Cross-examine witnesses
- Present defence
- Be represented by a lawyer
However, when an accused deliberately absconds and avoids trial, the justice system may be delayed for years. To address this, BNSS allows trial in absentia in limited and serious cases.

## Legal Position under BNSS

### Section 356 of BNSS
Section 356 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita provides that if a person declared as a proclaimed offender has absconded to evade trial and there is no immediate possibility of arrest, the court may proceed with the trial in the accused’s absence.
The court must:
- Record reasons in writing
- Treat the absence as a waiver of the right to be present
- Proceed with inquiry, trial and judgment as if the accused were present
This provision is important in cases where accused persons are outside India or deliberately avoiding the court process.

## Who can be Tried in Absentia?
- Trial in absentia does not apply to every accused person .
- It applies only to a proclaimed offender under Section 84 of BNSS .
Under Section 84(4) , a person may be declared a proclaimed offender if:
- A proclamation has been issued against him
- He is accused of a serious offence
- The offence is punishable with: Imprisonment of 10 years or more Life imprisonment Death penalty
- He fails to appear at the specified place and time
- The court conducts necessary inquiry before declaring him a proclaimed offender
Thus, trial in absentia is limited to serious offences , not minor criminal cases.

## How is BNSS Different from CrPC?
Under the earlier CrPC , there were limited provisions for dealing with absconding accused persons.
| Provision under CrPC | Purpose |
| Section 82 | Proclamation against absconding accused |
| Section 299 | Recording evidence in absence of absconding accused |
| Section 317 | Dispensing with personal attendance in limited cases |
- However, the CrPC did not permit a full-fledged trial in absentia .
- This meant that many serious cases remained pending for years because the accused could not be arrested or produced before the court.
- The BNSS has changed this by allowing a full trial in absentia in specific cases involving proclaimed offenders.

## Procedural Safeguards under Section 356 BNSS
The law includes several safeguards to protect the accused’s right to fair trial.
1. Two Warrants of Arrest
- Before trial in absentia can begin, the court must issue two consecutive warrants of arrest .
- There must be a gap of at least 30 days between the two warrants.
2. Public Notice
A notice must be published in a local or national newspaper , giving the accused 30 days to appear before the court.
3. Notice at Last Known Residence
The notice must also be displayed at the accused’s last known place of residence .
4. Intimation to Relative or Friend
A relative or friend of the accused must be informed about the trial.
5. Waiting Period after Framing of Charges
The trial cannot begin immediately.
It can start only after 90 days from the date of framing of charges. This gives the accused enough time to appear before the court.
6. State-appointed Defence Lawyer
If the absconding accused does not have legal representation, the court must appoint a defence lawyer at the State’s expense .
This protects the constitutional principle of fair trial .
7. Audiovisual Recording
Witness deposition and examination may be recorded through audiovisual electronic means .
These recordings must be preserved to maintain transparency and help future review.

## What Happens if the Accused Later Appears?
If the accused later appears before the court or is arrested, the court may allow him to cross-examine prosecution witnesses .
This is important because cross-examination is a core element of a fair trial.
Thus, the law tries to balance:
- Need for speedy justice
- Rights of victims
- National security concerns
- Accused’s right to fair trial

## Why was Trial in Absentia Introduced?
Trial in absentia was introduced to address delays caused by absconding accused persons.
It is especially relevant in:
- Terrorism cases
- Cross-border offences
- Organised crime
- Cases involving fugitives
- National security offences
In such cases, the accused may deliberately avoid appearing before Indian courts. Without trial in absentia, justice may remain blocked indefinitely.

## Significance of Trial in Absentia
1. Strengthens Criminal Justice System
It prevents accused persons from defeating justice by simply absconding.
2. Useful in Terror Cases
It is useful where accused persons are located outside India and cannot be easily extradited.
3. Ensures Speedy Trial
It prevents long pendency of serious criminal cases.
4. Supports Victim Justice
Victims and families need closure. Trial in absentia helps avoid indefinite delay.
5. Strengthens National Security
In terrorism and cross-border conspiracy cases, it helps India move forward with prosecution even when the accused is beyond physical reach.

## Concerns and Challenges
1. Risk to Fair Trial
Trying an accused in his absence may affect his ability to personally defend himself.
2. Quality of Defence
A State-appointed lawyer may not always have full instructions from the accused.
3. Evidence Scrutiny
The absence of the accused may weaken effective cross-examination.
4. Misuse Concerns
The provision must not be used casually. It should be limited to serious cases where the accused is deliberately avoiding trial.
5. International Enforcement
Even after conviction, enforcement may remain difficult if the accused is in another country.

## Way Forward
- Courts must strictly follow all safeguards under Section 356 BNSS .
- Trial in absentia should be used only in serious cases involving proclaimed offenders.
- State-appointed defence lawyers must be competent and independent.
- Audiovisual records must be preserved properly.
- India should strengthen extradition and mutual legal assistance mechanisms.
- The provision should be used to support justice, not to weaken due process.

## Conclusion
Trial in absentia under Section 356 of BNSS is a major reform in India’s criminal justice system. It allows courts to proceed against proclaimed offenders who deliberately evade trial. However, because it affects the accused’s right to be personally present, the provision must be applied with strict safeguards. If used carefully, it can help India deal with terrorism, fugitives and cross-border crimes while maintaining the constitutional commitment to fair trial and rule of law.
